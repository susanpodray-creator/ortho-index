Orchestrate 3D

Orthodontic CAD and clear-aligner treatment-planning software from Park Dental Research for in-office or hybrid scan-plan-print workflows, with optional lab planning, model print, and DigiLine direct-print aligners.

HQ: Ardmore, Oklahoma, USA · Founded 2012 · Researched 2026-09-21 · Official site

Overview

Orchestrate 3D (often shortened to O3D) is orthodontic treatment-planning and CAD software sold today through Park Dental Research (PDR) at shop.pdrus.com. The live product pages describe a scan → plan → print stack for clear aligners and related appliances: import STL scans, segment and move teeth, stage attachments and IPR, then export printable models — or hand steps to PDR’s lab. The published contact address on those pages is 2401 North Commerce, Suite E, Ardmore, OK 73401, so this brief uses Ardmore, Oklahoma, USA for hq.

The brand started as a California orthodontic software company founded by orthodontist Todd Ehrler, DDS, MS. Trade coverage of the September 1, 2021 deal (Orthodontic Products, Sep 10, 2021; Compendium) says PDR acquired Orchestrate 3D, which had been headquartered in Redlands, California. The FDA 510(k) for the software (K181112) lists the applicant as Orchestrate 3D at a Rialto, California address with Ehrler as applicant contact. After the acquisition, Orchestrate 3D is PDR’s digital orthodontic brand; it is not a separate storefront from shop.pdrus.com in the pages reviewed for this draft.

Founded date: PDR’s software page says the product has been “trusted by Orthodontist globally since 2012.” A 2019 industry article on the FDA clearance said Orchestrate Orthodontic Technologies was founded in 2007. This page sets founded to 2012 to match PDR’s public “since 2012” marketing for the shipped product, and treats 2007 as an older company-formation claim, not the live storefront’s primary date.

What it is in plain terms: O3D is the planning/CAD application. PDR also sells lab services around it (scan conversion, technician planning via a web viewer, model printing on JUELL™ printers, aligner fabrication and private-label packaging) and markets DigiLine™, a direct-print aligner path that PDR presents as working with O3D or other planning software. EasyRX is named as the third-party portal for lab case submissions. PDR also lists itself as a distributor of the Shining 3D AoralScan.

This page is a software-category brief for offices comparing in-house or hybrid aligner platforms. It is not a protocol, a quote, or a clearance summary for every current module.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists who want doctor-controlled staging on open STL files instead of a single closed aligner portal for every mild, finishing, or hybrid case
  • Offices building an in-house or hybrid lab (scan in office or mail stone/PVS; plan in O3D or via PDR techs; print models or DigiLine trays in office or at PDR)
  • Practices that already own open-architecture scanners and printers and need fast multi-arch STL export, auto labeling, and IPR/Bolton tools rather than a turnkey appliance-only subscription
  • Groups that use a large national aligner brand for complex cases but still want a second path for relapse, limited treatment, early deband finishing, or retainers — which is how PDR’s FAQ describes many of its heavier users
  • Labs and OEM-style shops evaluating PDR’s manufacturing/software story (trade coverage of the 2021 deal emphasized PDR/Orchestrate OEM aligner manufacturing; confirm current OEM terms with sales, not from this page)

It is a weaker fit if you need macOS-native chairside software: published system requirements are Windows 10 64-bit with a recent NVIDIA CUDA GPU. It is also the wrong shortlist if you only want a fully managed aligner brand with no interest in staging files or printers.

Confirm shipping territory before you buy hardware or licenses. The FAQ states PDR merchandise ships to the United States and U.S. territories (including Puerto Rico, Guam, and the U.S. Virgin Islands). Older Orchestrate marketing claimed worldwide doctor use; treat current eligibility as a sales confirmation.

Notable products

Names below match PDR storefront pages and FDA records reviewed September 21, 2026. License tiers, turnaround, DigiLine hardware availability, and feature lists move. Street prices are not pasted here from marketing tables. Speed, comfort, and “better outcomes” language on vendor pages are manufacturer claims.

Treatment-planning software

  • Orchestrate 3D Treatment Planning Software (O3D) — desktop CAD for scan-based tooth movement, staging, attachments, IPR, Bolton analysis, distance measurement, auto labeling, and batch STL export of upper/lower printable models. PDR advertises AI-assisted segmentation, tooth movement, and attachment placement, plus “scanned STL … ready for tooth movement in 5 minutes or less.” Treat that timing as a marketing figure, not a service-level agreement. Published computer specs call for Windows 10 64-bit, .NET 3.5 SP1 / 4.0, 16 GB RAM recommended, and an NVIDIA GPU less than ~2 years old with driver > 418.96.
  • Web Viewer — high-resolution viewer used when PDR’s planning team sends setups for doctor review and revision requests.
  • FDA 510(k) K181112 — device name Orchestrate 3D, classified as orthodontic software (21 CFR 872.5470, product code PNN), decision substantially equivalent on November 26, 2018. The 2018 applicant address is historical (Rialto). A 2018 clearance is not a certificate that every 2026 AI control or DigiLine module sits inside that submission.

License and support pages are linked from the software storefront (“LICENSE & FEES,” “SOFTWARE SUPPORT & WARRANTY”). Public research for this draft did not lock current monthly/annual/perpetual seat prices into the body — get the live SKU list from PDR before budgeting.

Hybrid / lab services around the software

  • Scanning services — mail stone models or PVS impressions; PDR scans to STL and either returns files for office planning or forwards them to the Orchestrate planning team.
  • Treatment planning services — technicians build a setup from your STLs; you review in the web viewer and request changes.
  • 3D printing services — PDR prints aligner-ready models with JUELL™ 3D Flash OC software; the overview page cites 3–5 business days for printed models so staff can thermoform in office.
  • Aligner fabrication and packaging — full cases in customizable white packaging for practice-branded private label; optional fabricate-only paths appear in EasyRX tutorial titles on the FAQ page.
  • EasyRX — third-party case portal PDR documents with step videos for mailed-in and digital submissions, plan approval, model print orders, DigiLine submissions, and thermoform retainers.

DigiLine and related hardware

  • DigiLine™ Direct Print Aligner System — marketed as a direct-to-aligner print path (scan → O3D plan → DigiLine module → JUELL DigiLine printer/resin → deliver). PDR’s DigiLine page still carried “anticipated release 2023” printer language in the copy reviewed for this draft; confirm what is shipping today versus roadmap. FDA lists DigiLine Direct Print Aligner System under 510(k) K233935, applicant Park Dental Research Corporation (Ardmore address), decision substantially equivalent on April 5, 2024, product code NXC (sequential aligner).
  • JUELL™ 3D printers and UV resins — production printers and materials named across support and DigiLine pages for model and direct-print workflows.
  • Shining 3D AoralScan — intraoral scanner PDR distributes; O3D marketing also claims compatibility with open-source / open STL scanners and printers generally.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Real in-house control story: open STL in, editable staging, printable STLs out — without forcing every case through one national aligner brand.
  • Hybrid ladder is explicit (mail impressions → tech plan → print models → fabricate in office or at PDR → optional DigiLine). Offices can grow into scanners and printers instead of buying the whole stack on day one.
  • K181112 and K233935 are real FDA records under the software and DigiLine device names. That is more than a marketing adjective, and less than a blanket blessing of every UI feature on the 2026 storefront.
  • Useful as a second platform beside a full-treatment aligner vendor for finishing, limited, and retainer work — which matches how PDR describes many customers in its FAQ.
  • Private-label packaging and OEM-oriented trade history matter if the office brand (not a third-party logo) should be on the box.

Cons / watch-outs

  • Windows + NVIDIA CUDA requirements narrow chairside hardware. Mac-first offices will need a Windows workstation plan.
  • “Since 2012” vs “founded 2007” and the move from Redlands/Rialto to Ardmore under PDR mean corporate history is easy to mis-cite. Use the live PDR address for purchasing; use K181112 for the software clearance vintage.
  • AI and “5 minutes or less” / “30 minutes” DigiLine claims are vendor speed marketing. Segmentation quality, revision cycles, and print/post-process time still depend on scan quality, case difficulty, and lab staffing.
  • DigiLine storefront copy looked partly dated (“anticipated release 2023”) while a 2024 510(k) exists. Ask which printer, resin, IFU, and software build are actually orderable before you design a same-day workflow around the brochure.
  • Pricing is not a public catalog fact in the pages used here. License seats, per-model lab fees, and DigiLine hardware bundles need a current quote.
  • U.S.-centric shipping on the FAQ may block international buyers even if older Orchestrate marketing said “worldwide.”
  • In-office production is still your lab: printer, resin or sheet, trimmer, QC, labeling, and staff time. Software export buttons do not replace that bench.
  • Marketing comparison tables on PDR pages (comfort, stain resistance, “faster results”) are competitive claims. Do not brief patients with them.

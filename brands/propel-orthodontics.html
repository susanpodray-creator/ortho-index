Propel Orthodontics

Orthodontic accessory device brand known for the Propel in-office device and the VPro high-frequency vibration line. Its assets were acquired by Dentsply Sirona in 2021, and VPro is now sold as SureSmile VPro.

HQ: Historically Briarcliff Manor, New York and San Jose, California, USA (assets now owned by Dentsply Sirona) · Researched 2026-11-02 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

Propel Orthodontics developed orthodontic accessory devices, including the in-office Propel device and the at-home VPro high-frequency vibration (HFV) line. Dentsply Sirona’s 2021 press release describes Propel Orthodontics as having offices in Briarcliff Manor, New York, and San Jose, California. openFDA lists Propel Orthodontics, LLC of Milpitas, California, as the 510(k) applicant.

Ownership and acquisition: In June 2021 Dentsply Sirona (Nasdaq: XRAY) announced that it had acquired substantially all of Propel Orthodontics’ assets, including the VPro device and the VPro Fastrack mobile app. Propel’s old site now says the SureSmile brand “welcomes SureSmile VPro” and that its contents will be moved. The former propelorthodontics.com domain now redirects to SureSmile. Dentsply Sirona also said VPro was already included in Byte treatments, branded as HyperByte. We did not verify whether Propel Orthodontics still exists as a separate operating company.

FDA status (openFDA): Propel Orthodontics, LLC holds three 510(k) clearances:

  • K150392 (2015) and K172164 (2018), both for the PROPEL device under product code DZJ (manual driver, wire and bone drill; Class II)
  • K183018 (2019), for VPro5 under product code OYH (orthodontic vibratory accessory; Class II, 21 CFR 872.5470)

The current SureSmile page describes VPro as “designed to facilitate minor anterior tooth movement when used 5 minutes per day.” Check the cleared indications in the 510(k) summaries and the IFU.

Who it’s for

Orthodontic and dental offices that offer clear aligners or fixed appliances and want a patient-use vibration accessory. In the US it is now sold through Dentsply Sirona’s SureSmile portfolio.

Notable products

  • SureSmile VPro (formerly VPro5, VPro+ and VPro): an at-home vibration device worn with aligners in place for one 5-minute session a day, according to the company FAQ. It has a gradual ramp-up and isn’t waterproof; you detach the mouthpiece to rinse it.
  • VPro Fastrack app: an optional app that tracks device and aligner use, takes progress photos, and shares usage with the provider.
  • Propel device: an in-office device cleared by FDA under product code DZJ (see openFDA K150392 and K172164). We couldn’t confirm that it’s still sold.
  • Excellerator: this older Propel product name appears in some references, but we couldn’t confirm its current status from an official source.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • FDA 510(k) clearances for VPro5 and the Propel device are on public record
  • Backed by a large public company (Dentsply Sirona) and part of the SureSmile aligner ecosystem
  • The daily routine is simple and short, and the app lets providers see usage

Cons

  • The company’s marketing says HFV can improve comfort and “may reduce overall treatment time.” A 2015 Cochrane review of non-surgical ways to speed up tooth movement, which covered vibration, rated the available evidence very low quality and said it was not possible to determine whether these interventions have a positive effect. Treat speed claims as unproven.
  • The brand is in transition: the legacy Propel site is being moved into SureSmile, and product names have changed
  • Pricing isn’t published
  • Some legacy products (the in-office Propel device and Excellerator) have unclear current availability
  • Patients have to stick with it daily, and it adds a device and an app to manage

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

VPro is an add-on to aligner or retention protocols, not a treatment system on its own. Offices dispense it to patients and can track compliance through the Fastrack app. When you evaluate it, ask Dentsply Sirona or SureSmile for the cleared indications and IFU, which regions it’s available in, pricing for each kit, how it works with non-SureSmile aligners, and the app’s data and privacy terms. Weigh the company’s claims against independent evidence.

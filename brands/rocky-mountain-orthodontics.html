Rocky Mountain Orthodontics

Long-standing U.S. orthodontic manufacturer of brackets, wires, and appliances—including Synergy, Alpine SL, FLI, Elgiloy, and TRU-CHROME—serving specialty practices worldwide.

HQ: Franklin, Indiana, USA · Founded 1933 · Researched 2026-08-24 · Official site

Overview

Rocky Mountain Orthodontics (RMO) is a specialty manufacturer and supplier of orthodontic brackets, archwires, appliances, and related chairside products. The company was founded in 1933 by Denver orthodontist Dr. Archie Brusse and positions itself among the earliest makers of prefabricated orthodontic appliances. Public history materials also credit RMO with early stainless-steel adoption in orthodontics and later use of CAD and metal injection molding (MIM).

Corporate headquarters is listed at Franklin, Indiana (2165 Earlywood Drive). RMO also notes RMO Europe in Strasbourg, France, and a global dealer network. Ownership materials name Michael Jahns and Brandon Bernacchi as current owners investing in manufacturing and service. The brand is fixed-appliance oriented—brackets, wires, buccal tubes, early-treatment appliances, elastomerics, TADs, and instruments—rather than a clear-aligner platform company.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontic specialists stocking metal twin, self-ligating, and aesthetic brackets from one specialty vendor
  • Offices that want a deep wire catalog (NiTi, stainless, cobalt-chromium Elgiloy, beta titanium) alongside brackets
  • Practices using Bioprogressive, Ricketts, Roth, MBT, SWLF, or related prescriptions that appear in RMO’s kit listings
  • Teams comparing early-treatment and fixed-appliance options (e.g., Wilson System accessories, multi-action appliances) with standard bracket inventory
  • Multi-country offices that source through RMO’s dealer network rather than a single mega-vendor aligner stack

Notable products

Official shop categories and SKU names change; names below match current rmortho.com listings as researched:

  • Synergy® — patented tri-wing metal bracket line marketed for Friction Selection Control (multiple ligation modes); prescriptions include Roth, SWLF, Standard Edgewise, Bench/Classic BioPro, Ricketts BioPro, and Trimorphic facial types; related SKUs include Synergy FX and Synergy R
  • Alpine SL — two-piece stainless steel self-ligating brackets listed in Bioprogressive (including Trimorphic facial variants), MBT, and Roth prescriptions (.018 / .022 kits and packs)
  • FLI® Twin — twin metal brackets with flared/chamfered lead-in design; MBT, Roth, and Ricketts BioProgressive kits on the metal brackets shop
  • Aesthetic brackets — FLI Signature Clear, Signature III Ceramic, and FLI Clear M lines under Brackets Aesthetic
  • Other metal brackets — Mini Twin, Mini-Taurus, Integra, MIM, and related twin/one-piece options in the metal brackets category
  • Archwires — broad wire shop including nickel-titanium (superelastic and heat-activated), stainless steel, beta titanium, Elgiloy® cobalt-chromium, and aesthetic-coated options; catalog materials also reference TRU-CHROME® stainless, Bendaloy® TMA, Orthonol®, Thermaloy®, and FLI® CuNiTi families
  • Appliances and auxiliaries — early-treatment items (Wilson System accessories/tubes, Bimetric Distalizing Arch, multi-action palatal/mandibular appliances, lingual holding arches), buccal tubes, bands, fixed appliances, headgear, elastomerics (including Energy Chain™ called out in company history), TADs, and instruments (APEX Series, Schweickhardt)

Confirm slot sizes, prescriptions, and kit contents with the current product page or your RMO rep before ordering.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Deep specialty catalog spanning brackets, wires, tubes, early treatment, and auxiliaries under one brand
  • Distinctive Synergy tri-wing platform plus Alpine SL self-ligating and FLI twin options for mixed inventory strategies
  • Long company history (founded 1933 per official About Us) and prescription breadth that includes Bioprogressive/Ricketts-oriented kits
  • Wire metallurgy variety (Elgiloy, TRU-CHROME, NiTi families, beta titanium) useful for doctors who bend and stage actively
  • Direct e-commerce storefront plus international dealers; Made in the USA called out on Synergy and FLI product pages

Cons

  • Not a clear-aligner ecosystem play—practices needing a turnkey aligner brand will look elsewhere or pair RMO fixed appliances with another partner
  • Large SKU surface area (many prescriptions, pack sizes, and wire forms) requires disciplined formulary choices for startups
  • HQ moved to Franklin, Indiana; older materials still emphasize Denver roots—use the Contact page for current corporate address
  • Marketing claims on friction, treatment time, and “best in class” value are vendor positioning; verify against your own chairside criteria and IFUs

uLab Systems

uLab Systems makes uDesign aligner-planning software, the uAssist concierge planning service, and Memphis-made uSmile aligners. It is not SmileStream, which is a separate diagnostic platform from Progressive Orthodontics.

HQ: Memphis, Tennessee, USA · Founded 2015 · Researched 2026-09-18 · Official site

Overview

uLab Systems is a U.S. orthodontic company whose public stack is uDesign treatment-planning software, the uAssist concierge planning service, and uSmile clear aligners and retainers. The live site is ulabsystems.com. The legal name on the careers page is uLab Systems, Inc. The careers page states that headquarters and manufacturing are in Memphis, Tennessee; the published contact address is 7005 Appling Farms Parkway, Suite 103, Memphis, TN 38133. This page uses Memphis, Tennessee, USA for hq.

The company says it was founded in 2015 (its August 18, 2022 Inc. 5000 release) and that it was started in 2015 (its February 29, 2024 milestone release). Those are the company’s own dates, so founded is 2015. The same 2024 release lays out the product sequence the company still tells: uDesign first, built so orthodontists could plan and fabricate aligners in an office lab; uSmile aligners and retainers from the Memphis factory beginning in 2021; uAssist added in 2022.

This is not SmileStream, and it is not a SmileStream rebrand. Public sites describe different companies and different jobs:

  • uLab Systems (ulabsystems.com) plans and supplies clear aligners: browser and desktop staging, optional concierge planning, in-office STL export, and factory aligners. No uLab page reviewed for this draft names SmileStream, Progressive Orthodontics, or an acquisition either way.
  • SmileStream (smilestream.com) is a cloud diagnostic, education, consulting, and community platform. Progressive Orthodontic Seminars describes it as “cloud based, orthodontic software developed by Progressive Orthodontic Seminars,” with charting, cephalometric analysis, model measuring, visual treatment objectives, and treatment-plan templates. That is diagnosis and office workflow software, not uLab’s aligner manufacturer portal. On May 7, 2026, Medit announced that Medit USA acquired Progressive Orthodontics (Aliso Viejo, California) and that Progressive would keep teaching while expanding its SmileStream planning and hardware offerings. That deal is Medit and Progressive Orthodontics. It does not rename uLab, and uLab is not a party in that release.
  • Smile Stream Solutions (smilestreamsolutions.com) is a third, separate orthodontic supplies storefront (brackets, wires, prep products). It is not uLab and not the SmileStream diagnostic app. The similar spelling is a search trap, not a corporate history.

Nothing in the uLab, SmileStream, or Medit pages reviewed for this draft presents one company as a former name, product, or acquirer of the other.

What the 2026 product is, in plain terms: uDesign is the planning software. uDesign Cloud is the browser app (Mac or PC, no separate Cloud installer). uDesign Cloud 2.0, announced January 20, 2026, is the release that moved segmentation, self-planning, model printing, and STL export into that browser so a case can be planned and either sent to Memphis or printed in the office without opening desktop. uDesign Desktop is still offered for specialized tools, including indirect bonding. uAssist is paid planning help from the company’s licensed clinicians, with a VIP uAssist tier reintroduced in a March 21, 2026 release. uSmile is the aligner/retainer you order; Reva is uLab’s thermoforming material, used in those factory appliances and also sold as sheets for in-office forming.

This page is a software-category brief for practices comparing planning platforms. uLab also sells appliances. It is not a protocol, a clearance summary for every 2026 feature, or a quote.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists who want one planning file that can end as a uSmile order, an in-office print, or a mix, instead of a lab that only accepts its own aligners
  • Offices that will self-plan some cases and send others to uAssist, including mild “Social Six” setups, finishing, and hybrid or combo treatments the company markets as in-scope
  • Practices that already scan with 3Shape TRIOS, Alliedstar, or Medit and want the direct Cloud import those brands were named for in the January 2026 Cloud 2.0 notes
  • Retainer-heavy offices looking at Retainer Fast Track and multi-shipment retainer bundles, knowing a “no approval step” path still needs the doctor’s own prescription review
  • Groups that print models or aligners in house and need STL export, not only a finished appliance in the mail
  • Offices adding indirect bonding tray design inside uDesign Desktop, and willing to confirm printer qualification and current bracket libraries before they count on it

It is a poor fit if you wanted SmileStream: cephalometrics, VTO, charting, CE, and case consulting from Progressive Orthodontics / Medit. Those products are not on ulabsystems.com. It is also the wrong vendor if you are shopping Smile Stream Solutions supply catalogs.

Confirm country eligibility before you demo. The March 21, 2026 company blurb says uSmile, uAssist, and uDesign are available to practices in the USA. Older company releases (2022 and 2024) also listed Canada, Australia, and New Zealand. Treat current territory as something sales must confirm, not as a four-country fact.

Notable products

Names below match the live site and company releases reviewed September 18, 2026. Bundles, turnaround, scanner coverage, and Cloud-versus-Desktop feature parity move. Street prices are not on the public pages. Speed, thinness, stain, force, satisfaction, and “on-time” figures below are manufacturer claims.

Planning software

  • uDesign — the aligner treatment-planning platform. FDA lists ULab Systems uDesign Software under 510(k) K200772, decision substantially equivalent on June 23, 2020, device class orthodontic software (21 CFR 872.5470, product code PNN). The 2020 applicant address on that record is San Mateo, California, which is a historical filing address, not the Memphis headquarters used on the current site. A 2020 clearance is not a certificate that every 2026 Cloud control is in that submission.
  • uDesign Cloud / Cloud 2.0 — browser workspace announced as the primary web experience. Cloud 2.0 (company release January 20, 2026; feature article January 13, 2026) adds, in the company’s words, cloud segmentation and self-planning, direct model print, STL export for in-office aligners and retainers, uSmile ordering without Desktop, case starts for uAssist / Self-Plan / Retainers, Retainer Fast Track, direct import from 3Shape TRIOS, Alliedstar, and Medit, attachment and staging tools, and a Social Six SmartRx for mild cosmetic or relapse cases. The live uDesign page says Desktop remains available for specialized workflows. The company FAQ says Cloud itself needs a current browser, a supported OS, and broadband, not a Cloud installer.
  • uDesign Desktop — still the chairside app. Cloud 2.0 shipped alongside Desktop 10.4, which the company describes as adding bracket-flip and placement tweaks for indirect bonding (IDB). The home page also advertises IDB trays exported from uDesign. The standalone indirect bonding page still showed placeholder “lorem ipsum” quotes during this research, so do not spec IDB libraries, fees, or printer lists from that URL. Use the release notes plus a current demo.
  • SmartRx — saved prescription templates for sending cases to uAssist, including the Social Six form called out for Cloud 2.0. A July 10, 2025 release credited SmartRx forms with fewer modification cycles. That is an operational claim, not a clinical outcome.

Earlier marketing still describes guided setup, auto staging, key frames, virtual bracket removal, scalloped or straight trimlines, and sprint ordering. Those phrases are not all repeated on the current Cloud feature list. Ask which of them are in Cloud versus Desktop before you train a team on an old screenshot.

Planning service

  • uAssist — concierge setups from what the company calls licensed orthodontists and dentists. The live service page says a 3-day turnaround and that you can use it whether you order uSmile or print in the office. The January 2026 Cloud article says less than three business days. The February 2024 milestone release claimed an average of twenty-one hours and said the doctor can edit in uDesign without sending the case back. The 2022 Inc. release said less than 72 hours. Those numbers do not match. Treat them as marketing vintages, not a service-level agreement.
  • VIP uAssist — reintroduced in the March 21, 2026 release as a premium tier with priority access and more personalized support. Public pages do not spell out the price gap versus standard uAssist.

The March 2026 note also lists EasyRx integration, more one-click scanner connections, and extra self-planning tools (global movement, pontics and domes, space management) as recent and upcoming. Do not assume every item on that list was live on the day of this draft.

Appliances and material (sold with the software)

  • uSmile clear aligners and retainers — manufactured in Memphis with Reva, uLab’s proprietary thermoforming material. The uSmile page’s comfort and thinness copy includes 17% thinner per aligner than leading brands, plus stain and force-retention language marked in places as data on file. The about page says more than 80% of molds are recycled and cites 3–10 business day delivery from being near a national carrier hub. A July 10, 2025 release claimed over 98% on-time delivery since early that year. Custom logo packaging is a stated option.
  • Reva thermoforming sheets — launched for in-office use on the company shop (shopulab.com, named in the July 10, 2025 release). The March 21, 2026 release also announced a 1-year warranty on Reva retainers. Confirm that warranty still matches the current terms.
  • Published aligner bundles on the live uDesign page, prices not listed: Mild up to 24 aligners, Moderate up to 48, Comprehensive unlimited aligners, each with two retainers, templates, uAssist, and a 3-year case window; Finishing up to 6 aligners, two retainers, two templates, and a 6-month window. Retainer ordering is called out separately (premium shipping, Fast Track, uAssist bracket removal, custom packaging). The home page still mentions à la carte ordering for limited treatment. The March 2026 AAO booth promotions (including a published per-retainer price) were tied to a meeting that ended in May 2026. Do not treat those discounts as current.

uLab’s July 10, 2025 release said uDesign Cloud itself had launched the previous April, with desktop tools promised to follow. Cloud 2.0 in January 2026 is the release that claims those tools actually landed. If a rep is still selling the July 2025 “coming soon” list, ask for the Cloud 2.0 checklist instead.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • The commercial model matches a real clinical split: self-plan, hand the setup to uAssist, print, or order. You are not forced into one of those four to use the software, at least on the published workflow.
  • Cloud 2.0 is a genuine platform change, not a renamed login. Segmentation and self-planning in the browser matter if the team was tired of a desktop install for routine cases.
  • In-office STL export is still first-class. Practices that already own printers are not asked to throw that lab away to “join” uLab.
  • Bundle windows (finishing through comprehensive) and sprint-style ordering, as the company describes them, are easier to explain to a treatment coordinator than a single unlimited black box. You still need the contract, not the webpage, for what “unlimited” and a 3-year window actually include.
  • Reva is a named material with both a factory aligner and an in-office sheet, so the in-office and outsourced paths can use the same plastic family. Confirm thickness, heating instructions, and biocompatibility documents for the sheet you buy.
  • uDesign has a real 2020 510(k) record under the company name. That is more than a marketing adjective, and less than a blanket clearance of the 2026 cloud UI.

Cons / watch-outs

  • Two apps remain. Cloud is “primary,” Desktop is “specialized.” IDB, and any tool not listed in the Cloud 2.0 notes, can still strand a case on the desktop. Feature parity has been a rolling promise since at least July 2025.
  • Turnaround copy is inconsistent. Three days, less than three business days, 72 hours, and a 21-hour average cannot all be the promise. Get the current uAssist and VIP clocks in writing.
  • Material and survey math is the company’s. “17% thinner,” stain tests, force retention, 94% would recommend, 98% product satisfaction, and 90% saying hybrid cases became possible are vendor figures. The satisfaction footnote on the home page points at a 2022 customer survey. Do not brief patients with those numbers.
  • Prices are not public. Mild / Moderate / Comprehensive / Finishing are package shapes. AAO 2026 discount figures are stale for a September 2026 decision.
  • Territory drifted in the company’s own about blurbs from USA plus Canada, Australia, and New Zealand (2022–2024) to USA in the March 2026 release. International buyers should not assume the older list.
  • The IDB marketing page is unfinished. Placeholder Latin text was still on it during this check. That is a documentation miss, and it means you cannot trust that URL for bracket coverage or export fees.
  • K200772 is not a 2026 feature inventory. It cleared uDesign Software as submitted in 2020. Ask which indications and which software version your IFU actually covers before you cite “FDA cleared” for Cloud 2.0, Reva sheets, or IDB trays.
  • Retainer Fast Track skips an approval step on purpose. That is speed, not a reason to stop looking at the setup.
  • Scanner integrations publicly named in January 2026 were three brands, “with more coming soon,” and March 2026 still called further one-click links upcoming. If your scanner is not TRIOS, Alliedstar, or Medit, plan on an STL hop until support confirms otherwise.
  • In-office production is still your lab: printer, resin or sheet, trimmer, labeling, and staff time. Cloud print buttons do not staff that bench.

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

Planmeca

Finnish, privately owned dental technology maker (Planmeca Oy, Helsinki) covering CBCT, panoramic and cephalometric imaging, intraoral scanners, Romexis software, CAD/CAM, and dental units and patient chairs—one connected equipment ecosystem.

HQ: Helsinki, Finland · Founded 1971 · Researched 2026-10-01 · Official site

Overview

Planmeca is a Finnish dental technology company. The legal entity is Planmeca Oy, the parent of Planmeca Group, and the live brand site is planmeca.com. Planmeca says it is headquartered in Helsinki, Finland (its Helsinki address is Asentajankatu 6), that it was founded there in 1971 by Heikki Kyöstilä, and that it is the largest privately owned company in the dental equipment field. It reports more than 4,300 employees and a Group turnover of EUR 1.24 billion for 2025. Planmeca lists U.S. contact details through Planmeca USA Inc. in Charlotte, North Carolina.

Naming, checked against the live site: the brand is still called Planmeca. We found no rename, and this page does not invent one. Product names carry the Planmeca prefix, for example Planmeca Viso, Planmeca ProMax, Planmeca Romexis, and Planmeca Emerald S.

How the story goes, in Planmeca’s own words:

  • It started with dental stools and instrument cabinets built in a Helsinki garage, then grew into patient chairs (first developed in 1977) and dental units (1979).
  • A microprocessor-controlled panoramic X-ray unit followed in 1986.
  • Its first CBCT system arrived in 2005, and Romexis grew from there into a single platform for 2D, 3D, and CAD/CAM.

The company says its dental units, imaging devices, and software are designed and manufactured in Helsinki.

What this page covers: the parts of Planmeca an orthodontic office is most likely to meet: CBCT, panoramic and cephalometric imaging, intraoral scanning, Romexis software, in-house printing, and the chair and unit line. It is not a purchasing recommendation. Availability, configurations, and regulatory labeling differ by country. Planmeca’s own releases say “regional restrictions apply” and send buyers to a local distributor.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontic and multi-specialty offices that want imaging, scanning, software, and chairs from one vendor, with one software platform (Romexis) tying them together.
  • Practices that want a cephalometric option on a pan or CBCT platform. Planmeca offers a one-shot cephalostat (ProCeph) and a scanning cephalostat (ProMax), and its CBCT comparison table lists a cephalometric option across the Viso and ProMax 3D lines.
  • Offices that care about CBCT dose-reduction options (Ultra Low Dose imaging) and motion-artifact correction (CALM, sold as an option), and that want to review how those fit their own protocols.
  • Offices that want an open-file intraoral scanner (STL and PLY import and export) and a stated lifetime user license on at least one scanner (Emerald S).
  • Groups or schools that need fleet visibility. Planmeca Insights reports device status, usage, and maintenance data for one device or many sites.
  • Teams that want to add in-house printing for models, surgical guides, and appliances alongside their scanning.

Notable products

Names below come from planmeca.com pages and newsroom releases checked on October 1, 2026. Manufacturer feature and performance language (speed, accuracy, dose, “AI-powered”) is marketing copy, not independent testing. Treat product pages as current catalog signals and confirm exact models with your Planmeca distributor.

CBCT / 3D imaging

  • Planmeca Viso family: the current comparison page lists Viso G1 (volumes up to 11 × 11 cm), G3, G5 (3 × 3 to 20 × 17 cm), and G7 (3 × 3 to 30 × 30 cm). Planmeca’s March 2025 IDS release announced Viso G1 as new.
  • Planmeca ProMax 3D family: 3D Classic (volumes up to 11 × 8 cm), 3D Plus (4 × 5 to 20 × 10 cm), and 3D Mid (3.4 × 4.2 to 20 × 17 cm). The 3D Mid page highlights ENT presets and a “Braces” imaging protocol, and says the unit has been certified for use with the SureSmile treatment system. The CBCT index page also lists a ProMax 3D LEC as a gateway model.
  • Common to the lineup per Planmeca: CALM movement-artifact correction (option), Ultra Low Dose imaging, 3D face photo on most models, and 3D model scanning of impressions or plaster casts on ProMax 3D units.

Panoramic and cephalometric imaging

  • Planmeca ProOne: compact panoramic unit with Autofocus and child mode.
  • Planmeca ProMax 2D (S2 and S3): panoramic, extraoral bitewing, TMJ, and sinus programs. The modular design can be upgraded with a cephalostat or CBCT.
  • Planmeca Viso 2D Pro and 2D Classic: announced at IDS 2025. Check current availability for your market.
  • Planmeca ProCeph (one-shot) and Planmeca ProMax cephalostat (scanning): Planmeca lists image sizes up to 30 × 25 cm and 30 × 27 cm respectively, including magnification.

Intraoral imaging

  • Planmeca ProX intraoral X-ray unit and Planmeca ProSensor HD intraoral sensor.
  • Planmeca ProX GO: handheld X-ray device, announced for the U.S. market first. Check local rules for handheld use before buying.

Intraoral scanners and CAD/CAM

  • Planmeca Onyx: new wireless-or-wired intraoral scanner introduced in 2025.
  • Planmeca Emerald S: compact scanner with autoclavable tips, open STL and PLY, and a stated lifetime user license with no click fees. Planmeca says it is accepted by a range of orthodontic solution providers. Confirm your specific aligner or bracket provider directly.
  • Planmeca Imprex: mobile scanning station.
  • Planmeca Creo X: dental 3D printer launched November 2025 as the successor to Creo C5. The Creo C5 web address now redirects to the Creo X page. Planmeca’s page describes an orthodontic workflow of CBCT plus scan, virtual setup in Romexis, and printed dental and setup models.
  • Planmeca PlanMill milling units and the CAD module in Romexis for chairside restorations. These matter mainly for restorative or multi-specialty groups.

Software

  • Planmeca Romexis: all-in-one software for 2D and 3D imaging, 2D and 3D cephalometry, Smile Design, intraoral scanning, restoration design, and more. Romexis 7 launched in June 2025 with built-in AI tools, including automatic anatomy segmentation, CBCT-to-scan fitting, automated implant-plan proposals, and AI landmark detection for 3D cephalometric analysis. Planmeca says AI processing of patient data runs locally on the workstation, except the smart-chat helper, which uses cloud services. A 2026 update added AI tools for 2D intraoral images.

Dental units and chairs

  • Current comparison page: Planmeca Pro50, Pro50 S, Pro40, Compact i5, Compact i Classic, Compact i3, and the standalone Pro50 Chair. Older names such as Compact i Touch and Sovereign Classic appear in archived newsroom copy but not in the current comparison table, so do not treat them as current catalog items without confirming.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Deep, connected portfolio. Few vendors cover CBCT, pan/ceph, intraoral X-ray, scanners, printing, software, and chairs under one name. That can simplify support conversations and data handoffs.
  • Orthodontics is visible in the product pages. The cephalometric options, a certified 3D Mid “Braces” protocol (per Planmeca), 2D and 3D ceph modules in Romexis, scanner compatibility language, and Creo X orthodontic workflow notes show ortho is part of the plan, not an afterthought.
  • Open scan formats and licensing language. Emerald S advertises STL and PLY import and export with no click fees and a lifetime user license. Verify the exact terms for the model you are quoted.
  • Clear company footprint. The company is privately owned, with a long history and a stated Helsinki headquarters and manufacturing base. Planmeca publishes its history and annual figures on its own site.
  • Active development. The 2025–2026 releases (Viso G1, Onyx, Creo X, Romexis 7 and its updates) show continuing investment, not a legacy-only line.

Cons / watch-outs

  • A big catalog is hard to navigate. Viso, ProMax 3D, ProMax 2D, ProOne, and the different scanner generations have overlapping names and different option lists. Match the written quote to the exact model and licensed options, not the family name.
  • Options and software modules drive the real price. CALM, cephalometric arms, specific Romexis modules, and extra volume sizes may be separate options or licenses. Ask for an itemized entitlement list and renewal terms.
  • No public pricing, and sales run through local partners. Planmeca routes ordering through distributors and says regional restrictions apply. Expect to negotiate service, installation, and training terms with the partner as well as the manufacturer.
  • Ecosystem pull. The tightest integrations (Romexis, Insights, the dental-unit scanner connection, PlanMill, Creo X) are strongest inside Planmeca’s own range. Open file formats help, but a mixed-vendor office should test its real handoffs before committing.
  • Not every product is ortho-specific. Dental units, milling, and restorative CAD/CAM are broad dentistry tools. A pure ortho office may not need most of that breadth. That is our observation, not a Planmeca statement.
  • Marketing claims need context. “Low dose,” “premium image quality,” “fast and accurate,” and AI automation claims come from Planmeca. Evaluate them in a demo with your own protocols and cases. They are not universal performance claims.
  • Catalog pages can lag product changes. For example, the old Creo C5 page now points to Creo X. Confirm that a model is still orderable in your country before you plan around it.

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

OrthoFX

Venture-backed U.S. clear aligner company (Fremont, CA) selling doctor-prescribed AirFlex, FXClear and FXBright aligners, plus retainers, FXOnTrack remote monitoring and FXPay patient financing.

HQ: Fremont, California, USA · Researched 2026-10-14 · Official site

Overview

OrthoFX is a privately held, venture-backed clear aligner company based in Fremont, California (the dateline on its own press releases). It was started by former clear-aligner industry people and raised a $13 million Series A in 2020, led by SignalFire, for about $17 million total at the time (per Crunchbase News). Early coverage pitched it as consumer-friendly. Today, the patient journey on orthofx.com runs through a doctor: a consultation and 3D scan, then treatment the doctor supervises. It sells to practices with a “Become a provider” path and a doctor login.

Its main selling point is material. OrthoFX says its AirFlex aligners use a patented “HyperElastic” polymer and calls AirFlex the first FDA-cleared aligner system for at least 12 hours of continuous daily wear. It markets this as “up to 50% less wear time” than other brands. Those are the company’s claims, footnoted on its site. Its other systems use a standard 22-hour wear protocol.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists and general dentists who want a second aligner brand built around material science instead of a giant ecosystem.
  • Practices with patients who struggle to wear aligners all day, if the doctor agrees a reduced-wear protocol fits the case.
  • Offices that want financing (FXPay) and remote monitoring (FXOnTrack) bundled with the aligner instead of bolted on.

Notable products

  • AirFlex™: The flagship. A HyperElastic polymer aligner cleared for at least 12 hours of daily wear. It comes as full and shorter plans (the company’s FAQ refers to AirFlex Full / FX10).
  • PrecisionFinish™: Finishing aligners in a separate proprietary material, announced October 2025. OrthoFX says they’re automatically included at no extra cost: the last 4 stages of comprehensive AirFlex cases and the last 2 of shorter plans. The company recommends full-time (22-hour) wear for these stages.
  • FXClear™: A stain-resistant aligner with scalloped edges and a standard 22-hour wear time.
  • FXBright™: A translucent aligner the company markets as giving a whiter look from day one without whitening gels. Standard 22-hour wear.
  • FXRetainers: Retainers for after treatment.
  • FXIntegrated Buttons™: Elastic buttons formed from the aligner polymer itself, with no bonding or welding. They launched at AAO 2026, and OrthoFX says they withstand up to 10 lb of force. Availability dates differ across OrthoFX’s own materials (“early Summer 2026,” “early Fall,” “early 2027”), so confirm with your rep.
  • FXOnTrack™ (AI-assisted remote monitoring) and FXPay™ (patient payment plans).

OrthoFX’s press page references an FDA clearance under the earlier NiTime name, with that artwork now captioned AirFlex; confirm current naming with your rep.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • A real point of difference: the only aligner system currently marketed as FDA-cleared for 12-hour continuous daily wear. That can help when compliance is the bottleneck.
  • Finishing aligners are included in AirFlex cases at no extra cost, which goes after a common aligner pain point: detailing at the end of treatment.
  • Financing and remote monitoring come built in, so a practice juggles fewer vendors.
  • A steady pace of new products (PrecisionFinish in 2025, integrated buttons in 2026).

Cons

  • A much smaller company than the category leaders, with less independent long-term evidence. Its head-to-head study against Invisalign is promoted by OrthoFX itself, so read it critically.
  • Reduced wear applies to AirFlex only. FXClear, FXBright and the PrecisionFinish stages still expect about 22 hours a day, so “half the wear time” isn’t the whole story for a case.
  • Marketing leans hard on comparisons (“up to 7.5x more force,” “50% less wear time”). These are company claims with footnotes, not independent findings.
  • No public lab-fee pricing for practices. Integrated-button timing is inconsistent across official pages.
  • Private and venture-funded, so there’s no public financial disclosure to judge long-term stability.

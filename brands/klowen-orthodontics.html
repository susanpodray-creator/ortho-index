KLOwen Orthodontics

U.S. custom digital orthodontics company (KLOwen Braces, Inc.) in Richardson, Texas, offering fully custom metal and clear brackets, in-house treatment-planning software, a digital lab, and 3D-printed indirect bonding trays, sold only to orthodontists.

HQ: Richardson, Texas, USA · Founded 2019 · Researched 2026-10-23 · Official site

Overview

KLOwen Orthodontics is a U.S. custom digital orthodontics company. The legal entity is KLOwen Braces, Inc., named in the site’s terms of service and in SEC Form D filings; FDA’s establishment registration lists it as “KLOwen Braces dba KLOwen Orthodontics.” The company says it was founded in 2019 by a practicing orthodontist.

Headquarters is Richardson, Texas. The official site doesn’t give a street address, but FDA’s registration (a North Plano Road address in Richardson), the company’s SEC Form D filings, and the dateline of its own Series B announcement all point to Richardson.

KLOwen is privately held and venture-backed. It announced a Series B round of up to $25.5 million in October 2024, following earlier seed and Series A rounds.

The offering is a single bundled system: the orthodontist submits a case in KLOwen’s own planning software, approves a digital setup, and KLOwen’s digital lab selects a bracket for each tooth and delivers the prescription through a 3D-printed indirect bonding (IDB) tray. KLOwen is a separate company from LightForce Orthodontics, which also sells custom brackets.

In the U.S.: FDA’s public database shows an active registration for the Richardson site, as a manufacturer and as a repackager/relabeler, under the name “KLOwen Orthodontic Solution.” Listings cover indirect bonding trays (a Class I device type) and plastic orthodontic brackets referencing 510(k) K251196, a 2025 clearance for composite brackets submitted through a third-party certification service rather than by KLOwen itself. We found no 510(k) in KLOwen’s own name, and no separate listing for its metal brackets in the openFDA data we checked. Ask the company for the regulatory status of each component you plan to use.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists (the company sells only to orthodontists, not general dentists or consumers)
  • Practices that want fully custom bracket prescriptions without bending wires or repositioning brackets themselves
  • Offices ready to adopt digital case submission, a vendor’s planning software, and indirect bonding as a standard workflow
  • Practices that want metal or clear bracket options, including a custom self-ligating version

Notable products

  • Custom Metal SL: custom-prescription metal self-ligating bracket with gingival-opening doors and a 19.5 x 26 slot; the company notes AAO Ortho Innovator honorable mentions in 2024 and 2025
  • Custom Metal Twin: 17-4 stainless steel twin bracket, hooked or non-hooked
  • Custom Clear Twin: clear bracket made from a ceramic-filled copolymer
  • KLOwen Software: cloud case submission, digital setup review and approval, and collaboration with the lab
  • KLOwen Digital Lab: selects brackets and builds the per-tooth prescription from the approved setup
  • 3D-printed IDB trays: custom-fit indirect bonding trays that place each bracket
  • Practice support: practice development specialists to help with implementation

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • One vendor covers planning, brackets, lab work, and bonding trays, so the pieces are designed to fit together
  • Choice of metal twin, clear twin, and metal self-ligating, including upper/lower mixes
  • Published bracket specs (19.5 slot with 19 x 25 wires) and a stated turnaround of about two weeks
  • Verifiable FDA establishment registration and a clear legal entity in public filings

Cons

  • It’s a closed system: you plan in KLOwen’s software and use its brackets and trays, so switching away means changing your whole workflow
  • Headline figures on the site (shorter treatment, fewer appointments) are the company’s own; we found no independent comparisons
  • Custom cases need lead time, so same-day starts or quick rebonds depend on the lab
  • Pricing isn’t public, and the site doesn’t publish a street address; FDA data shows a 510(k) only for the composite bracket type, cleared under another submitter’s file
  • A young, venture-funded company, with a shorter track record than long-established bracket makers

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

KLOwen suits orthodontists who want to move fixed-appliance cases onto a fully digital, custom workflow and are comfortable handing bracket selection to a vendor lab. Expect to scan, submit and approve setups in KLOwen’s software, then bond with its trays. Before adopting it, check scanner and file compatibility, approval-to-delivery timing for your case volume, how repairs and lost brackets are handled, the regulatory status of each component, and the total per-case cost compared with your current system.

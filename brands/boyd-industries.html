Boyd Industries

Clearwater, Florida maker of specialty operatory equipment since 1957—orthodontic and pediatric treatment chairs, chairside and rear delivery units, LED exam lights, operator stools, monitor mounts, and custom clinical, sterilization, and 3D-printing cabinetry, built largely in-house in the USA.

HQ: Clearwater, Florida, USA · Founded 1957 · Researched 2026-10-05 · Official site

Overview

Boyd Industries, Inc. is a U.S. manufacturer of specialty operatory equipment: patient exam and treatment chairs, delivery systems, exam lights, clinician seating, and clinical cabinetry. Orthodontics is one of its core markets, along with pediatric dentistry, oral surgery, and endodontics, and more recently ENT and other medical specialties. It does not make brackets, wires, aligners, or imaging. For an ortho office, Boyd belongs in the operatory build-out conversation.

The live site is boydindustries.com. Boyd’s About page says the company was founded in 1957 by William Gray Boyd in St. Petersburg, Florida, as a custom residential furniture maker. Its contoured “lounge” chair inspired the logo. Boyd describes its founder as one of the pioneers of “sit-down” dentistry. After that approach caught on, the company narrowed its focus to specialists: orthodontists, oral surgeons, pediatric dentists, endodontists, and select medical specialties. Boyd says it entered a new phase in 2012, when current CEO Adrian LaTrace acquired the company.

Headquarters and manufacturing: the contact page gives the address as 12900 44th Street North, Clearwater, FL 33762, and the facility page describes a 63,000 sq ft Clearwater building that houses all staff and production. Boyd calls it a vertically integrated plant. In-house work includes cutting and sewing upholstery, machining steel parts, welding and powder-coating chair frames, thermoforming Kydex covers, and running a CNC cabinet shop. Boyd says the factory is FDA-registered. An older Boyd news post’s company boilerplate also describes Boyd as an ISO 13485:2016 certified manufacturer. Ask for the current certificate if that matters for your procurement.

Recent corporate moves (sourced):

  • SMR ENT line (2023). Boyd acquired Global Surgical’s SMR ENT equipment brand (exam chairs, microscopes, treatment cabinets). Dental Products Report covered it on March 8, 2023. This is why ENT chairs and cabinets now appear next to dental products in Boyd’s catalog.
  • Canada (2026). On August 25, 2026, Boyd named Medline Sinclair its exclusive Canadian distributor. Per Boyd’s release, Medline Sinclair has handled sales, installation coordination, support, and service for Boyd dental operatory equipment in Canada since August 4, 2026.

Product names and specs below come from live Boyd product pages researched on October 5, 2026. Configurations, upholstery, and options change, so treat this page as a map, not a quote.

Who it fits

  • Orthodontic offices designing open-bay or multi-chair clinics where chair reliability, quick positioning, and chairside storage matter more than full GP dental-unit instrumentation
  • Practices that want one manufacturer for chairs, delivery, lights, stools, and cabinetry, with custom cabinetry built to the floor plan instead of only catalog modules
  • Combined ortho + pediatric practices, since most of Boyd’s chairs and delivery units are tagged for both specialties, and its pediatric line adds benches, concealed delivery, and brushing stations
  • Offices adding in-house 3D printing (aligners, retainer models) that want a purpose-built print, wash, and cure workstation
  • Buyers who value U.S. manufacturing and a parts/labor warranty from the factory that builds the chair
  • New grads or practices leaving the military, who may qualify for Boyd’s Resident/First Office in-house financing program (see notes)

Notable products

Patient exam and treatment chairs

Boyd’s orthodontic chair category lists the M300X, M3000FB, M3000LC, M3000LS, M3010FB/LC/LS, P3000LS, E530, and E535, plus a pediatric booster seat. Shared features across the M-series pages: all-steel frames, ergonomically designed winged backs, low-voltage DC motor actuators, integrated full-function foot control, three operator-programmable preset positions (on the programmable models), an auto “return to home” switch, snap-on/snap-off upholstery, and a clear plastic foot-protector cover. Standard upholstery is Boltaflex™ vinyl with a Prefixx™ finish in over 100 colors, with an Ultraleather™ upgrade.

  • M3000LC — Boyd calls it its most popular orthodontic and pediatric treatment chair. Lift-column base with 8” motorized vertical lift, contoured one-piece seat, and FH-series fixed headrests. Options include a lever-release armrest, a swivel base (41.5° from center), an articulating headrest, and premium upholstery.
  • M3000LS — updated version of the LC. Boyd says it starts 2” lower at home and reclines up to 35% faster (Boyd’s footnote bases that on a 250 lb patient). It has independent motors for lift and recline, a programmable foot control, FDA-listed/ETL-approved labeling, and a 45° swivel option.
  • M3010LC / M3010LS / M3010FB — the same platforms with a two-piece seat and synchronized drop toe, which Boyd positions for exams and consultations. FB models use a fixed pedestal base instead of a lift column.
  • M3000FB / M300X — fixed-pedestal chairs with motorized back recline. Boyd calls the M300X its most economical chair (manual back-adjustment lever, auto return-to-home). Both can take a chair-mounted exam light.
  • P3000LS — pediatric-focused version of the M3000LS, with armrests and a single-articulating headrest standard and a 19” seat height.
  • E530 / E535 — Boyd calls these its “records room” solution: manually synchronized back and toe, armrests, LL-69 double-articulating headrest, and 360° base swivel. The E530 has a pump lift base and the E535 a motorized lift base.

Delivery systems

  • CSU-356 series — Boyd’s most popular chairside delivery unit, under 25” wide with a tapered cabinet. Automatic delivery with two handpieces, HVE, saliva ejector, and 3-way syringe, plus an external medical-grade duplex with USB and an internal fused ETL-rated receptacle. Construction includes ¾” plywood, soft-close undermount glides with dovetailed hardwood drawer boxes, and a laminate top or Corian™/Silestone™ upgrade, on a mobile or free-standing base.
  • CSU356SL Slim Line — a 15”-wide cabinet (single-tier or two-tier SL2). Boyd says it holds the same storage as its tapered or square units in 9½” less width, with four full-size drawers, left- or right-hand setup, and optional CWS-07 self-contained water. Useful for packing more chairs into an open bay.
  • CSU-362 / CSU-370SL / SQ variants — the CSU-362 is the widest at 27¾” for more storage. The CSU-370SL is a 19”-wide retraction unit that can be used for side or rear delivery.
  • CSU-456SQ — Boyd’s next-generation chairside unit: Corian work surface, countertop under-mounted 1.7 qt sharps container with a lockable side door, push-to-open top drawers (fewer glove changes), an internal control panel, and options such as a waste chute, CaviWipes holder, and CWS-07 water.
  • CSU-320 / AP-11 — chair-mounted options. The CSU-320 is a side chair-mount doctor unit that swivels 150°. The AP-11 is a swing-mount assistant package (3-way syringe, HVE, saliva ejector, solids collector) that fits the 300X, M3000, and M3010 chairs.
  • CDU-415 / CDU-425 concealed delivery and Rear Delivery Units — Boyd designed these with pediatric practices in mind, but they are also tagged for orthodontics. Handpieces stay out of the patient’s sight in a pull-out drawer. The rear units support four-handed dentistry, with a self-contained 1 L clean-water bottle and a pocket door for a customer-supplied nitrous unit.

Boyd’s FAQ says its delivery units carry FDA 510(k) clearance and that products conform to IEC 60601, with CSA (Canada) and CE (Europe) marks referenced.

Cabinetry and specialty stations

  • Custom clinical cabinetry. Boyd’s FAQ says it builds “the widest variety of cabinetry options,” and its designers can adapt standard configurations or design fully custom cabinetry. Wood components come from Boyd’s own cabinet shop.
  • Center Islands — divide and serve open bays with ¾” plywood, Corian or laminate tops, a stainless sink with faucet, paper-towel dispenser, 1–3 drawer banks, and options like a trash-chute ring, glove/mask dispensers, and Tap Master hands-free faucets.
  • Sterilization Center cabinetry, tooth-brushing stations (optional stainless sink, towel dispenser, trash chute), and padded storage benches for patient-facing areas.
  • 3D Printing Workstation — a modular station built to fit most 3D printers, with an internal air scrubber for fumes, separate rinse and cure stations, a mobile post-processing cart, a beveled waste drawer, and pull-out storage for finished parts.

Lighting, seating, mounts, and sterilization accessories

  • C300 LED Exam Light — 4-LED oval head at 5000 K, intensity adjustable up to 30,000 lux, 95 Ra color rendering, IR sensor, sterilizer-safe handles, three-axis head, and chair-post, ceiling, or wall mounts. Boyd lists it as FDA-listed and UL-approved.
  • C600 LED Exam Light — touch panel with separate dental and bonding modes, a 5,000–50,000 lux dial, motion-sensor on/off, and detachable sterilizer-safe handles. The bonding mode matters in ortho to avoid curing adhesive too early. Confirm the spectrum and mode behavior with your adhesive’s instructions.
  • Operator and assistant stools (BOS series) — several models. For example, the BOS-46 has a round seat, a five-caster aluminum base, and pneumatic height (doctor 19”–24”, assistant 22”–30”).
  • Monitor mounts — FS, UC, and SM series for single, dual, Apple, or under-counter screens. For example, the FS-21 holds two 22” monitors with 75/100 mm VESA.
  • DryCurve 1100 instrument dryer — a stainless countertop or built-in dryer with two cassette-sized trays and a 20-minute heated-airflow cycle before sterilization.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Built for specialists. Boyd has focused on orthodontics, pediatrics, and oral surgery for decades, and its catalog shows it: lift-column chairs, drop-toe consult chairs, records-room chairs, slim delivery cabinets, and bonding-mode lighting.
  • Vertically integrated U.S. manufacturing. Upholstery, steel frames, plastics, and cabinetry are made in Clearwater, so parts and finishes come from the same factory that built the chair.
  • Lots of ways to fit an open bay. Widths from the 15” Slim Line to the 27¾” CSU-362, plus side, rear, concealed, and chair-mounted delivery, make it easier to fit the chair count you want.
  • Upholstery choice. Over 100 vinyl colors, Ultraleather upgrades, stitch patterns, and custom embroidered toe sections on several chairs make it easy to match a practice’s look.
  • Published buying terms. The FAQ states lead times, deposit terms, FOB point, return policy, and warranty in plain language, which helps when comparing quotes.
  • Service reach. Boyd says it uses a network of 300+ technicians across North America for warranty and non-warranty service.
  • Newer workflow pieces. The 3D-printing workstation, push-to-open and countertop-sharps CSU-456SQ, and slim delivery units show the line is still evolving beyond legacy chair SKUs.

Cons / watch-outs

  • Freight and installation are extra. Boyd quotes everything FOB Clearwater, Florida. Budget for shipping, receiving, install, and plumbing/electrical tie-ins on top of equipment prices.
  • Custom means hard to undo. Returns are typically not accepted because of the custom nature of the equipment, except for factory errors against the signed acknowledgement or drawings (other exceptions only with approval and a restocking fee). Check drawings, finishes, and handedness carefully before signing.
  • Cash flow. Doctor-direct orders call for a 50% deposit with the balance net 30. Standard configurations ship about 6 weeks after the signed acknowledgement and deposit, and custom work about 8 weeks after drawings are approved. “Quick Ship” (about 2 weeks) requires 100% prepayment and may not be available in peak seasons.
  • Warranty limits. Boyd-manufactured products carry 3 years parts / 1 year labor from the invoice date. Distributed (non-Boyd) products carry only their own maker’s warranty. Service must be done by an authorized technician with Boyd pre-approval, and service logs must be kept. Third-party installation damage and freight damage are excluded.
  • No office design service. Boyd’s FAQ says it does not offer office design. It will coordinate equipment and utility locations with your architect or contractor and can refer you to designers.
  • Water-system claims need context. The FAQ calls Boyd the only orthodontic manufacturer with a “truly sterile” delivery water system, then calls it a “clean” system. Treat this as manufacturer positioning. Follow your own waterline testing and infection-control protocol, and verify any certifications rather than relying on marketing wording.
  • Marketing and tax framing. Boyd’s home page features a Section 179 tax-savings slider and notes that it is not tax advice. Talk to your own tax professional before timing a purchase around it.
  • No imaging or practice software. Boyd is chairs, delivery, lights, and cabinetry. Plan CBCT/pan/ceph, scanners, sterilizers (beyond the dryer and sterilization cabinetry), compressors, and vacuums separately.
  • Catalog quirks. Some product URLs and names don’t match (for example, the C600 light sits at a “c510” URL, and the M300X page mentions a “C510” light). Some FAQ sections are labeled for the wrong specialty. Confirm exact model numbers on the formal quote.

Evaluation notes

  • Start from the floor plan: chair count, bay width, and whether delivery should be side, rear, concealed, or chair-mounted. Then pick delivery widths (15”, under 25”, 27¾”) before choosing finishes.
  • Pick chairs by job. A lift-column chair (M3000LC/LS) fits bonding and adjustment chairs, a drop-toe M3010 fits consult or exam rooms, and an E530/E535 fits a records room. Ask for demo time on recline speed, low home height, and headrest choice.
  • Decide on upholstery durability vs. aesthetics (Boltaflex vs. Ultraleather) for high-turnover ortho chairs, and get samples before locking colors.
  • If curing at chairside, check that the light’s bonding mode works with your adhesive and curing-light workflow.
  • Get the full payment schedule, freight, install, plumbing/electrical, and water-system choice (CWS-07 self-contained vs. municipal) in writing, along with who the local authorized service technician is.
  • Ask whether you’re buying factory-direct through a Boyd sales rep or through an authorized dealer, and how that affects install coordination and warranty labor. In Canada, purchases go through Medline Sinclair as of August 2026.
  • New practices should ask about the Resident/First Office “10/12 Program”, in-house financing of up to $50,000 for residents or people leaving active-duty military, versus Boyd’s Bank of America Practice Solutions program. Compare terms against your own lender.

Bottom line

Boyd Industries is a long-running, Florida-built specialty operatory brand worth shortlisting for treatment chairs, chairside delivery, exam lights, stools, and custom cabinetry. The core ortho picks are the M3000LC/LS treatment chairs, M3010 consult chairs, E530/E535 records chairs, CSU-356 / Slim Line / CSU-456SQ delivery units, and center islands for open bays. A 3D-printing workstation is available for in-house aligner and retainer work.

The trade-offs are practical, not clinical: FOB freight and install costs, a 50% deposit, 6–8 week lead times, a strict no-returns policy on custom work, and the need to source imaging and mechanical-room equipment elsewhere. Lock the floor plan, finishes, and handedness before signing.

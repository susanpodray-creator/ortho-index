Zendura (Bay Materials)

Thermoplastic sheet materials for making clear aligners, retainers and attachment templates, sold under the Zendura Dental brand by Bay Materials, LLC, a Straumann Group company based in Andover, Massachusetts. Product lines include Zendura FLX and Zendura VIVA (aligners), Zendura A and Zendura Guard (retainers), and Zendura AT and Zendura TRAK (attachment templates).

HQ: Andover, Massachusetts, USA · Researched 2026-11-25 · Official site

Overview

Zendura is a family of thermoplastic sheet materials used to make clear aligners, clear retainers and attachment templates. Labs, aligner manufacturers and practices with in-house labs heat the sheets and press them over printed or poured models (thermoforming), then trim and polish the result. Zendura does not plan treatment or ship finished aligners itself; it supplies the plastic that other companies and labs turn into appliances.

The brand belongs to Bay Materials, LLC, which now trades as Zendura Dental. Bay Materials has been part of the Straumann Group since October 2019, when Straumann announced it had acquired the company outright. Straumann described it as the group’s center of excellence for aligner and retainer thermoplastics, and said it would keep selling the materials to outside manufacturers as well as using them in its own aligner production. Straumann also owns ClearCorrect; this page does not assume ClearCorrect uses Zendura, as neither company states that on the pages we reviewed.

Location. The company’s current website and its August 2026 instructions for use give a global headquarters in Andover, Massachusetts, and the company says its materials are made in Massachusetts. Older sources, including Straumann’s 2019 announcement and several published studies, place Bay Materials in Fremont, California. Ortho Index uses Andover based on the company’s current materials.

Founding year. Straumann’s 2019 release says Bay Materials was founded in 1999; the company’s own about page says 2000. Because the two conflict, Ortho Index leaves the founding year out. Both agree the company began as a polymer research firm in Silicon Valley.

Regulatory status. We found no FDA 510(k) clearance in Bay Materials’ own name and no FDA establishment registration under that name in openFDA’s public data. That is not unusual for a raw sheet material: in the US, the 510(k) generally belongs to the company that makes and markets the finished aligner. Zendura materials do show up inside other companies’ clearances. FDA summaries for the uLab Systems Dental Aligner Kit (K192596, 2020; K211510, 2022) list Zendura A or Zendura FLX as the aligner material, and STR8 Clear Aligners (K223141, 2023) and Clear Moves Aligners (K240038, 2024) list Zendura FLX. These are sequential aligners in product code NXC, Class II. The STR8 summary says biocompatibility testing of the material was done by its manufacturer under ISO 10993-1. If you make appliances from Zendura in your own office, ask the company and your regulatory adviser what that means for your practice.

Who it’s for

  • Commercial dental and orthodontic labs that thermoform aligners, retainers or attachment templates.
  • Aligner companies that buy sheet material rather than making their own.
  • Orthodontic and dental practices that run an in-house lab and want to make aligners or retainers on their own printers and thermoformers.

It is a professional product. The instructions for use say the materials are for appliances designed by a registered orthodontist or dental lab technician and are for professional users.

Notable products

Product names and descriptions below are as the company states them.

  • Zendura FLX: the company’s main aligner material, a three-layer sheet with two harder outer layers around a softer elastomeric middle. Sold at 0.76 mm thickness in round and square formats for common pressure thermoformers. The company claims up to 150% better force retention than single-layer materials and about 40% lower initial force; treat these as company claims based on its own testing.
  • Zendura VIVA: a newer three-layer aligner material at 0.89 mm, which the company says is built on the FLX design and delivers higher sustained force than FLX and the competitor materials it tested.
  • Zendura A: a single-layer polyurethane sheet the company now positions mainly for retainers, though FDA summaries also list it as an aligner material.
  • Zendura Guard: a newer three-layer retainer material that the company says is softer and more tear-resistant than a common competing retainer sheet in its own lab tests.
  • Zendura AT and Zendura TRAK: thin sheets for attachment templates, the trays used to place composite attachments before aligner treatment. The company describes AT as stiffer and more positionally accurate and TRAK as more flexible and easier to remove.

The company sells direct through online stores for North America and for Europe, the Middle East and Africa, and through authorized distributors in other countries. Its distributor page notes that product availability varies by country and depends on local regulatory approvals.

Pros / cons

Pros

  • Widely used. Zendura materials appear as the named aligner material in several cleared US aligner systems, so it is a known quantity for labs and regulators.
  • A broad, single-supplier range covering aligners, retainers and attachment templates, with published instructions for use, safety data sheets and thermoformer settings.
  • A meaningful body of independent research. One in vitro study found Zendura A wore less than two other retainer materials under simulated night-time grinding. Studies of FLX and VIVA found that the multilayer sheets deliver lower forces than single-layer sheets, and one found less force decay after ageing, especially for VIVA.
  • Backed by a large parent company, which may matter for supply continuity.

Cons

  • It is only a material. Results depend on the planning, models, thermoforming, trimming and finishing done by whoever makes the appliance.
  • Independent results are mixed. One study found Zendura and another retainer material had the most surface wear of the four it tested, with Zendura absorbing the most water in artificial saliva. Another found Zendura’s yield stress dropped about 30% after thermoforming.
  • An in vitro study found slight cytotoxicity for all four aligner materials it tested, including Zendura. A separate study found that bisphenol release from Zendura A and FLX was below reference intake levels under its test conditions.
  • Many of the headline performance numbers on the website come from the company’s own testing against named competitors. They are not independent.
  • The material must be thermoformed within about 15 minutes of opening its moisture-barrier pouch, according to the instructions for use, which leaves little room for delay in a busy lab.
  • No 510(k) in the company’s own name was found, so the regulatory picture for your own in-house appliances depends on how you use the material.

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

Most practices meet Zendura indirectly, as the plastic inside aligners or retainers from a lab or aligner company. It becomes a direct purchasing decision if you fabricate in-house: you would pair it with a 3D printer, model resin, a compatible pressure thermoformer and trimming tools, and follow the thermoformer settings in the instructions for use.

When evaluating it, ask:

  • Which Zendura material your lab or aligner provider actually uses, and at what thickness.
  • Whether the material and your workflow are covered by any FDA clearance, or what your regulatory obligations are if you make appliances in-house.
  • Whether your thermoformer is on the supported list, and the shelf life and storage conditions for opened and unopened stock.
  • What independent data exists for the specific material you plan to use, rather than for the range as a whole.
  • Pricing, minimum orders and lead times from the direct store or your regional distributor.

These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

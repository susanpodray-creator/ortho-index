OnyxCeph³™

Windows-based orthodontic imaging and planning software from Image Instruments GmbH (Chemnitz, Germany), covering 2D ceph analysis, 3D model work, virtual setups, aligner and retainer design, indirect bonding trays, and STL export, with modular, publicly listed EUR pricing.

HQ: Chemnitz, Germany · Founded 1999 · Researched 2026-10-16 · Official site

Overview

OnyxCeph³™ is orthodontic imaging and treatment-planning software made by Image Instruments GmbH, a software company in Chemnitz, Germany. The company’s legal notice says it was founded on April 27, 1999, lists Dr. Rolf Kühnert and Dr. Tom Kühnert as managing directors and owners, and describes its business as developing, manufacturing, and marketing image-based measuring systems. The old onyxceph.de and onyxceph.com addresses now redirect to onyxceph.eu.

The software started out as a cephalometric tool and now covers much of the digital ortho workflow in one database: 2D ceph and photo analysis, 3D model analysis and segmentation, virtual setups, aligner and retainer design, indirect bonding trays and jigs, surgical wafers, and export of 3D data for printing. It’s sold as a set of separately priced modules on top of a base version.

Unlike most ortho software, OnyxCeph publishes its prices. As of October 2026, the official price list shows PRO at €3,600 one-off (RL+ purchase license) or €400 a year (OSL subscription), and LAB at €4,800 or €690 a year. Both have a mandatory annual maintenance fee, and optional 3D modules cost roughly €600 to €1,800 each, all plus VAT. Prices shown are for licenses bought direct; distributor pricing may differ.

Regulatory status: The license page says an unregistered trial install “has no CE approval as a medical product” and may not be used for real diagnosis or treatment planning. That implies registered copies carry CE marking in the EU. We did not find a U.S. FDA status stated on the official site.

U.S. availability: The company’s “Users Worldwide” page lists the USA and Canada among countries with licensed users. Image Instruments sells direct and through distributors. Its support page says it does not provide hands-on user support itself and points users to independent training partners. We did not find a named U.S. distributor or support partner on the official pages we reviewed.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontic and oral-surgery practices that want in-house ceph analysis, virtual setups, and appliance design without a per-case fee to an outside platform
  • Practices printing their own aligners, retainers, or indirect bonding trays and needing STL/OBJ/PLY export
  • Orthodontic labs and dental service providers (the LAB version, with more concurrent clients)
  • Teaching programs (a non-commercial EDU version)
  • Offices running Windows on a local server network

Notable products

  • OnyxCeph³™ PRO: for ortho and OMS practices using 2D images and 3D data. Up to 5 concurrent network clients, per the feature table.
  • OnyxCeph³™ LAB: for labs and service providers. Up to 20 concurrent clients.
  • OnyxCeph³™ EDU: a non-commercial version for training institutions.
  • 2D modules: Digitize (ceph tracing and analysis), Combine, Edit, Mirror, Image Comparison, Treatment Simulation, CO-CR Conversion, and Ricketts V.T.O.
  • 3D modules: Segmentation, Cast Adjust, V.T.O.3D (virtual setup), Aligner 3D, Retainer 3D, Bonding Trays 3D, Bonding Jigs 3D, FA_Bonding, Wire_Bonding, Bracket Adapt, Bracket Erase, Ortho Apps 3D (virtual appliance construction), Bite Splint 3D, Waefer Creation 3D, Sim 3D (combined ortho/surgical planning), TADmatch™ (mini-implant planning), Smile 3D, and Articulate.
  • Add-on programs: OnyxAdmin (user permissions), OnyxStat (search and statistics across findings), and OnyxSync (syncing between databases).

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Transparent, published pricing and a choice between buying outright and subscribing
  • A broad, modular toolkit, so a practice can pay only for the modules it uses
  • In-house aligner, retainer, and bonding-tray design with open 3D export, so you’re not tied to one lab or printer
  • A long track record (company founded 1999) and a detailed public wiki for documentation
  • A free trial with full 3D Pro features (limited to test cases, not real patients)

Cons

  • Windows only. On a Mac it needs Boot Camp; Parallels isn’t supported.
  • Local network only. The company says VPN-to-server and cloud server setups aren’t supported, which rules out a cloud-first office.
  • Support is limited. Image Instruments says it doesn’t provide user support itself and refers clinicians to independent training partners, so setup help depends on who you buy from.
  • No named U.S. distributor on the official site, and no FDA status stated
  • With this many modules there’s a learning curve, and the costs add up as you add 3D options
  • The site’s English is sometimes rough, with a few untranslated German labels

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

OnyxCeph sits on the practice server as the imaging and planning hub. Ceph X-rays, photos, and intraoral or model scans go into one SQL database. Each workstation logs in over the local network, and a second standalone copy (on a laptop or home PC) can sync through container export/import or OnyxSync. A typical in-house path is: import the scan, segment it, build a virtual setup in V.T.O.3D, then design aligners, retainers, or bonding trays and export STL files to your printer or lab. Before you buy, have your IT person check the Windows and GPU requirements, and line up a training partner for onboarding.

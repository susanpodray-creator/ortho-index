OrthoPulse (Biolux)

Near-infrared light (photobiomodulation) accessory worn during orthodontic treatment, sold as OrthoPulse 2.0 and the newer OrthoPulse lite. Originally developed by Biolux Research Ltd. of Vancouver; the brand is now run by Biolux Technology GmbH of Austria, part of the Swiss Biolux Group, and remains on sale with FDA 510(k) clearance.

HQ: Absdorf, Lower Austria, Austria · Researched 2026-11-16 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

OrthoPulse is an at-home mouthpiece that shines low-level near-infrared light (850 nm, according to the company) on the bone around the front teeth during orthodontic treatment. The technique is called photobiomodulation (PBM). It is prescribed by an orthodontist or dentist and used alongside braces or aligners, in short daily sessions of about 5 minutes per arch. The company markets it to speed up tooth movement, reduce discomfort and help aligners “track”; those are company claims (see Pros / cons).

Ownership and history: OrthoPulse was developed by Biolux Research Ltd. of Vancouver, British Columbia, which obtained the original FDA clearance in 2015 and the first Health Canada licence in 2014. The Canadian trademark record for ORTHOPULSE (which claims use in Canada since at least April 2010) shows the mark moving from Biolux Research Ltd. to Biolux Research Holdings, Inc. (Hong Kong) in April 2019, then to LLLT Technologies SA, a Swiss corporation, in April 2021. LLLT Technologies SA changed its name to Biolux Group SA (Anières, Switzerland) in July 2025.

Today the brand is run by Biolux Technology GmbH, which the OrthoPulse imprint and the IFUs name as the manufacturer of record. The Austrian company register shows it was registered in March 2020, has its registered office in Absdorf, Lower Austria, and has had LLLT Technologies SA as its sole shareholder since April 2024. The company also gives a mailing address in Vienna. Its 2025 press release describes Biolux Technology as “headquartered in Switzerland with U.S. operations in New York,” and U.S. press contacts are listed under Biolux Technology USA LLC. The 2023 FDA clearance went to Biolux Technology GmbH rather than Biolux Research Ltd. We found no public announcement describing exactly how OrthoPulse passed from Biolux Research to Biolux Technology, or whether the original Vancouver company still operates. We treat this as an asset and trademark transfer within the Biolux group and have not confirmed further details. Because the original company and the current operator were founded at different times, Ortho Index has left the founding year blank.

Still sold? Yes, as far as public sources show. The official site is active, its newsroom ran announcements through 2026, and it offers current IFUs for OrthoPulse lite and OrthoPulse 2.0. In January 2026 the company announced Canadian distribution through Ortho Arch. Its IFUs list distributors or sponsors in Australia and Italy.

FDA status (openFDA): Two 510(k) clearances are on record under product code PLH (orthodontic LED accessory; Class II, 21 CFR 872.5470; prescription use):

  • K143120 (July 24, 2015): OrthoPulse, applicant Biolux Research Ltd., Vancouver. Traditional 510(k). Cleared for use “during orthodontic treatment… in conjunction with brackets and wires or aligners” to help “facilitate minor anterior tooth movement.”
  • K230905 (June 9, 2023): OrthoPulse 2.0E (model OPi2E-100), applicant Biolux Technology GmbH, Absdorf, Austria. Special 510(k) with K143120 as predicate. Its indications add that the device “is intended to accelerate orthodontic movement of teeth which may reduce the overall treatment time.” The summary says no new clinical data were needed because the technology was unchanged from the original device.

We found no De Novo record for OrthoPulse. FDA’s device listing database shows Biolux Technology GmbH listing OrthoPulse Lite and OrthoPulse Standard Gen 2 under K143120, and OrthoPulse Extended Gen 2 under K230905. The listing also names contract manufacturers in Serbia (Gen 2) and Delta, British Columbia (Lite). There is no separate 510(k) under the OrthoPulse lite name, and we could not confirm how the lite design changes were documented with FDA.

Health Canada: Biolux Research Ltd.’s original ORTHOPULSE licence was first issued in November 2014 and ended in December 2020. A new Class II device licence, ORTHOPULSE GEN 2.0 (Standard and Extended), was issued in February 2026; the listed licence holder is Ortho Arch Company Inc., the Canadian distribution partner. We found no Health Canada licence under the OrthoPulse lite name.

Europe: The company says OrthoPulse is “EU MDR approved,” and the OrthoPulse 2.0 IFU carries a statement of compliance with Regulation (EU) 2017/745. We did not find the notified body or certificate details on official pages.

Who it’s for

Orthodontic and dental offices treating patients with clear aligners or fixed appliances that want to offer a prescription, patient-use light accessory. The company also pitches it to DSOs, orthodontic support organizations and aligner brands through partnership programs.

Notable products

  • OrthoPulse 2.0: the current full-size device, sold in two models, Standard (OPi2S-100) and Extended (OPi2E-100, with a longer mouthpiece flange and more LEDs, per the K230905 summary). It has a wireless charging case and syncs by Bluetooth.
  • OrthoPulse lite: launched in December 2025 as a lower-cost version. It uses the same 850 nm near-infrared approach, with a mouthpiece that plugs into a separate battery unit by cable. The company says its price lets practices include it in every case; pricing isn’t published.
  • OrthoPulse app and Doctor Portal: a patient app for reminders and tracking, and a web portal where providers see usage and compliance data.
  • OsseoPulse (legacy): an earlier Biolux Research extra-oral phototherapy system. Health Canada records show its licence ended in 2017; we found no sign it is sold today.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • FDA 510(k) clearances (K143120, K230905) and a current Health Canada licence are on public record, with published FDA summaries
  • Actively sold and supported, with current multilingual IFUs, a 24-month warranty on registration, and distribution in North America, Europe and Australia
  • Short daily routine at home, with no added in-office procedure
  • The app and portal give providers usage data

Cons

  • The company claims OrthoPulse can cut treatment time “by up to 50%,” reduce pain “up to 70 %” and reduce refinements. Independent evidence is weaker. The 2023 Cochrane update rated the evidence “low to very low certainty” and concluded that “there is no additional benefit of light vibrational forces or photobiomodulation for reducing the duration of orthodontic treatment. Although there may be a limited benefit from photobiomodulation application for accelerating discrete treatment phases, these results have to be interpreted with caution due to their questionable clinical significance.” A 2026 umbrella review concluded that “current evidence is insufficient to recommend the routine clinical use of LLLT/PBM or vitamin D in orthodontics.” Treat speed and time-saving figures as company claims.
  • Several recent company data points (for example, fewer visits or shorter aligner treatment) come from single-practice, preliminary analyses
  • Corporate history is complicated (Canadian origin, Hong Kong and Swiss holding entities, Austrian operator), and the transfer from Biolux Research isn’t publicly documented
  • OrthoPulse lite has no separately named 510(k) or Health Canada licence that we could find
  • Pricing isn’t published, and adoption depends on patients using it every day

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

OrthoPulse is an add-on to aligner or fixed-appliance treatment, not a treatment system on its own. The practice prescribes and dispenses the device, and the patient uses it at home while staff can check compliance in the portal. When evaluating it, ask Biolux or its local distributor for:

  • the cleared indications and current IFU for the specific model (2.0 Standard, 2.0 Extended or lite)
  • regulatory status in your country, including how OrthoPulse lite is covered
  • per-device pricing and any bundle or program terms
  • warranty, replacement and support arrangements
  • data and privacy terms for the app and portal

Weigh the company’s claims against the independent reviews above. These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

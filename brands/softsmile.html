SoftSmile

Clear-aligner treatment-planning software and services from SoftSmile Inc. of New York. VISION, its FDA 510(k)-cleared planning software, is offered to labs, aligner brands, and clinicians, alongside a case portal, an outsourced design service, and the company's own SoftSmile Aligners system.

HQ: New York, New York, USA · Researched 2026-11-13 · Official site

Overview

SoftSmile makes clear-aligner treatment-planning software. Its website footer names the operator SoftSmile Inc. at 175 Varick St, New York, NY 10014, and the company’s press releases are datelined New York. Its flagship product is VISION, software that turns intraoral scans into a staged aligner treatment plan and exports 3D models for printing. Around VISION, the company now sells a cloud case portal, an outsourced design service, and its own doctor-facing aligner system, SoftSmile Aligners.

Founding: The company’s October 2026 press release says SoftSmile is “a New York-based dental technology company founded in 2020.” Hub71’s startup directory and several third-party company databases give 2019 instead. Because the two disagree, Ortho Index has left the founding year out of the summary fields.

Ownership and funding: SoftSmile appears to be privately held and independent. We found no public report that it has been acquired or merged. A January 2023 year-in-review letter on the company blog thanks unnamed “investors”. SoftSmile was among the startups picked for Hub71, Abu Dhabi’s government-backed tech-startup program, in late 2022, and Hub71 lists SoftSmile in its startup directory. The company has not published funding amounts or a list of investors. Dollar figures on third-party databases vary and are not confirmed here.

Offices: New York is the stated headquarters. The careers page says the company has offices in New York, Paris, and Abu Dhabi. The January 2023 letter said it had opened offices in Abu Dhabi and Cyprus during 2022. We found no current official statement of where software development or treatment-plan design staff are based, so treat any other R&D locations as unconfirmed. Public FDA records list SoftSmile, Inc. at a Delaware address rather than the New York office.

Scale (company figures, not independently verified): The website claims 130+ U.S. patents, which it lists on a virtual patent-marking page, plus 1,000+ doctors, 200,000+ patients treated, and more than 10,000 VISION treatment plans exported a month across all channels. The 2026 release says those numbers span licensed software, design work for aligner brands and labs, and SoftSmile’s own aligner service.

Who it’s for

  • Aligner labs and aligner brands that design and manufacture aligners themselves. The VISION page targets orthodontic labs and says more than 50 labs use it. The design-service page says SoftSmile has done setups for dozens of aligner brands.
  • Orthodontists and dentists who want to plan aligners in-house, either to print them in the office or to send files to a fabricator of their choice. The company points solo practitioners to a version called Dr. Vision.
  • Practices that want to outsource planning, through the OrthoDesign service or through SoftSmile Aligners, where the company does the setup and an orthodontist on its team plans each case.

Notable products

  • VISION (planning software): Company pages describe automatic tooth segmentation and scan cleanup, AI-assisted automatic setup, staging, collision detection, attachment and elastic planning, a virtual articulator, CBCT support for viewing roots and bone, and “matching”, which remodels a plan against progress scans. For production, VISION exports STL and OBJ files and can prepare hollow, ribbed, or solid print models with cut lines, using export presets for common printing labs. The company says VISION is currently built for aligners, not brackets. The “20–30x faster” claim and the “most precise” segmentation claim are the company’s own.
  • Dr. Vision and Vision LAB: The portal page names these as clinician-focused and lab-focused editions of VISION. Public pages don’t spell out exactly how they differ, so confirm the feature set during a demo.
  • VISION Portal, Vision Viewer, and Vision Editor: The Portal is a browser-based, cloud-stored case management space with role-based access, case timelines, and version history. The Vision Viewer lets doctors, staff, and patients view a plan in a browser. The Vision Editor lets a clinician move teeth and add or remove attachments, bite ramps, and elastics before approving. The company says the Portal isn’t sold on its own and is included with its software or design purchases.
  • VISION Forecast: A Portal module announced in October 2026. The company describes it as AI decision support that summarizes a new case’s complexity, estimates how many aligners it may need, and shows similar past cases. It is new, and Ortho Index has not seen independent evaluation of it.
  • SoftSmile OrthoDesign: An outsourced aligner-design service. Practices or labs upload scans and photos, then review and approve the design in Vision Viewer before receiving STL files.
  • SoftSmile Aligners: The company’s own aligner system for doctors. SoftSmile says its orthodontists plan each case in VISION, the treating doctor can edit the plan, and the aligners are made in the U.S. by an unnamed “exclusive partner lab.” It also runs a separate patient-facing marketing site. The company’s marketing calls the aligners “FDA-cleared”, but see the FDA status below.
  • Integrations: In February 2024, SoftSmile announced a Medit Link connection that lets users pull Medit intraoral scans straight into the Portal. Otherwise, VISION accepts STL uploads from any scanner and exports standard files for 3D printers and outside labs.

FDA status: openFDA and the FDA 510(k) database list one clearance under a SoftSmile name: K212770, “Vision”, submitted by SoftSmile, Inc. and found substantially equivalent on December 21, 2021. It is a Class II device under product code PNN (orthodontic software), with additional code LLZ. The cleared use is software for managing and analyzing orthodontic models and for the virtual design of a series of dental casts, which may be used to make sequential aligner trays or retainers. It requires trained users. A search of openFDA found no aligner or material clearance under a SoftSmile name. Any FDA clearance behind the “FDA-cleared” wording for SoftSmile Aligners presumably belongs to the unnamed partner lab or its materials, which we could not confirm.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • VISION has an FDA 510(k) clearance (K212770) as orthodontic planning software, which is in the public FDA database
  • Flexible ways to buy: licensed software for labs, a clinician edition, outsourced design, or a full aligner service
  • Open file exports (STL/OBJ) and print-model preparation support in-office or outside fabrication, so you’re not tied to one manufacturer
  • Browser-based viewing and editing for doctors and patients, with no local install needed for review
  • A published per-case pricing model in the support FAQ (the company lists $50 per exported case for VISION, and $120 for a full outsourced design with three revisions)
  • An independent 2025 typodont study found that VISION’s automatic tooth segmentation was more accurate than one competitor’s and comparable to another’s

Cons

  • A small, privately held company. Funding, investors, and its full office footprint aren’t fully disclosed, and sources disagree on its founding year
  • The FDA clearance covers the VISION software. We found no SoftSmile-named clearance for SoftSmile Aligners, and the partner lab that makes them isn’t named
  • Many headline numbers (patents, doctors, patients, plans per month, speed multiples) and superlatives (“most powerful,” “most precise”) are company claims
  • The same 2025 study found that all three tested programs tended to oversize segmented teeth, with tooth widths often off by more than 0.1 mm. The authors noted that this could affect interproximal reduction planning. Planners should check segmentation and IPR rather than relying on automation
  • Built for aligners, not brackets. Clinician and lab editions aren’t clearly separated on public pages
  • FAQ pricing may not reflect current contracts, and aligner-service pricing isn’t published. Get quotes in writing
  • In-office aligner production means the practice takes on the printing, thermoforming, materials, and quality-control steps and their regulatory responsibilities. Cloud storage of patient scans means reviewing data-protection and business associate terms

How it fits in a practice

SoftSmile sits at the treatment-planning step of aligner therapy. It is not a practice-management system. A practice could use it in one of three ways:

  • Plan aligners in-house in VISION or Dr. Vision and print them in the office or send files to a fabricator.
  • Send scans to SoftSmile’s OrthoDesign team and approve the plan in the Viewer.
  • Order finished SoftSmile Aligners from the company.

Labs and aligner brands use it as their production planning tool. Scans come in through STL upload or Medit Link, and plans go out as STL/OBJ files for printing.

When evaluating it, ask:

  • Which edition fits your volume: Dr. Vision, VISION, or Vision LAB.
  • Current per-case pricing and what revisions and refinements include.
  • Which scanners, CBCT formats, and printers your workflow needs.
  • Who actually performs and signs off on setups.
  • Which partner manufactures SoftSmile Aligners, and under which FDA clearances.
  • How patient data is stored, how long it’s kept, and whether a business associate agreement is offered.
  • How you would export your cases if you stopped using the platform.

These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

Ultradent

South Jordan, Utah dental materials manufacturer known for Opalescence whitening, Ultra-Etch, Opal Seal/Opal Bond orthodontic adhesives, UltraSeal XT sealants, Peak Universal Bond, and VALO curing lights—bonding-adjacent supplies and whitening for specialty practices.

HQ: South Jordan, Utah, USA · Founded 1978 · Researched 2026-09-11 · Official site

Overview

Ultradent Products, Inc. is a U.S. dental materials and devices manufacturer headquartered at 505 West Ultradent Drive, South Jordan, Utah. Orthodontic offices usually meet Ultradent through whitening, etch/bond/sealant chairside materials, ortho-labeled adhesives, and LED curing lights—not through brackets, wires, or aligner systems.

Ultradent’s February 2018 newsroom anniversary piece places founding in 1978, when Dr. Dan Fischer patented and began marketing Astringedent® hemostatic and launched the company. The same campus address appears across live product footers. Public third-party directories sometimes note later corporate ownership changes; for Ortho Index purposes, treat South Jordan as the published dental-products headquarters and confirm remittance/legal entity language on any formal purchase order.

What Ultradent is—and is not—for an ortho buyer:

  • Is: a deep supplies lane for professional whitening (Opalescence™), phosphoric-acid etching (Ultra-Etch™), orthodontic primers/adhesives (Opal™ Seal, Opal™ Bond), pit-and-fissure sealants (UltraSeal XT™), general bonding (Peak™ Universal Bond), and curing lights (VALO™ family, including ortho-oriented cordless options listed under the Orthodontics category).
  • Is not: an appliance manufacturer. Brackets, archwires, expanders, and clear-aligner planning platforms come from other brands.
  • Portfolio note: Ultradent also sells restorative, tissue-management, hygiene, and equipment lines used in GP and specialty offices. This page focuses on the ortho-relevant subset.

Names below match live Ultradent category and product pages researched September 11, 2026. Concentrations, shades, tips, kit contents, warranties, and territory availability change—confirm against the current catalog, IFU, and SDS before standardizing a protocol. Who it fits

  • Orthodontic practices that offer in-office or take-home whitening (including aligner/retainer tray workflows) and want a single professional whitening family
  • Offices bonding metal or ceramic appliances that want ortho-specific etchant/primer/paste stacks (Ultra-Etch + Opal Seal + Opal Bond) rather than only general restorative adhesives
  • Clinics placing pit-and-fissure sealants alongside ortho care and already using Ultradent delivery tips/lights
  • Teams standardizing LED curing across bonding, sealants, and light-cured barriers, including VALO Ortho Cordless listed on the Orthodontics equipment page
  • Purchasing leads comparing IFUs, fluoride-release claims, viscosity options (flow vs medium), and tip systems before locking a bonding protocol
  • Dual ortho/restorative environments that already stock Peak Universal Bond, Ultra-Etch, or Opalescence and want continuity of brand and tip inventory

Notable products

Whitening (Opalescence™)

Ultradent’s whitening category is built around Opalescence™:

  • Opalescence Go™ / Go Advanced — prefilled take-home trays (UltraFit™ tray language on product pages); listed under both Whitening and Orthodontics category navigation for chairside/home dispensing
  • Opalescence Custom — take-home gel for custom trays in multiple carbamide peroxide concentrations (live whitening category lists 10%, 15%, 20%, 35%, and 45%, plus a Non-PF 10% option)
  • Opalescence Pro — in-office chemically activated hydrogen peroxide gel; Ultradent states lights are not required for activation and recommends waiting 7–10 days after whitening before bonding procedures
  • Opalescence Endo, Opalustre™ / OpalCups™ — non-vital and enamel microabrasion options on the whitening category (verify indications carefully; not routine bracket-bonding materials)
  • OpalDam™ / OpalDam™ Green, Umbrella™, IsoBlock™ — isolation accessories commonly used with in-office whitening
  • UltraEZ™ — desensitizing gel with potassium nitrate and fluoride, positioned for whitening-related sensitivity
  • Opalescence Whitening Toothpaste — home-care adjunct also listed under Orthodontics home care

Tray fabrication accessories (Sof-Tray™ sheets, LC Block-Out Resin, Chromaclone alginate, pocket tray cases) sit on the whitening category for offices that fabricate custom trays in-house.

Orthodontic adhesives and etch

  • Ultra-Etch™ — 35% phosphoric acid etchant with viscosity Ultradent positions for precise placement; total-etch only (not a self-etch substitute). Official FAQ timing: 15 seconds on cut enamel/dentin, 30 seconds on uncut enamel (explicitly including orthodontic brackets and unprepared sealants). Also listed under the Orthodontics etchants path.
  • Opal™ Seal — 38%-filled fluoride-releasing and recharging primer for etched enamel before bracket placement; fluorescent for reapplication/removal visibility; used with Metal Dento-Infusor™ tip language on the product page
  • Opal™ Bond MV — medium-viscosity light-cured orthodontic adhesive for metal and ceramic appliances; fluoresces for cleanup; contra-angle syringe delivery; manufacturer language emphasizes limited run-on and bracket drift control
  • Opal™ Bond Flow — flowable light-cured adhesive for metal and ceramic appliances; positioned for permanent retainers, bite ramps, occlusal buildups, and indirect bonding; A2 and Blue shades
  • Opal™ Etch — companion etchant SKU often cross-sold with Opal Bond kits (confirm concentration and IFU on the quote)

Bonding-adjacent restorative adhesives

  • Peak™ Universal Bond — light-cured adhesive with 0.2% chlorhexidine; marketed as compatible with total-etch and self-etch techniques and multiple substrates (enamel, dentin, porcelain, metal, composite, zirconia). Useful when an ortho office also does restorative repairs; not a drop-in replacement for Opal Bond unless your protocol and IFU say so.
  • Peak™ SE Primer — self-etch primer companion for Peak Universal Bond (JetMix syringe). Ultra-Etch FAQ explicitly points Peak SE + Peak Universal Bond for self-etch workflows instead of Ultra-Etch.

Sealants

  • UltraSeal XT™ plus — hydrophobic, light-cured, radiopaque, fluoride-releasing pit-and-fissure sealant; 58%-filled; used with PrimaDry™ drying agent for moisture control. Cure guidance on the product page references VALO™ Xtra Power ~3 seconds or Standard Power ~10 seconds. Common in mixed pediatric/ortho preventive workflows; verify sealant vs bracket-adhesive indications separately.

Curing lights and ortho chairside accessories

  • VALO™ X — current flagship LED curing light with ~12.5 mm lens, Standard/Xtra Power curing modes, white/black diagnostic-aid modes, corded or cordless use, and published multi-year warranty language
  • VALO™ Ortho Cordless — explicitly listed under Orthodontics → Equipment on the live Orthodontics category page (ortho-oriented cordless curing option alongside the broader VALO family)
  • Gishy Goo™ — silicone bracket relief aid (Orthodontics home care)
  • Opalpix™ — interproximal cleaner (Orthodontics home care)
  • Ultrapro™ Tx prophy angles, paste, and air products — oral hygiene / polishing lane listed under Orthodontics

Always match light output/mode and material IFU cure times; “compatible with any light” marketing still requires verifying intensity and exposure for your specific adhesive or sealant.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Ortho-labeled adhesive stack exists — Ultra-Etch, Opal Seal, and Opal Bond MV/Flow are published under Orthodontics adhesives, which simplifies protocol writing versus borrowing only GP restorative adhesives
  • Whitening depth — Opalescence covers prefilled trays, custom-tray gels, in-office gel, isolation accessories, desensitizer, and toothpaste under one brand family
  • Tip and delivery ecosystem — Blue Micro™, Inspiral™, Metal Dento-Infusor™, and related tips recur across etch, sealant, and primer pages, which helps inventory standardization
  • Curing-light continuity — VALO X plus VALO Ortho Cordless keep light-cured bonding, sealants, and barriers in one equipment conversation
  • Strong public documentation — IFU/SDS links are prominent on product pages; etch timing and whitening-before-bonding guidance are stated in plain language
  • U.S. manufacturing identity — company communications historically emphasize South Jordan vertical integration; useful context for supply-chain conversations (confirm current manufacturing claims on the formal packet)

Cons / watch-outs

  • Not an appliance company — you still need separate bracket, wire, aligner, and imaging vendors
  • Broad catalog = SKU discipline required — Peak vs Opal Bond, Ultra-Etch vs Opal Etch, Go vs Custom vs Pro, and multiple VALO generations can mix protocols if purchasing is loose
  • Whitening timing affects bonding — Opalescence Pro materials note waiting 7–10 days after whitening before bonding; schedule finishing/whitening sequences deliberately
  • Marketing language is manufacturer positioning — fluoride recharge, bond-strength comparisons, “#1 professional whitening,” and cure-completeness claims should be read against IFUs and your own criteria, not as guaranteed clinical results
  • Channel and kit contents vary — direct web pricing, distributor formularies, and tip inclusions differ; login/formulary views can hide true landed cost
  • Equipment cost and training — VALO purchases and tip-system habits are capital/process decisions, not just material swaps

Evaluation notes

  • Separate the whitening lane (patient-facing Opalescence SKUs + isolation) from the bonding lane (etch/primer/paste + light) on any RFP so each can be compared to specialty competitors (e.g., Reliance for adhesives-only).
  • If adopting Opal Seal + Opal Bond, write a single chairside card: etch time on uncut enamel, primer thickness, paste viscosity (MV vs Flow), flash cleanup under fluorescence/black light, and cure mode/time with your specific VALO.
  • For sealants, confirm whether UltraSeal XT plus is in-scope for your pediatric/ortho preventive protocol and whether PrimaDry is stocked with it.
  • Ask whether Peak Universal Bond belongs in the ortho kit at all, or only in a restorative drawer, to avoid accidental protocol mixing.
  • For whitening-with-aligners/retainers, verify tray material compatibility, wear schedule, and sensitivity pathway (UltraEZ) before promising timelines to patients.
  • Confirm current corporate entity, warranty, and authorized-distributor terms on the quote—especially if public ownership headlines have changed since founding-era family-owned language.

Bottom line

Ultradent is a strong supplies shortlist for orthodontic offices that want professional whitening (Opalescence) and bonding-adjacent materials (Ultra-Etch, Opal Seal, Opal Bond, UltraSeal XT) plus a VALO curing-light path under one South Jordan manufacturer. Use it when whitening and chairside resin chemistry matter as much as appliance brands do.

It is not the brand to expect for brackets, wires, or aligner systems. Keep appliance comparisons elsewhere, and lock etch/bond/whitening protocols to current IFUs rather than catalog headlines.

Ortho-Tain (HealthyStart)

Prefabricated, removable early-treatment and functional appliances from Ortho-Tain, Inc., a manufacturer based in Toa Alta, Puerto Rico, with an Illinois (Winnetka) business address. Lines include the Nite-Guide, Occlus-o-Guide, Ortho-T, Habit Corrector and Class III appliances, and the HealthyStart system it markets for children's sleep and breathing. Most appliances are FDA-listed as Class I, 510(k)-exempt devices; Nite-Guide (K931615, 1995) and SnoreCure anti-snoring devices (K980952, 1998; K263697, 2026) have 510(k)s.

HQ: Toa Alta, Puerto Rico, USA · Researched 2026-11-27 · Official site

Overview

Ortho-Tain makes prefabricated, removable intraoral appliances. They come in stock sizes rather than being custom-made from impressions or scans, and they are worn at night and for a few hours during the day. In the research literature this type of appliance is usually called an eruption guidance appliance (EGA) or preformed activator: a soft, one-piece appliance meant to guide erupting teeth and the bite in growing children. Ortho-Tain’s Nite-Guide and Occlus-o-Guide are among the appliances studied in that research. The company also sells appliances for older children, teens and adults, and markets a newer HealthyStart system around children’s sleep and breathing.

Company. FDA registration data, the 510(k) records and the HealthyStart terms and conditions all name the company as Ortho-Tain, Inc. A Puerto Rico government release refers to it as Ortho-Tain Enterprises; we did not confirm whether that is a separate legal entity or another name for the same business. We found no public information indicating an outside parent company, and none is assumed here.

Where it is based. The company’s FDA-registered manufacturing site is in Toa Alta, Puerto Rico, and the Puerto Rico Department of Economic Development and Commerce describes it as a local Toa Alta manufacturer that was expanding its operations there. The company also has a long connection to Illinois: its origins are there, and the current HealthyStart terms (dated July 2026) give a Winnetka, Illinois suite as the address for product returns. Ortho Index lists Toa Alta as the base because that is where the registered manufacturing site is; the Illinois address appears to serve as an office.

Founding. Sources disagree. The HealthyStart site says Ortho-Tain was created in 1967 and the Ortho-Tain site says its first appliance was invented that year, while the Puerto Rico government release says the company was founded in 1976. Ortho Index does not give a founding year.

HealthyStart and Bbite. The Ortho-Tain home page describes HealthyStart and Bbite as subsidiary companies, while the HealthyStart terms describe HealthyStart as a division of Ortho-Tain, Inc. Bbite is presented as a children’s sleep and habit program with its own UK provider sign-up. Ortho Index treats all three as one brand family on this page.

Not the same as Myobrace. Myobrace is a separate range from Myofunctional Research Co. of Australia. Both companies sell prefabricated appliances for early treatment and both market sleep and breathing benefits, and reviews of prefabricated appliances often group them together, but they are different companies with different product designs.

Regulatory status.

  • Listed as Class I devices. In FDA’s registration and listing data, Ortho-Tain, Inc. lists the Ortho-Tain, Ortho-Tain Preformed Positioner, Occlus-o-Guide, Occlus-o-Guide Preformed Activator and Nite-Guide Preformed Activator under product code ECM, “Band, Preformed, Orthodontic,” and the Habit-Corrector, Class III Guide, Snore-Cure and other items under product code DYO. Both codes fall under 21 CFR 872.5410 (orthodontic appliance and accessories, Class I), which FDA’s classification data marks as exempt from 510(k) premarket notification, subject to the general limits in 21 CFR 872.9. Listing is not FDA approval or a finding of effectiveness.
  • Nite-Guide 510(k). FDA found Nite-Guide (K931615) substantially equivalent on April 14, 1995, under product code KMY, preformed tooth positioner (21 CFR 872.5525, Class I). The submission was received in 1993. That device type is now 510(k)-exempt.
  • Anti-snoring devices. Snore-Cure (K980952, June 1, 1998) and SnoreCure 0, 1 and 2 (K263697, November 13, 2026) were found substantially equivalent as anti-snoring devices (product code LRK, Class II, 21 CFR 872.5570). We did not confirm their cleared indications, patient ages or how they relate to the HealthyStart appliances, and SnoreCure is not described on the Ortho-Tain website.
  • What the company says. The Ortho-Tain site says its appliances are “cleared by the FDA” to correct a range of problems including TMJ disorders. We did not find a 510(k) clearance for those uses; the records we found are the ones listed above. Ask the company which clearance or listing covers each appliance and use.
  • Other markets. We did not verify CE marking, UK registration or other listings, including for the Bbite program.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists, pediatric dentists and general dentists who offer early or interceptive treatment in the primary and mixed dentition and want a low-cost, prefabricated option.
  • Practices interested in eruption guidance for children with increased overjet, deep bite or mild crowding, which is where most of the independent research sits.
  • Practices that want a removable option for older children, teens or adults. The company markets the Ortho-T for ages 12 and up, but most independent studies are in children.
  • Dentists interested in children’s sleep and breathing screening. HealthyStart and Bbite are marketed for this, with training and provider programs, but the independent evidence is thin (see below).

The company recruits dentists, pediatric dentists and orthodontists as HealthyStart providers and offers training and treatment-plan review. Its consumer pages direct parents to providers, so the appliances are sold through clinicians.

Notable products

Product names and descriptions below are as the company states them.

  • Nite-Guide: a clear appliance for ages 5 to 7, worn only while sleeping, which the company describes as preventive treatment that guides teeth as they erupt.
  • Occlus-o-Guide: a clear, soft, removable appliance for ages 8 to 12 (mixed dentition), worn actively for two to four hours during the day and at night. The company says it can straighten teeth in 4 to 10 months.
  • Ortho-T: an appliance for ages 12 to adult, which the company calls an all-in-one orthopedic, functional and finishing appliance.
  • Habit Corrector: a pediatric appliance the company describes as an orthopedic and airway health appliance, with sleep-disordered breathing as its primary focus.
  • Class III appliance: designed, the company says, to correct a pseudo Class III and keep a skeletal Class III from worsening. It comes in two sizes depending on whether the first permanent molars are present.
  • HealthyStart system: a series of appliances chosen by age and by the problems being addressed, marketed for symptoms of sleep-disordered breathing.

Pros / cons

Pros

  • Low cost and quick to start. Stock sizes need no lab work, and the company’s long history means the appliances have been used and studied for decades.
  • Reasonable independent evidence of short-term benefit for overjet and overbite. A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis of prefabricated EGAs, including Nite-Guide and Occlus-o-Guide, found reductions in overjet of about 1 to 2 mm and in overbite of about 0.6 to 2 mm in mixed dentition, with some improvement in crowding and molar relationship. A 2022 meta-analysis reached a similar short-term conclusion.
  • A 2015 randomized trial of 7- to 8-year-olds found one year of EGA treatment reduced overjet, overbite and lower anterior crowding, and the share of children with a Class II molar relationship fell sharply, while untreated controls did not improve.
  • A 2008 controlled study of children treated from about age 5 found the appliance mostly changed the lower teeth and jaw, with greater mandibular growth than in untreated controls.
  • A 2016 retrospective comparison in adolescents found results, breakages and emergency visits broadly similar to Twin Block and activator-headgear treatment, with no statistically significant differences.

Cons

  • Most of the change is dental, and skeletal effects are uncertain. The 2026 review says more studies are needed on skeletal effects, and both meta-analyses included only a handful of studies.
  • Relapse is documented. The 2022 meta-analysis found overbite increased again after retention, and a 2007 long-term study found overjet and molar relationship stayed stable but overbite and lower crowding relapsed.
  • Results depend on wear. The appliances are removable and part-time, and the 2016 comparison found non-compliance of about 32% with the EGA, numerically higher than with the other appliances.
  • A 2023 study found a customised, 3D-printed EGA corrected crowding and incisor position significantly better than a preformed one, which suggests stock sizes have limits.
  • Like other functional appliances, EGA treatment was linked to more root resorption than in untreated children in a 2007 study, though the amounts were mostly small and similar to the Fränkel appliance.
  • The company’s claims go well beyond the evidence. Its sites say Ortho-Tain straightens teeth “without braces” in months, “naturally resists relapse,” removes the need for fixed treatment later and treats TMJ disorders. HealthyStart is marketed as opening the airway and addressing children’s sleep and breathing problems. Treat all of these as company claims.
  • Sleep and airway evidence is especially weak. The main published study of the company’s preformed sleep and habit appliances was led by the company’s founder, used parent questionnaires and had no control group. We found no independent controlled trials of HealthyStart for sleep-disordered breathing.
  • The main US status for the orthodontic appliances is a 510(k)-exempt Class I listing, not a clearance for the outcomes being marketed.

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

Ortho-Tain usually sits in the early-treatment part of a practice, as a low-cost first phase for growing children with increased overjet, deep bite or mild crowding, sometimes followed later by braces or aligners. Because the appliances are prefabricated, the practice’s work is case selection, sizing, coaching and monitoring wear, rather than lab fabrication. HealthyStart adds a sleep and breathing screening layer that some practices may prefer to coordinate with a child’s physician or sleep specialist.

When evaluating it, ask:

  • Which cases the independent evidence supports, mainly overjet and overbite in the mixed dentition, and where a custom functional appliance or comprehensive treatment would be expected to do better.
  • How you will measure and encourage wear, and what retention plan you will use, given the documented overbite and crowding relapse.
  • How you will describe expected results to families without repeating marketing claims about avoiding braces, permanent results, TMJ or breathing.
  • Which FDA listing or clearance covers each appliance you plan to use, especially for any sleep-related use, and the status in your country if you are outside the US.
  • Provider program costs, training requirements, appliance pricing and ordering.

These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

Lancer Orthodontics (Lancer Global)

Long-running orthodontic manufacturer founded in 1967 that makes MIM and milled-slot metal brackets, self-ligating and ceramic brackets, buccal tubes, bands, wires, TADs, expanders and accessories. Since 2021 it has been marketed through Lancer Global Dental Partner, a venture with the Italian implant company Kristal.

HQ: Carlsbad, California, USA and Trezzano sul Naviglio (Milan), Italy, per Lancer Global contact pages; manufacturing site in Mexicali, Mexico · Founded 1967 · Researched 2026-11-09 · Official site

Overview

Lancer Orthodontics is a U.S.-origin orthodontic manufacturer founded in 1967. It makes a broad catalog of fixed-appliance products: metal brackets, self-ligating and ceramic brackets, buccal tubes, bands and attachments, archwires and wire products, elastomerics, adhesives, temporary anchorage devices (TADs), palatal expanders, extraoral appliances and instruments. It sells to orthodontists, dentists and distributors and also does private-label work.

Ownership and status: Today Lancer is marketed under Lancer Global Dental Partner (Lancer Global). The company’s About page describes it as born from “the union” of Lancer Orthodontics and Kristal, an Italian maker and distributor of dental implants, including PHI implants. Lancer Global’s LinkedIn profile dates the venture to 2021. The official site does not publish the equity split or say which entity controls the other, so this page describes it as a combined venture rather than an acquisition. Lancer Global lists offices in Trezzano sul Naviglio (Milan), Italy, Carlsbad, California, and Mexicali, Baja California, Mexico. It sells Lancer orthodontic lines alongside Kristal implant lines. Lancer is still operating: its 2025 catalog (Volume 58) and product pages were live as of this page’s research date, and we found no announcement that it had closed or been folded into a brand already covered on Ortho Index.

Corporate history (public filings and press): Lancer was based in Carlsbad, then San Marcos and later Vista, California. The diagnostics company Biomerica, Inc. bought about 36% of Lancer in 1988 and for years held a controlling stake. Biomerica stopped consolidating Lancer on December 1, 2005, when its holding fell below 20%. It sold its remaining interest in a private transaction in May 2008, and the buyer was not disclosed. Lancer was a public company (OTC ticker LANZ). It filed SEC Form 15 to deregister in January 2005 and has filed no SEC reports since, so there are no current audited public financials. Third-party quote sites still show an LANZ quote. Biomerica filings also describe a Lancer manufacturing facility in Mexicali.

Mexico trade-program action (2025): In June 2025, Mexican press including El Economista and T21 reported that Mexico’s Secretaría de Economía had cancelled and suspended the IMMEX export-manufacturing program registrations of “Lancer Ortodontics” and a textile company, citing irregular import-export operations. The reports did not name the Mexican legal entity, its address or any product line. We found no company statement and no public record of how the matter was resolved. It is noted here as reported, not as a finding about product quality or U.S. supply.

A separate Spanish-language site, lancerorthodontics.com, describes Lancer Orthodontics de México as a union of Lancer and Avanti Medical Technologies, a Mexican medical-device company, based at the same Mexicali address. Lancer Global’s site does not mention that arrangement. Parts of the Mexican site look unfinished, and how it relates to Lancer Global is unconfirmed. The older lancerortho.com domain now shows a parked page, so it is not linked here.

FDA status (openFDA): openFDA lists many historic 510(k) clearances with Lancer Orthodontics, Inc. as applicant. Examples include bracket and buccal-tube families (1990), Paragon (1999), O.A.S.I. System (2005), BluGoo HD (2007) and the Storm Mini Screw orthodontic anchorage implant (K122069, cleared April 11, 2013, Vista, CA). Many routine bracket, band and wire products fall under FDA classifications that may not need a new 510(k) for each item. Using the name searches we tried, we did not find a current openFDA establishment registration under a Lancer or Lancer Global name. That may reflect a different registered entity name or importer, but we could not confirm it. U.S. practices should ask their supplier who the current FDA-registered manufacturer and importer are. Lancer Global publishes MDSAP, CE and ISO 13485:2016 certificates for Lancer, plus EU declarations of conformity, on its documentation page.

Who it’s for

Orthodontists and general dentists who use conventional fixed appliances and want a broad, single-catalog supplier. Lancer covers twin and mini-twin brackets in several prescriptions (MBT, Roth, Ricketts, Hilgers, standard edgewise, Tweed), self-ligating brackets, ceramics, tubes, bands, wires, TADs and expanders. Lancer positions itself on “quality products at competitive prices,” so it is aimed at price-conscious practices and distributors. Its Redi-Pak prescription kits are designed for offices that bond from pre-sorted, per-patient packs.

It is a weaker fit if you want a custom or digitally designed bracket system, an aligner platform, or a vendor with well-documented U.S. direct sales and service. Lancer Global’s site is professional-only, is operated from Italy and is oriented toward an international distributor network. It does not publish U.S. pricing or an online store.

Notable products

Names below come from Lancer Global’s orthodontics page and the 2025 catalog (Volume 58). Product lines, prescriptions and availability change. Confirm current part numbers and regulatory labeling for your market with your supplier.

  • Praxis™ Pro and Praxis™: metal twin brackets. The catalog says some new Praxis Pro items were “available upon request from 2025,” with a stated manufacturing lead time of about 4 to 6 weeks.
  • Velocity™ Mini-Twin: a mini-twin metal bracket the catalog describes as milled from a single piece of metal.
  • Sinterline™ Plus and Sinterline™ V-Slot: long-running MIM bracket lines. V-Slot adds a vertical slot.
  • Tweed brackets and the Straight Forward Philosophy® (SFP) treatment system, a kit that pairs a prescription with a custom arch-form series.
  • Velocity™ Passive SLB, Velocity™ Active SLB and Velocity™ Active SLB Ceramic: self-ligating brackets.
  • Optyc™ Ceramic and Velocity Whisper™: aesthetic brackets.
  • Buccal tubes: weldable, bondable, convertible and non-convertible options.
  • Bands and attachments: anatomical molar and bicuspid bands. Lancer says it welds attachments free of charge.
  • Wires and wire products: archwires the company says are made in the U.S. with American materials, plus multistrand, retainer, spring and ligature wire.
  • Storm Mini Screw (TADs) and rapid palatal expanders. The Storm Mini Screw has a 2013 FDA 510(k), K122069.
  • Kristal Clear: thermoplastic discs for aligners, retainers and other vacuum-formed appliances.
  • Elastomerics, adhesives, extraoral appliances (facebows, facemasks, headcaps, neckpads) and instruments.
  • Redi-Pak™: a resealable prescription kit that holds a patient’s brackets in “tweezer-ready” order.

On manufacturing, the catalog explains that Lancer uses robotic metal injection molding (MIM) for its brackets and precision-milled archwire slots on selected lines. It cites tolerances “as small as 0.0005 inches.” That is a vendor specification, not an independently tested figure.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Long track record: the brand dates to 1967, and the company has a long list of public FDA 510(k) records.
  • Broad catalog covering brackets, self-ligating and ceramic brackets, tubes, bands, wires, TADs, expanders and accessories, so you can consolidate suppliers.
  • Several prescriptions and systems, including Tweed and SFP, for clinicians with established mechanics.
  • Lancer Global publishes its quality-system certificates (MDSAP, ISO 13485:2016, CE) and declarations of conformity.
  • Positioned on value pricing, and the company says it welds band attachments free of charge.

Cons

  • Ownership and control are not fully transparent. The site describes a “union” with Kristal (2021), but who owns or controls Lancer is not published. Lancer stopped SEC reporting in 2005, and Biomerica’s 2008 sale of its stake went to an undisclosed buyer.
  • Mexican authorities reportedly cancelled and suspended an IMMEX program registration in “Lancer Ortodontics’” name in June 2025. We found no public statement on the outcome or on any effect on manufacturing or supply.
  • We did not find a current openFDA establishment registration under a Lancer name. U.S. buyers should confirm the registered manufacturer and importer.
  • The corporate web presence is split. Lancer Global’s site is Italy-run and also sells implants, a separate Mexican site exists with an unclear relationship, and the older U.S. domain is parked.
  • No published U.S. pricing or online ordering, and the catalog warns of lead times of about 4 to 6 weeks on some newer items.
  • Marketing language such as “global leader,” “unmatched quality” and “predictable results” is vendor copy, not independently verified.

How it fits in a practice

Lancer is a catalog supplier for conventional fixed orthodontics. It is an alternative or a second source to larger bracket-and-wire suppliers for practices that want lower unit costs and a single vendor for brackets, tubes, bands, wires, elastomerics and anchorage. A typical path is to request the current catalog and a sample of your usual prescription, bond a few cases to judge fit, base retention and slot finish, and then set up ordering through Lancer Global or a regional distributor.

Before switching or adding Lancer, practices typically confirm which legal entity invoices and ships to them, the current FDA-registered manufacturer and importer for U.S. orders, lead times and back-order history, return and warranty terms, and whether their preferred prescriptions and slot sizes (.018 or .022) are in stock. Given the 2025 IMMEX report and the limited public financial disclosure, it is reasonable to ask about supply continuity and where specific product lines are made.

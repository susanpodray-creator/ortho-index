Carestream Dental

Dental imaging hardware and connected software spanning CS 9600 and CS 8200 3D CBCT, CS 8100 Evo panoramic/cephalometric imaging, RVG sensors, intraoral X-ray systems, and CS Imaging 8—with Sensei handling the company’s practice-management portfolio.

HQ: Atlanta, Georgia, USA · Researched 2026-09-03 · Official site

Overview

Carestream Dental is an oral-health technology company centered on dental imaging hardware, imaging software, clinical software, and practice-management systems. For an orthodontic office, the most relevant current equipment runs from panoramic and optional cephalometric imaging through CBCT, intraoral radiography, cameras, and the software used to review and organize those records.

The live U.S. site is carestreamdental.com/en-us. Carestream Dental LLC’s published U.S. terms list an office at 3625 Cumberland Boulevard, Suite 700, Atlanta, Georgia 30339, and the company announced that address as its global headquarters in 2018. This page therefore uses Atlanta, Georgia, USA for hq.

The company history needs careful wording:

  • Carestream Dental traces its imaging heritage to Kodak dental products and routinely describes the business as built on more than 125 years of experience.
  • The current standalone company was created when the dental digital business of Carestream Health was acquired by funds managed by Clayton, Dubilier & Rice and Hillhouse/CareCapital; Carestream Dental announced completion on September 1, 2017.
  • Those are different dates: 1896 is a heritage date tied to Kodak dental X-ray film, while 2017 is the independent-company carve-out. This page omits founded rather than presenting either date as an uncontested corporate founding year.
  • Carestream Dental is not Carestream Health’s dental division today. It has operated as a separate company since the 2017 transaction.

How the current brand portfolio stacks:

  • Carestream Dental — oral-health devices and core imaging software: CBCT, panoramic/cephalometric systems, sensors, imaging plates, intraoral X-ray generators, cameras, CS Imaging, and CS 3D Imaging.
  • Swissmeda — Carestream Dental’s clinical-software brand, including cloud imaging, case collaboration, and planning applications. It is a sibling portfolio, not a CBCT model.
  • Sensei — Carestream Dental’s practice-management brand. Sensei Cloud Ortho and the server-based OrthoTrac product sit here. They connect to imaging workflows, but they are not imaging units.

One important boundary: Carestream Dental no longer owns the CS intraoral-scanner business. Envista completed its acquisition on April 20, 2022, said the acquired business would be rebranded as DEXIS, and explicitly excluded Carestream Dental’s imaging-equipment and practice-management businesses. That means the old CS 3600, CS 3700, and CS 3800 scanner family should not be listed as current Carestream Dental scanner hardware. Current Carestream CBCT pages instead describe open connectivity to leading third-party intraoral scanners.

This page covers the current Carestream Dental imaging ecosystem from an orthodontic-office perspective. It is informational, not a selection protocol or a substitute for current manuals, local radiation rules, service terms, or professional judgment.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontic offices comparing a compact panoramic/cephalometric platform such as the CS 8100 family with a larger 2D/3D CBCT platform
  • Specialists who need selectable fields of view, optional cephalometric acquisition, and a software path for 2D, 3D, airway, and model data
  • Practices that want one vendor for extraoral imaging, intraoral sensors or plates, intraoral X-ray generation, cameras, and image-management software
  • Existing Carestream offices standardizing records in CS Imaging 8 while retaining the ability to import/export common formats and connect selected third-party systems
  • Ortho groups using OrthoTrac or Sensei Cloud Ortho that need to separate practice-management decisions from imaging-hardware decisions
  • Buyers prepared to evaluate the full implementation: room dimensions, shielding and registration, workstation/server requirements, software modules, service coverage, training, migration, and future upgrade licensing—not just the scanner cabinet

Notable products

Names below match live U.S. manufacturer pages researched September 3, 2026. Availability, clearance, bundles, field-of-view licenses, cephalometric arms, workstation requirements, and software modules vary by country and configuration. Performance superlatives and workflow claims are manufacturer positioning unless otherwise noted.

Extraoral 3D / CBCT

  • CS 9600 — Carestream Dental’s broadest current CBCT family. The official page calls it a multifunctional 5-in-1 platform with panoramic and CBCT imaging plus configuration-dependent cephalometric imaging, 3D object scanning, and facial scanning. It lists up to 14 fields of view, from 4 × 4 cm to 16 × 17 cm, optional 120 kV, camera/AI-assisted positioning, optional retractable seat, CS MAR metal-artifact reduction, and resolution down to 75 microns. For orthodontics, the large-volume and optional cephalometric configurations are the relevant lane; not every CS 9600 quote includes every modality.
  • CS 8200 3D Advance Edition — compact 2D/3D family with up to 11 fields of view and a maximum listed volume of 16 × 10 cm. Carestream describes three upgradeable FOV configurations, optional cephalometric imaging, model scanning, CS MAR, and integration with CS Imaging / CS 3D Imaging. It spans a wider range than the Access edition but does not reach the CS 9600’s 16 × 17 cm maximum.
  • CS 8200 3D Access — entry configuration in the current CBCT comparison. Official copy describes a 4-in-1 platform: panoramic, CBCT, 3D model scanning, and optional cephalometric imaging. It lists up to six fields of view, from 4 × 4 cm to 10 × 10 cm, and an open workflow linking CS Imaging with leading intraoral scanners.

The family comparison is useful because “CS 8200 3D” by itself is not a complete configuration. Confirm Access vs Advance, maximum FOV license, ceph arm, model-scan capability, software package, and upgrade rights on the actual proposal.

Panoramic / cephalometric

  • CS 8100 Evo Edition — compact digital panoramic system. The live page lists full and segmented panoramic, maxillary sinus, TMJ, and 2D+ programs, with an optional cephalometric arm that can be added later. It is positioned for general and orthodontic offices that want 2D extraoral imaging without buying CBCT.

Older regional and support pages may show names such as CS 8100SC 3D or other CS 8100-family variants. The current U.S. discovery page led with CS 8100 Evo Edition during this research. Confirm the exact current U.S. model and whether “SC,” “3D,” or “Evo” appears on the quote rather than treating those suffixes as interchangeable.

Intraoral radiography and cameras

  • RVG 6200 — current top-listed Carestream Dental wired sensor in sizes 1 and 2; the comparison page lists up to 24 lp/mm true resolution, USB connectivity, waterproof/shock-resistant construction, TWAIN compatibility, and optional CS Adapt image customization.
  • RVG 5200 — simpler size 1 and 2 wired sensor for routine imaging; USB and TWAIN are listed on the family comparison.
  • CS 6200 Size 0 — smaller intraoral sensor for smaller mouths. Do not confuse this sensor with the RVG 6200 size 1/2 family merely because both contain “6200.”
  • CS 7200 Neo Edition — compact imaging-plate scanner for common intraoral plate sizes, positioned for chairside or shared use.
  • CS 7600 — network imaging-plate system that accepts the wider plate range and uses Scan & Go identification/routing for shared multi-operatory workflows.
  • CS 2200 / CS 2100 — wall-mounted/room-mounted high-frequency intraoral X-ray generator families. The current U.S. category page also displays the CS 2500P handheld generator, but treat it as a quote-level item: confirm the exact U.S. SKU and current orderability, plus all applicable state and local rules for handheld operation, registration, training, storage/security, and shielding before purchase or use.
  • CS 1300 — current featured USB intraoral camera with HD output, rotating head, adjustable lighting, and a stated 10–100 mm focus range; integrates with CS Imaging and supports TWAIN.
  • CS 1500 — a U.S. product page remains live and describes the camera family, while the current discovery flow features the newer CS 1300. Page visibility does not establish current new-sale availability, configuration, or lifecycle status; confirm the exact quoted model and support terms instead of assuming CS 1500 and CS 1300 are equally current.

Imaging and orthodontic software

  • CS Imaging version 8 — the core desktop imaging hub for 2D images, 3D images, and CAD/CAM data. Carestream lists JPEG, DICOM, and STL import/export, integration with most practice-management software, Darkroom viewing, and links to CS 3D Imaging. It requires regular updates; software maintenance and version compatibility belong in the ownership budget.
  • CS 3D Imaging — 3D review and planning environment paired with Carestream CBCT. The current CBCT pages also show CS 3D Imaging Premium, CS Airway Module, and a Prosthetic-Driven Implant Planning Module as additional software lanes. Do not assume all modules ship with the base acquisition software.
  • Orthodontic Imaging Software / CS Orthodontic Imaging (CSOI) — a legacy-facing public page remains live and describes orthodontic imaging/presentation tools, OrthoTrac integration, and add-on modules. The page also references transferred or older hardware such as CS 3600 and CS 8100SC 3D; it does not establish a current version, new-install availability, or support lifecycle. Confirm all three—and the migration path to current CS Imaging/Sensei workflows—before treating CSOI as a new deployment option.
  • Swissmeda — clinical-software portfolio that currently includes cloud imaging, Imaging & Case Collaboration, and planning applications. It complements the imaging stack but is not automatically included with a CBCT purchase.
  • OrthoTrac — server-based, on-premise orthodontic practice-management software now presented as a Sensei product. Sensei’s current support page says existing OrthoTrac offices can continue using the software, with no immediate requirement to move, while also explaining a Sensei Cloud migration option.
  • Sensei Cloud Ortho — cloud practice-management lane for orthodontics, including scheduling, orthodontic contract billing, charting, aligner tracking, communication, and integrations. It is a Carestream Dental-owned sibling product, not the replacement name for CS Imaging or a piece of imaging hardware.

Not current Carestream Dental products

  • CS 3600, CS 3700, and CS 3800 intraoral scanners — sold to Envista and rebranded under DEXIS after April 2022. Old brochures, installed units, and software bridges can keep the CS names visible, but a new scanner comparison should use DEXIS’s current lineup and ownership.
  • Carestream Health medical imaging products — separate company and portfolio.
  • Kodak-branded legacy dental equipment — historical lineage does not make every old Kodak dental product a current Carestream Dental SKU.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Broad imaging ladder: 2D panoramic/ceph, compact-to-large-volume CBCT, sensors, plates, generators, cameras, and image software can be sourced as one connected imaging program.
  • Orthodontic configurations are explicit: optional cephalometric arms appear on CS 8100, CS 8200 3D, and CS 9600 materials; the CS 9600 reaches a listed 16 × 17 cm maximum FOV.
  • Upgradeable segmentation: the CS 8100 can add ceph, while the CS 8200 Advance family advertises upgradeable FOV configurations. That can reduce an immediate overbuy if contract pricing and future-license costs are clear.
  • Open-data language: CS Imaging lists JPEG, DICOM, and STL support, and the current CBCT family advertises connectivity with leading third-party intraoral scanners.
  • Existing install base and support library: the manufacturer maintains product pages, training, manuals, service resources, software updates, and installed-product support rather than offering hardware alone.
  • Clearer post-2022 boundary: Carestream retained CBCT, other imaging equipment, and practice management while the intraoral scanner business moved to DEXIS. Offices can evaluate the retained stack without pretending the old CS scanner family is still native hardware.

Cons / watch-outs

  • Names and configurations are easy to blur. CS 8200 Access vs Advance, CS 8100 suffixes, optional ceph, licensed FOVs, Face Scan, MAR, 120 kV, seat, and software modules materially change what is being purchased.
  • The ecosystem is split across three portfolio names. Carestream Dental, Swissmeda, and Sensei are related but have different product roles, web properties, subscriptions, and support paths.
  • No current Carestream-branded intraoral scanner. A fully digital ortho workflow now pairs the Carestream imaging stack with a third-party scanner; old CS 3600/3700/3800 familiarity should not drive a current purchase assumption.
  • Software is part of the capital decision. CS Imaging, CS 3D Imaging, Premium, Airway, cloud services, OrthoTrac, and Sensei are not one universal license. Ask for a module-by-module entitlement and renewal schedule.
  • Public lifecycle signals are uneven. Some older products retain live pages even when newer units are featured. A live web page is evidence of support/visibility, not proof that every configuration is available for a new U.S. order.
  • Vendor claims need context. “First-time-right,” low-dose percentages, AI automation, image-quality superlatives, practice-growth language, and user testimonials come from Carestream marketing or cited manufacturer studies. They are not universal performance or outcome guarantees.
  • Installation is more than footprint. CBCT/pan/ceph projects can involve shielding design, electrical/network readiness, local registration, acceptance testing, training, service access, and workstation/server planning. Published dimensions alone are not a site plan.
  • Migration risk matters for established offices. Image databases and server-based OrthoTrac environments need tested backups, version compatibility, bridge validation, and a written migration/rollback plan before hardware or cloud changes.

Evaluation notes

  • Define the required record set first: panoramic only, pan + ceph, limited-FOV CBCT, large-FOV CBCT, airway/model/facial imaging, or some combination.
  • Compare the actual quoted configuration—not just the family name. Record maximum FOV, ceph arm, sensor, seat, positioning aids, software modules, workstation/server, training, warranty, uptime/service terms, and upgrade pricing.
  • Verify how the system exports DICOM, JPEG, and STL, and test a real handoff to the office’s orthodontic planning, referral, and archive tools before go-live.
  • If pairing with an intraoral scanner, validate that exact scanner and software version. “Open platform” does not guarantee every workflow, metadata field, or automated launch is identical.
  • For an OrthoTrac office, separate the imaging database/integration plan from the practice-management migration plan. Sensei’s support page says OrthoTrac remains supported; moving to Sensei Cloud is a separate choice.
  • Ask which pages in the proposal are manufacturer claims, which features are included, and which require an option, maintenance plan, or later paid license.
  • Confirm current U.S. availability and regulatory labeling from the formal quote and manuals. Country pages are not interchangeable.

Bottom line

Carestream Dental remains a substantial dental imaging ecosystem, especially for an orthodontic office comparing compact pan/ceph with scalable CBCT. The practical shortlist is CS 8100 Evo Edition for 2D panoramic/optional ceph, CS 8200 3D Access or Advance for compact 3D, and CS 9600 when larger volumes and broader modality options matter. RVG/CS sensors, plate scanners, generators, cameras, and CS Imaging 8 fill out the records workflow.

The two purchasing traps are naming and ownership. Get every CS 8200/9600 option and software license in writing, and do not count the old CS 3600/3700/3800 intraoral scanners as current Carestream Dental products—they moved to Envista/DEXIS in 2022. Sensei and Swissmeda are Carestream Dental sibling portfolios, but practice management and clinical cloud software should be evaluated as their own subscriptions and migration projects.

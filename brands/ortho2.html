Ortho2

Orthodontic practice management software from Ortho2, LLC of Ames, Iowa. Current marketing centers on Edge Cloud, a cloud-hosted system for scheduling, billing, imaging, and patient communication; ViewPoint is the older Windows-based line.

HQ: Ames, Iowa, USA · Founded 1982 · Researched 2026-11-11 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

Ortho2 makes orthodontic practice management software. Its public website and privacy policy name the current operator Ortho2, LLC, at 1107 Buckeye Avenue (also written Buckeye Ave), Ames, IA 50010. Site footers in 2026 repeat that Ames street address. The privacy statement, last updated March 30, 2026, is titled in part “About Ortho2, LLC” and uses the same address. The company history page — and that same privacy section — say Ortho2 was founded in 1982. Early products included an Apple III system and, in 1985, a PC-based system called OneTouch.

Ownership: On April 30, 2018, Henry Schein, Inc. and Ortho Computer Systems, Inc. (Ortho2) announced a definitive agreement under which Henry Schein would own a 50% interest in Ortho2. The release was datelined Melville, New York, and Ames, Iowa. It described Edge Cloud and a Windows-based ViewPoint line, and it did not disclose financial terms. Ortho2’s current company-history and OSO pages still call this a partnership with Henry Schein and do not describe a later sale or buyout.

The legal name on today’s privacy policy is Ortho2, LLC, not Ortho Computer Systems, Inc. A Canadian trademark record for ORTHO2 lists Ortho2, LLC of the Ames address as the registered owner and records an assignment from Ortho Computer Systems, Inc. (same street address) dated June 29, 2020. Henry Schein’s fiscal 2025 Form 10-K Exhibit 21.1, filed February 24, 2026, lists Ortho2, LLC as a Delaware subsidiary. That exhibit does not state an ownership percentage, and we did not find a later public announcement that Henry Schein acquired any remaining interest. Treat the current split beyond the 2018 50% announcement as unconfirmed; what is clear is that Ortho2, LLC appears on Henry Schein’s subsidiary list and that the consumer-facing brand remains Ortho2.

Status: The public website is active and still marketed under the Ortho2 name. Current public pages present Edge Cloud as the product for new customers. Company history says Edge Cloud was introduced in 2010, with Edge Imaging, Edge Animations, and Edge Reminders the same year, and that doctor- and patient-facing apps and pediatric-dentistry components for combination practices were added later. The homepage states “25,000+ users across 3,000 locations in 10 countries.” That is a company figure, not independently counted here. A September 23, 2026 company blog post said Ortho2 had been named the exclusive practice-management software provider for two orthodontic service organizations, Phase 1 Equity and EPIC4 Specialty Partners.

Who it’s for

Orthodontic offices that want a specialty practice-management system rather than a general-dental package — including new graduates (the FAQ describes special startup pricing), single-doctor and established offices, multi-location groups, and OSO or DSO organizations. The OSO page describes a dedicated implementation team, custom programming, and an API so groups can pull KPIs into tools such as Tableau, Power BI, SAP, or Domo. inVisit is also marketed as usable with practice-management systems other than Edge Cloud.

Notable products

  • Edge Cloud: The current flagship. Company pages describe it as a cloud-hosted orthodontic practice-management system that centralizes scheduling, billing, imaging, patient communication, and analytics. Data is stored in an off-site data center rather than on a practice-owned server. The FAQ says Edge Cloud is nonetheless an application you install on each computer, not a browser-only site. Core tools described on the product page include a customizable dashboard, scheduler (including procedure timing and sibling appointments), treatment hub, workflows, electronic and paper insurance tools, reporting, an HR manager and timeclock, and Edge Pop (a caller-ID popup tied to the patient record). The FAQ lists subscription pricing that “starts at $384 per month,” plus a one-time onboarding and data-conversion fee; quotes vary by practice size, modules, and licenses. The company says support and training are included and that there is no separate hosting fee. Those dollar figures are the company’s, not independently verified.

  • ViewPoint: The older Windows-based line, launched in 1998 according to the company history page. The 2018 Henry Schein announcement still listed it alongside Edge Cloud. Ortho2 now maintains a dedicated ViewPoint-to-Edge Cloud conversion page and says contracts, AutoReceipts, schedule templates, treatment-chart entries, and full history can transfer. The current marketing site does not present ViewPoint as the product a new office would buy. We did not find a public end-of-life announcement, so whether new ViewPoint licenses are still sold, and how long existing systems will be supported, should be confirmed with the company.

  • Edge Imaging and Premier Imaging: Edge Imaging is described as included with Edge Cloud (and, historically, as a standalone imaging product). Features listed include photo editing with unlimited undos, timepoint comparisons, unlimited 2D/3D storage, and storage of non-image files such as STL files, with no extra workstation dongle. Premier Imaging adds cephalometric tracing, timepoint superimposition, and morphing. A company disclaimer says Premier Imaging must be used under the supervision of a licensed healthcare professional, does not itself identify a diagnosis, and is “currently not available for commercial sale outside of the U.S.”

  • Patient communication and access: Edge Reminders (phone, email, and two-way text, with responses syncing to the scheduler, plus Edge Broadcast newsletters), Online Scheduling, Online Forms (data written back into Edge Cloud), Edge Proposal (a financial slider that lets a responsible party choose among office-set treatment and payment options), Patient Rewards, Patient Tracker (operatory sign-in display), Edge Portal (staff access to charts, images, reports, and the schedule from a tablet or phone; Edge Portal Premium extends access to patients, responsible parties, and consulting professionals and can accept online card payments), and Edge Mobile (free smartphone and tablet access to practice data for users of the Edge software).

  • Edge Animations: A library of customizable patient-education videos, including annotation and the option to export or publish clips.

  • inVisit: A virtual consult and appointment tool. The company says patients complete a custom form and submit photos, staff can follow up by text or email, and the product integrates with Edge Cloud’s Treatment Hub and Edge Reminders. It is also described as usable with other practice-management systems. The inVisit page lists a company price of $109 per month.

  • Integrations: Marketing sometimes says Edge Cloud is complete enough that offices need not rely on third-party tools; the FAQ and help site also document partner connections. The FAQ names Weave, Invisalign, OrthoFi, Global Payments, Sesame, OrthoBanc, Osteoid, “and many more,” and lists card processors Global Payments, OrthoBanc, Vanco, and Worldpay. Ortho2’s help index additionally documents Invisalign (view details and open a patient’s Invisalign record from Edge Cloud), Gaidge, DDX, EasyRx, OrthoFi, New York Medicaid export, and the payment processors above. The OrthoFi help page says the integration can send patient, responsible-party, and appointment information from Edge Cloud into OrthoFi. Practices should ask which connections are current and how ledgers stay in sync.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Long-running, ortho-specific vendor (founded 1982) that still operates under the Ortho2 name, with public product and policy updates into 2026
  • Edge Cloud is positioned as an all-in-one system: practice management, imaging, reminders, education videos, and patient-facing scheduling, forms, and payments
  • Data is hosted off-site, so the company says offices do not need a practice management server; updates and backups are handled by Ortho2
  • A published starting subscription price and an explicit statement that support, training, and hosting are bundled, which is more transparent than vendors who publish no figures at all
  • Documented connections to financial, aligner, analytics, lab, and payment partners, including OrthoFi
  • A dedicated ViewPoint conversion path that the company says preserves full history
  • Explicit OSO/DSO tooling (multi-location reporting, an API for outside dashboards, and recent exclusive-provider announcements)

Cons

  • Despite the “true cloud” wording, Edge Cloud is an installed desktop application that talks to a remote data center, not a browser-only product. Offices still need supported workstations and a reliable internet connection; the company itself notes that a local network can be faster at moving data
  • ViewPoint’s status as a product you can newly buy, and the length of remaining support for offices that stay on it, is not clearly stated on the current site
  • Quoted prices are starting points or module-specific ($384 per month for Edge Cloud, $109 per month for inVisit). Actual cost depends on licenses, modules, and a one-time conversion fee
  • Company claims about user counts and “all-in-one” completeness were not independently verified
  • Premier Imaging’s tracing and morphing tools are clinical decision-support features, not a diagnosis, and the company says they are not sold commercially outside the United States
  • Hosting charts, images, and ledgers off-site means reviewing a business associate agreement, backup and export terms, and what happens to data if the subscription ends
  • The Henry Schein relationship is publicly described both as a 2018 50% investment and, later, by listing Ortho2, LLC as a Henry Schein subsidiary. The current ownership percentage is not itemized

How it fits in a practice

Edge Cloud would typically be the office’s system of record for the appointment book, treatment chart, imaging, patient accounts, insurance, and everyday communication. It is meant to replace another orthodontic practice-management system — including Ortho2’s own ViewPoint — rather than sit beside one. Separate financial platforms such as OrthoFi, payment processors, aligner portals, and analytics tools can connect through the integrations above. inVisit can add virtual consults and remote photo review, including for offices that are not on Edge Cloud.

When evaluating it, ask for pricing and contract terms in writing (including the conversion fee and any module charges), how a ViewPoint or third-party database will migrate, which integrations your office actually needs, workstation and bandwidth requirements, security and backup details, a business associate agreement, and how you would export images and ledgers if you later changed systems. Those are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

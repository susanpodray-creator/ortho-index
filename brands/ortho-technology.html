Ortho Technology

Value-priced U.S. orthodontic catalog label founded in Tampa, Florida in 1991 and owned by Henry Schein since 2012. Lines include Lotus Plus DS self-ligating, Pinnacle and Votion metal twins, PURE sapphire, and Reflections ceramic brackets, plus bands, tubes, wires, TruEase bite correctors, and lab and patient supplies. U.S. orders now go through Henry Schein Dental.

HQ: Savannah, Georgia, USA (mailing address; founded in Tampa, Florida) · Founded 1991 · Researched 2026-10-08 · Official site

Overview

Ortho Technology (often shortened to OT) is an orthodontic supply brand of Ortho Technology, Inc. Its official site is orthotechnology.com. The company’s About page says Brian Leaghty founded it in Tampa, Florida in 1991, and that Henry Schein, Inc. acquired it in 2012. Henry Schein’s July 17, 2012 press release confirms both points. It described Ortho Technology as a Tampa-based distributor of orthodontic products with about 90 team members, roughly $24 million in 2011 sales, and more than 10,000 customers worldwide, reached through direct U.S. sales and overseas distributors. Henry Schein’s outside law firm put the deal’s closing date at June 1, 2012.

Where it is today: The answer depends on which page you read. The Contact page gives a mailing address of 800 King George Blvd, Suite 100, Savannah, GA 31419. The site footer and Privacy Policy list 1822 Aston Avenue, Carlsbad, CA. The Privacy Policy still calls the company “based in the Tampa Bay Area, Florida.” The international order line and fax still use the 813 (Tampa Bay) area code. Older public records place the company in the Tampa area too: a third-party mirror of FDA data shows an expired (2020) Ortho Technology registration at 4614 Pet Lane in Lutz, FL, and a federal supplier (CAGE) record lists a Tampa address. We list Savannah as the HQ because it’s the mailing address on the official Contact page. Treat it as a mailing address, not proof of where products are made or stored.

Who’s behind the products: In current FDA registration and listing data, OT product names (Lotus Plus DS, Pinnacle, Votion, PURE, Reflections, TruEase, the Multi-Adjustable Facemask, Galaxy IPR items) appear under Ortho Organizers, Inc., registration 2081322, at the same Savannah address. That entity is the Henry Schein Orthodontics business (see our Henry Schein Orthodontics and Ortho Organizers pages). Those listings mark the Savannah site as a mix of manufacturing, relabeling, and “develop specifications but do not manufacture at this facility.” That suggests some OT items are made elsewhere to Schein’s specifications. OT says this itself: as the manufacturer it can offer value prices, and when it doesn’t make an item, it buys direct from suppliers. We could not confirm which OT products are made in-house and which are sourced. OT also says it passed an independent ISO 13485 quality-system audit, but the site doesn’t give a certificate number or date.

How you order now: The biggest practical change is the ordering channel. OT’s FAQ says that from February 11, 2026, U.S. direct orders for Ortho Technology, Ortho Organizers, Carriere, and Smilers all go through a Henry Schein Dental account, and OT’s own online ordering is retired. Old OT part numbers are supposed to map to Henry Schein Dental items when you search for them. International customers, including Puerto Rico and Canada, can’t order online for now and are asked to order by phone or email.

Product names below come from orthotechnology.com, researched October 8, 2026.

Who it fits

  • U.S. offices that already buy from Henry Schein Dental and want ortho brackets, bands, tubes, wires, and auxiliaries on the same cart, invoice, and shipment
  • Practices that want a value-positioned twin bracket (Votion or Pinnacle, both metal-injection-molded) instead of premium-brand pricing
  • Doctors who want passive self-ligation in a familiar twin-wing shape (Lotus Plus DS), and both sapphire (PURE) and polycrystalline ceramic (Reflections) aesthetic options from one catalog
  • Former GAC customers looking for equivalents. OT runs a “GAC Resources” page that pairs GAC items with OT alternatives (for example OmniArch PLUS → Votion, In-Ovation → Lotus Plus DS) and posts item-number conversion charts. GAC’s traditional lines were discontinued; see our Dentsply Sirona Orthodontics page.
  • Offices that want Class II and Class III auxiliaries from the same supplier: TruEase bite correctors and the Multi-Adjustable Facemask
  • Less of a fit for offices outside the U.S. that need online ordering right now, or for a practice that wants a small, independent supplier relationship. OT is now one label inside a large distributor.

Notable products

Availability, prescriptions, and kit contents change, and Henry Schein has said it may phase out redundant SKUs over time. Read this as a map, not a quote. Claims like “40% higher bond strength,” “reduced friction,” and “quicker” Class II correction are manufacturer positioning, not independent clinical outcome data.

Self-ligating

  • Lotus Plus® DS: a passive self-ligating bracket built on a mini-twin body. It has a nickel-titanium “planar” door with independent tabs, hooks angled at 15°, and an open design OT pitches for hygiene. OT says an orthodontist designed it, and that its mushroom-shaped pylon base bonds about 40% stronger than standard 80-gauge mesh (OT’s figure). The cuspid base has an adhesive overflow catch to reduce flash cleanup. The product page calls it passive, but the GAC Resources page calls it an “Interactive SLB,” and the tagline is “Dual System.” Ask how the door actually engages wires before you plan mechanics around it.

Metal twins

  • Pinnacle®: a one-piece MIM (metal injection molded) twin that OT describes as a redesign of its earlier Marquis™ bracket. It has cross scribe lines (with a raised horizontal line on the base that OT says is patented), anti-rotation built into the slot in OT’s version of the Roth prescription, rounded slot entries meant to prevent archwire notching, a micro-etched pylon base, and a removable color-coded dot plus a permanent recessed ID mark.
  • Votion®: a low-profile MIM twin with built-in anti-rotation in the cuspid and bicuspid slots in OT’s Roth version, a vertical scribe line along the full length of the bracket, 15° hooks, an ample under-tie-wing area, and a micro-etched 80-gauge foil mesh base. OT’s GAC page names Votion as the alternative to OmniArch PLUS and lists matching Votion buccal tubes.

Aesthetic

  • PURE®: a monocrystalline sapphire twin with a diamond- and heat-polished slot. Its base is coated with aluminum-oxide spheres in what OT calls a “Double Fusing Method,” which OT says gives strong shear bond strength and predictable debonding. OT also says PURE stays clear without staining. As with any sapphire bracket, judge clarity and debonding on your own cases.
  • Reflections™: a polycrystalline alumina ceramic bracket (99.9% pure, per OT) with a dovetail-groove base, compound-contour base, rounded corners, and a removable color-coded ID dot. OT says it’s designed to resist fracture and slot wear.

Other bracket names on OT’s GAC page

  • The GAC Resources page also lists Sensation™ M (an active stainless steel SLB), Sensation™ active ceramic SLB, and Encore!™ metal-lined ceramic brackets as OT alternatives. They aren’t on the main Bracket Systems page, so confirm they’re still available and get their literature before choosing them.

Class II / Class III and other appliances

  • TruEase™ Bite Corrector: an intraoral Class II/III corrector in two styles, Double Lock and Anchor Wire, each in standard and small kits. OT says it installs in one appointment, works in either direction (no left/right part numbers), suits extraction and non-extraction cases, and that the Double Lock version attaches to the archwire without a headgear tube or band. The page says it is not licensed for sale in Canada. OT markets it for “non-compliant patients.” That’s a design goal, not a promise about how a given case will go.
  • Multi-Adjustable Facemask: reverse-pull headgear for Class III cases in growing patients, with vented forehead and chin rests, a sliding range of motion, and a four-step fitting guide.
  • TruEase™ Rapid Palatal Expanders and the Spider Screw® TAD system also appear in OT’s GAC Resources list and Product Literature page.

Everything else (shop categories)

OT’s shop lists aligner accessories, attachments, bonding supplies, buccal tubes and assemblies, burs and discs, elastomerics, fixed appliances, headgear, impression supplies, lab supplies, anchorage, patient care, photography, and wires. Product names from OT’s GAC Resources and aligner pages fill in those categories: elastomerics (for example Creatures of the Sea® elastics, Maximum™ power chain, Power Sticks™ ligatures), bands and tubes (TruFit™ 2.0), lab materials (Clear Advantage™ retainer material and Sports Advantage™ mouthguard material), patient care (Chewies® aligner seaters, retainer cases, relief wax), and wires (TruFlex™ thermal and copper NiTi, BetaForce™ beta titanium, TruForce™ stainless steel, Tooth Tone® coated). The aligner page also lists Galaxy™ IPR gauges, strips, burs, and discs.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Clear value positioning. OT openly says it aims to be the “#1 value brand” in orthodontics. It promises low everyday prices, quarterly promotions, and reliable delivery. You know what kind of supplier you’re dealing with.
  • Very broad catalog. Brackets, bands, tubes, wires, elastomerics, appliances, lab, and patient-care items all come from one label, which helps a new office build a formulary fast.
  • One cart with Henry Schein Dental. For U.S. offices that already buy general dental supplies from Schein, the 2026 integration means one invoice and combined shipping. OT says existing negotiated pricing, credit terms, and your current ortho rep carry over.
  • Both kinds of aesthetic bracket, sapphire (PURE) and polycrystalline (Reflections), plus a passive SLB (Lotus Plus DS). That covers the common choices without adding a second vendor.
  • A practical GAC bridge. The GAC Resources page and item-number charts make it easier to switch formularies after GAC’s traditional lines ended.
  • Long track record. The brand has been around since 1991, with public acquisition records, FDA listings, and 510(k) numbers on some products (for example PURE and the Multi-Adjustable Facemask).

Cons / watch-outs

  • Who makes it isn’t clear. OT calls itself a manufacturer, Henry Schein’s 2012 release called it a distributor, and the FDA listings mix manufacturing, relabeling, and spec-only roles. If country of origin or the maker of record matters to you, ask for it per SKU.
  • Too many names. Ortho Technology, Ortho Organizers, Henry Schein Orthodontics, and Henry Schein Dental can all show up on boxes, invoices, and statements. Pre-February 2026 OT balances go to a separate Ortho Technology lockbox. Give your front desk a cheat sheet.
  • SKUs may be cut. Henry Schein says the portfolio should stay “largely the same” but that redundant SKUs may be phased out. Since Henry Schein Orthodontics also sells its own brackets (Carriere SLX, Aria, and others), check that your chosen OT bracket line is staying before you standardize on it.
  • No online ordering outside the U.S. for now. International, Puerto Rico, and Canada customers order by phone or email until further notice.
  • The website is inconsistent. Lotus Plus DS is called “passive” on one page and “interactive” on another. The HQ appears as Savannah, Carlsbad, and the Tampa Bay Area. Phone numbers are formatted oddly. None of that affects the brackets, but it means you should confirm specs against current instructions for use, not the web pages.
  • Marketing claims. Bond-strength percentages, “reduced friction,” “quicker” Class II correction, and stain-free sapphire are OT’s own claims. We haven’t seen independent comparative data, so ask for it if a claim matters to your choice.
  • Pricing varies by account. List and promotional prices depend on your Henry Schein Dental account and rep. Get a written quote.

Evaluation notes

  • Order sample kits of the bracket lines you’re considering (Votion vs. Pinnacle; PURE vs. Reflections). Check slot finish, pad retention, ID markings, and how cleanly they debond before committing a formulary.
  • Confirm prescription and slot for each line. The anti-rotation feature OT describes is in its version of Roth, so compare torque and angulation tables against what you use now.
  • For Lotus Plus DS, ask for the doctor brochure (linked from OT’s Product Literature page), clarify passive vs. interactive behavior, and get the opening tool and protocol before your first bond.
  • If you’re switching from GAC or another brand, use OT’s conversion charts, then double-check every converted item number in your Henry Schein Dental cart before the first big order.
  • Ask your rep which OT items are planned to stay through the Henry Schein Dental integration, and what the backorder policy is for core brackets and tubes.
  • Set up your account cleanly: one Henry Schein Dental account number, OT part-number lookup, and a note on where any pre-February 11, 2026 OT balance or return goes.
  • For TruEase or the facemask, review OT’s fitting guides and current instructions for use. Patient selection and wear protocols are clinical decisions for the treating orthodontist.

Bottom line

Ortho Technology is a long-running, value-focused orthodontic catalog label. It started in Tampa in 1991 and has been part of Henry Schein since 2012. Today its practical identity is a set of bracket lines (Lotus Plus DS, Pinnacle, Votion, PURE, Reflections) plus a deep supply catalog, all bought through Henry Schein Dental in the U.S. For a new office it’s a reasonable budget-minded option, especially if you already buy from Schein. The trade-offs are business ones, not clinical: layered company names, sourcing that isn’t always disclosed, possible SKU cuts, and an inconsistent website. Get samples, confirm prescriptions, and get written confirmation of which lines are staying before you standardize.

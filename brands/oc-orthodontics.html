OC Orthodontics

U.S. orthodontic manufacturer (World Class Technology Corporation, trading as OC Orthodontics) in McMinnville, Oregon, making metal-injection-molded self-ligating brackets, an aesthetic ceramic self-ligating bracket, buccal tubes, a Class II corrector, wires, elastics, and instruments.

HQ: McMinnville, Oregon, USA · Founded 1991 · Researched 2026-10-22 · Official site

Overview

OC Orthodontics is a U.S. orthodontic manufacturer based in McMinnville, Oregon. The company behind it is World Class Technology Corporation (WCT). FDA’s establishment registration lists WCT as the owner/operator at the McMinnville plant, and OC Orthodontics is the name the products are sold under. We found no separate parent company.

WCT dates to 1991. It started as a metal injection molding (MIM) shop and made brackets, buccal tubes, and self-ligating components for other orthodontic companies. In 2004 it began selling its own line directly to doctors and distributors. A 2012 trade-press profile calls that sales arm Ortho Classic, “a sales subsidiary of World Class Technology,” and later distributor and local-business coverage describes the same 2004 move under the OC Orthodontics name. Ortho Classic and OC Orthodontics are the same business; this page covers both.

The current site describes OC as a “wellness orthodontics” company. Its range centers on passive self-ligating bracket systems (Pitts21 PRO, H4 Plus, H4, and the ceramic Clear21), plus related tools, wires, and practice services.

In the U.S.: FDA’s public database shows WCT as a registered domestic establishment with 11 device listings. They cover metal brackets (Pitts21, Pitts21 PRO, H4, BioMIM, and the APII corrector), orthodontic tubes, the Clear21 ceramic bracket, bands, springs, crimpable stops, wire, elastics, hand instruments, and IPR discs. One 510(k) clearance is on file: Clear21 (K192202, 2019). Most of the other listed devices fall into classes that generally don’t need a 510(k). Check the status of each item you plan to use.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists who want a passive self-ligating system, metal or aesthetic, from a U.S. manufacturer
  • Practices interested in a protocol-driven system with company-run education, such as the Pitts21 approach and its CE courses
  • Offices that want to buy brackets, tubes, wires, elastics, and tools from one supplier
  • Larger or multi-location practices interested in vendor-managed inventory (OrthoVend Max) or an indirect-bonding digital workflow

Notable products

  • Pitts21 PRO: passive self-ligating metal brackets with a progressive slot (square anterior, rectangular posterior), opened with the Quick-Turn Tool
  • Pitts21: the original Pitts self-ligating system, paired with the Pitts archwire sequence
  • H4 Plus and H4: passive self-ligating metal brackets with a rectangular slot, reduced slot depth, and optional integrated hooks
  • Clear21: injection-molded alumina ceramic self-ligating bracket (FDA-cleared under K192202)
  • Buccal tubes and BioMini/BioMIM: tubes and conventional MIM brackets listed with FDA
  • APII Corrector: a universal, cut-to-length Class II/III corrector
  • Wires, elastics, bands, springs, and stops: supporting supplies in the catalog
  • Instruments: opening tools, positioning and debonding instruments, IPR discs
  • OrthoVend Max and Pitts Digital Aesthetic System: an in-office vending/inventory service and an indirect-bonding workflow built around Pitts21 PRO

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • U.S.-based manufacturing, with MIM bracket production that goes back to the early 1990s
  • A focused, coherent self-ligating lineup with metal and ceramic options and matching tools
  • Publicly verifiable FDA establishment registration with listings for its main product families, plus a 510(k) clearance for its ceramic bracket
  • Instructions for use and a full product catalog are posted openly on the official site
  • Education programs and inventory services for practices that want more than product supply

Cons

  • Marketing copy leans heavily on claims of faster treatment, lighter forces, and fewer appointments; these are the company’s own and we found no independent comparisons
  • The Pitts21 systems are tied to a specific protocol and need proprietary opening tools, so switching in or out takes some retraining
  • The “wellness orthodontics” positioning goes further than what product specs support; judge the products on their mechanics
  • Pricing isn’t public, and the ownership history (Ortho Classic vs. OC Orthodontics) is documented mostly in trade press, not on the official site

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

OC is mostly a choice for practices committed to passive self-ligation. Some adopt the whole Pitts21 protocol along with its education, tools, and wire sequence. Others use H4 Plus or H4 as a more conventional rectangular-slot self-ligating system. Before you switch, compare slot sizes and prescriptions with your current setups, check the opening-tool and wire requirements, review the IFUs, and ask your regional representative about lead times, returns, and whether inventory or digital-bonding services are available in your area.

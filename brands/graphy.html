Graphy

Seoul-based maker of Tera Harz photopolymer resins, best known for Tera Harz Clear (TC-85DAC, TA-28, TR-07) for directly 3D-printed Shape Memory Aligners, plus nitrogen post-curing, cleaning, and robotic equipment for in-house aligner production.

HQ: Seoul, South Korea · Founded 2017 · Researched 2026-10-19 · Official site

Overview

Graphy Inc. is a South Korean materials company that develops and manufactures photocurable (UV-cured) 3D printing resins under the Tera Harz name. Its headquarters is in Geumcheon-gu, Seoul, with a research lab in Ulsan and a branch office in Hilden, Germany. Graphy’s own history page and CEO message date the company’s founding to January 2017. It listed on the KOSDAQ in August 2025 (ticker 318060) under Korea’s technology special listing track.

For orthodontists, the headline product is Tera Harz Clear, a resin for printing clear aligners directly, with no printed model and no thermoforming step. Graphy brands the result the Shape Memory Aligner™ (SMA™). The company says the cured material softens in hot water for seating and recovers its printed shape at mouth temperature, giving light, continuous force. It also claims the system can work without attachments and shorten treatment. Those are Graphy’s claims. Some published research supports parts of the material story, but many of the clinical showcases come from company-affiliated speakers, so weigh them accordingly.

Graphy also makes resins for crowns and bridges, dentures, splints, surgical guides, and custom trays, plus the post-processing equipment (centrifuge, nitrogen curing unit, ultrasonic cleaner, and a robotic production cell) that goes with an in-house printing workflow.

Regulatory status (U.S.): The FDA 510(k) database lists two clearances for Tera Harz Clear as a sequential aligner (product code NXC), both with Graphy, Inc. as applicant:

  • K223355, “Tera Harz Clear,” decided November 3, 2022
  • K240597, “TERA HARZ CLEAR,” decided March 5, 2024. Its summary lists three models: TC-85DAC, TR-07, and TA-28.

The K240597 summary describes the cleared indication as treating tooth malocclusion in patients with permanent dentition. It also names the specific scanner, design software, printers, and post-cure units used in the cleared workflow (see “How it fits in a practice”). Graphy holds separate 510(k)s for other Tera Harz resins, including crown and bridge and denture materials.

U.S. availability: Graphy says it set up a U.S. subsidiary, Graphy USA (a Delaware company), in September 2025. The U.S. SMA site lists an office in Jupiter, Florida. In a February 2026 press release, Graphy said it is building an SMA central lab and production facility in Florida and has partnered with Benco Dental to distribute its materials and equipment in the U.S. Both are recent, so confirm what is actually stocked and supported before you plan around it.

Internationally, Graphy sells through regional distributors. Its partner page lists dozens of countries, and for Germany it names Smile Dental and medentis medical. Graphy also announced a partnership with Germany’s SCHEU Group (SCHEU-DENTAL / Smile Dental) in 2026.

Note: graphy.com belongs to an unrelated online-course company. The orthodontic Graphy’s sites are itgraphy.com and graphysma.com.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists and labs that already print in-house, or plan to, and want to print aligners directly instead of thermoforming over printed models
  • Clinicians who want control over aligner thickness and shape in specific areas, which direct printing allows
  • Practices weighing a Graphy-assisted route, where you scan and approve the plan and Graphy manufactures, against a full in-house setup
  • Offices also interested in printed retainers, splints, or restorative resins from the same supplier
  • Teams that can handle real post-processing: centrifuging, nitrogen curing, and cleaning, done the same way every time

Notable products

  • Tera Harz Clear TC-85DAC: Graphy’s original direct-print, shape-memory aligner resin and the basis of its Shape Memory Aligner™
  • Tera Harz Clear TA-28: a stiffer aligner resin. Graphy’s spec sheet lists higher flexural strength and modulus than TC-85.
  • Tera Harz Clear TR-07: a clear resin that Formlabs’ resin list labels for direct-printed retainers
  • Other Tera Harz resins: TC-80DP and BR-23 (crown and bridge), THD (denture base), TFDH (flexible partial denture), TE-151 (splint), SG-100 (surgical guide), TT-Bu (custom tray), and others
  • Tera Harz Cure (THC): a nitrogen-atmosphere UV post-curing unit. Graphy says curing without oxygen improves strength and reduces surface tack.
  • Tera Harz Spinner: a centrifuge for removing excess resin, holding up to 16 aligners per Graphy’s brochure
  • Tera Harz Care: a small ultrasonic cleaner and warmer for finished aligners
  • Tera Harz Smart Robot (THSR): a 6-axis robot arm that automates the steps from print removal through curing for higher-volume in-house production
  • GraphyTeams and SMARTCheck: a cloud ordering and treatment-planning portal for clinics that outsource production to Graphy, where you review, modify, and approve plans
  • Direct Aligner Designer (DAD): Graphy’s own aligner setup and shell-design software with automatic print supports, described in a 2024 JCO article as free to use. The current official sites don’t feature it, so confirm it is still offered and check its regulatory status before relying on it.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • A real alternative to thermoforming: aligners printed to the designed shape, with thickness you can vary by area
  • FDA 510(k) clearances on record for Tera Harz Clear (K223355, K240597), plus CE and other national registrations that Graphy lists
  • Listed on Formlabs’ Form 4B third-party resin page, with print settings available, along with other printers Graphy names
  • A complete ecosystem from one maker: resins, curing, cleaning, automation, and an outsourcing option if you don’t want to print
  • A growing body of published research on the material, and an official hands-on certification course (listed from USD 880 at the Seoul HQ)
  • A publicly listed company, so more financial disclosure than a typical private resin supplier

Cons

  • Big claims, mostly the company’s own. Attachment-free movement, shorter treatment, and “pain-free” wear are marketing claims. Many clinical showcases come from Graphy-affiliated opinion leaders, not independent trials.
  • U.S. operations are new. The subsidiary dates to September 2025, and the Florida hub and Benco partnership were announced in 2026.
  • The cleared workflow is specific. The 510(k) summary names particular printers and post-cure units, so check the IFU before swapping in other equipment.
  • In-house production is a serious setup. You need a printer, a centrifuge, nitrogen curing (with an air compressor), cleaning steps, and staff time, plus a learning curve on supports and post-processing.
  • No public pricing for resins or equipment on the official sites
  • Software is a moving target. DAD’s status is unclear, and a 2023 European distributor FAQ said it had no medical device certification for use in the EU. Many users design in third-party software instead.
  • Information is scattered. Product pages on the official English site weren’t loading during our research, so specs had to come from brochures, the FDA, and partners.

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

There are two routes. With the Graphy-assisted route, you scan, upload to GraphyTeams, review and approve the plan in SMARTCheck, and receive finished aligners. That keeps your up-front investment low. With the full in-house route, you do the setup and aligner design, print in Tera Harz Clear, centrifuge off excess resin, cure under nitrogen, then finish and clean.

The K240597 summary lists this cleared system: 3Shape TRIOS 3 scanner, 3Shape Ortho System design software, UNIZ SLASH 2 or NBee or SprintRay Pro 95 printers, and CureM U102H or Tera Harz Cure post-curing. Graphy’s 2025 brochure also features Formlabs Form 4B and 4BL, UNIZ UBEE, and AccuFab CEL printers. Match your equipment to the current IFU and ask Graphy USA or your distributor which combinations they support. If you’re in the U.S., also confirm who handles service and training locally before buying hardware.

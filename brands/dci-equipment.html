DCI Equipment (DCI Edge)

Newberg, Oregon dental parts and operatory equipment maker founded in 1983, now part of NSK. It sells DCI Edge chairs, delivery systems, LED operatory lights, stools, and cabinetry, including a small orthodontic line of chairs and ortho carts, through authorized dealers. DCI-branded replacement parts are sold separately.

HQ: Newberg, Oregon, USA · Founded 1983 · Researched 2026-10-06 · Official site

Overview

DCI (DCI International, LLC) is a U.S. maker of dental replacement parts and operatory equipment. It sells two lines under separate sites. DCI Edge (dciedge.com) covers chairs, delivery systems, LED operatory lights, stools, and cabinetry. DCI Parts (dcionline.com) sells replacement components such as syringes, valves, tubing, and control blocks. In the queue we called it “DCI Equipment”; the brand you’ll see on equipment and quotes is DCI Edge. DCI doesn’t make brackets, wires, aligners, or imaging. For an ortho office, it fits in the operatory build-out conversation, next to brands like Boyd and Midmark.

History (from DCI’s own timeline): John and Janelle Spencer founded DCI in 1983. They started with a small home loan, an idea for a better dental syringe, and a living-room office. DCI says it became the world’s largest dental parts manufacturer by 1993. It bought Pelton & Crane and Marus in 1997, expanding into equipment, and sold those companies in 2005 while keeping the parts business. DCI returned to equipment in 2016 with the DCI Edge Series 5 operatory package. It added cabinetry and an orthodontic line in 2018. An older DCI brochure dates the founders’ start to “Portland, Oregon, 1982,” but the current About page says 1983. We use 1983.

Ownership: NSK, the Japanese handpiece maker (Nakanishi Inc.), became a minority investor in October 2020. On August 21, 2023, NSK announced it would buy the remaining 51% for full ownership. Trade coverage at the time said DCI’s management, brand, and sales channels would stay in place. DCI’s own timeline lists becoming “an NSK company” in 2024. That NSK tie shows up in the products: the Series 5 and Series 5 Plus delivery systems integrate NSK electric motors as factory-installed options, and DCI Edge promotes NSK handpiece bundles.

Headquarters and manufacturing: the mailing address is 705 S. Springbrook Rd, Building C, Newberg, OR 97132. DCI Edge lists two manufacturing facilities with showrooms, one in Newberg, Oregon and one in Charlotte, North Carolina. Its ortho brochure says it opened the 40,000 sq ft Charlotte cabinet plant in 2018. A third showroom sits at NSK America’s headquarters in Hoffman Estates, Illinois. DCI says each chair is tested with 600 lbs before it leaves the Newberg factory.

Self-reported claims to treat as marketing: DCI calls DCI Edge North America’s “fastest-growing” and “second-largest” equipment manufacturer. It also says the Series 5 became the chair most often chosen by U.S. private-practice and DSO dentists in 2022. Trade press repeated the “No. 2 dental chair manufacturer” framing in 2023. We haven’t seen independent market-share data, so read these as positioning, not ranking.

Product names and specs below come from live DCI Edge pages researched on October 6, 2026. Configurations and options change, so treat this page as a map, not a quote.

Who it fits

  • Ortho offices that want simple, value-focused chairs and ortho carts for an open bay, without premium GP-unit features they won’t use
  • Practices that want one brand for chairs, lights, stools, cabinetry, and sterilization casework, ordered and serviced through a familiar dental dealer
  • Ortho + pediatric or ortho + GP offices that need both ortho carts and full delivery systems (with handpieces and electric motors) under one brand
  • Offices that put a high value on a long parts warranty. DCI Edge offers 10 years on chairs, units, lights, and cabinetry, but labor isn’t included (see cons)
  • Practices with older equipment that want to upgrade delivery while keeping the chair. DCI says the Series 4 delivery system can be retrofitted to most dental chairs

Notable products

Orthodontic chairs and carts

DCI Edge’s ortho page lists two chair options and ortho carts (sold by dealers as “ortho cabinets”):

  • Fixed-base ortho chair — a solid steel fixed base with an adjustable backrest, offered with ErgoBack Asepsis or Narrow Back Asepsis backs. The ortho brochure mentions an integrated foot control for positioning.
  • Adjustable-base option (Series 4 chair) — for offices that want seat-height adjustment so clinicians can work seated or standing. The older ortho brochure gives a 16”–31” seat range, but the current comparison table lists 16”–30”. Confirm on the quote.
  • Headrests and armrests — ponytail, V, and dual-articulating headrests. The brochure says armrests are optional on both fixed and adjustable chairs. Upholstery comes in many colors, and you can request samples.
  • Ortho carts/cabinets — standard 28” or narrow 24” widths, in a fixed version (6” powder-coated steel sub-base that can sit over existing utility hookups) or a mobile version (moves with its utility hookups). Features include soft-close drawers and doors, configurable drawer sizes and layouts, a slide-out delivery module for maintenance, and a recessed groove along the countertop edge so bands and brackets don’t roll off. Countertops come in laminate or solid surface. Options include a trash drop, monitor mount/CPU package, quick-disconnect panel, and glove dispenser. A Patterson Dental listing gives right/left-hand model numbers CZ1450/CZ1451 (free-standing), CZ1550/CZ1551 (narrow free-standing), CZ1650/CZ1651 (mobile), and CZ1750/CZ1751 (narrow mobile). Confirm them on your quote.
  • Kid Cushion — a patented booster cushion in all upholstery colors, useful if you treat younger patients.

DCI also nudges ortho buyers toward the Series 5 chair if they want a more premium patient chair.

Patient chairs (Series 5 and Series 4)

From DCI Edge’s comparison table, both chairs share hydraulic lift and back movement, a 60° swivel, armrests that rotate 170° for entry and exit, a push-button dual-articulating headrest, a thin narrow backrest, accelerometer position sensors, Bluetooth diagnostics through an Android phone, and a 30” top height.

  • Series 5 — DCI’s best-seller. It has a cast ductile-iron base, a 550 lb patient capacity, soft start/stop, a headrest “scoop,” multi-layer memory-foam upholstery, a 15” low height, and an optional AirSoothe air-cushion massage. Ultraleather upholstery is an upgrade.
  • Series 4 — the value chair. It has a laser-cut steel base, a 350 lb patient capacity, a 16” low height, and several back styles (ErgoBack or Narrow Back, Plush or Asepsis) and headrest styles.

Delivery systems

  • Series 4 — post-mount, swing-mount, or cart configurations, with rear or telescoping assistant packages. Swing-mount units convert between left- and right-hand use. DCI says it’s the only delivery system that can be retrofitted to most dental chairs.
  • Series 5 — a rear floor-mounted unit (swing or post mount, with or without cuspidor) with 6” of side-to-side travel, 3” of height adjustment on the mobile surface, an assistant touchpad that pivots 180°, and support for ancillary devices. The NSK NLZE electric motor is an integrated option.
  • Series 5 Plus (announced June 10, 2025) — adds single or dual NSK NLZ PRO electric motors with a touchscreen, a bigger flex arm, a USB hub, dual touchpads, and room for devices like ultrasonic scalers, intraoral cameras, and curing lights.

In most ortho operatories, the ortho cart’s delivery module is the more relevant piece. The Series 5/5 Plus units are built around handpiece-heavy GP and specialty dentistry.

Lights, stools, and cabinetry

  • Series 4 and Series 5 LED operatory lights — 5000 K color temperature with a CRI of 90. The Series 5 adds dual programmable no-touch sensors, third-axis head rotation, a 3” × 6” pattern, and a no-cure setting. DCI’s brochure says the no-cure setting filters UV light to extend working time on light-cured resins. Lights come in track, ceiling, post, wall, and cabinet mounts. Check how the no-cure mode works with your bonding adhesive before you rely on it.
  • Series 5 Plus stools — clinician and assistant stools with a 13° anterior-tilt seat, textured anti-slip upholstery, 65 mm low-profile casters, a 300 lb capacity, and a patent-pending brake on the assistant’s stool.
  • Cabinetry (Series 4/Series 5) — side cabinets, 12 o’clock workstations, center islands, lab casework, and sterilization centers. DCI lists a 6” powder-coated steel sub-base, 1 mm thermal-fused PVC edge banding, soft-close hardware, standard Wilsonart solid-surface countertops (20+ colors), and 32 cabinet colors. The sterilization centers have red/blue lighting for contaminated vs. sterile storage, flip-down wrap stations, an undermount stainless sink, and waterproof sterilizer towers.

Parts

DCI Parts sells replacement components for many brands of dental equipment, not only DCI Edge. The parts site lists same-day shipping on orders placed before 3 p.m. Pacific and a one-year warranty on most parts. That can help keep older chairs and units running whoever built them.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Long parts warranty on paper. DCI Edge equipment bought on or after February 20, 2025 gets 10 years of free replacement parts for defects in chairs, units, lights, and cabinetry, 5 years on upholstery (up from 1), and 1 year on tubing.
  • A ready-made ortho cart. Standard or narrow, fixed or mobile, with a grooved countertop edge and slide-out delivery module. These are practical details for bonding and adjustment chairs in an open bay.
  • Value positioning. DCI openly pitches the ortho chair and Series 4 as “no unnecessary, costly extras,” which suits high-volume ortho chairs that mostly recline, rotate, and hold position.
  • Parts depth. DCI has made dental replacement parts for decades, so parts support is a core business, not an afterthought.
  • One brand for the whole operatory. Chairs, lights, stools, operatory cabinetry, and sterilization casework from one maker can make design, ordering, and color matching easier.
  • U.S. manufacturing on both coasts. Chairs come from Newberg, Oregon, and cabinets from Newberg and Charlotte, North Carolina.

Cons / watch-outs

  • The warranty covers parts, not labor. DCI won’t pay dealer or service-company labor or shipping to its factory. Upholstery coverage excludes normal wear, stains, cuts, scratches, and damage from disinfectants. Ask what labor costs for a typical chair repair in your area.
  • Dealer-only service. The warranty applies to equipment sold, installed, and serviced by authorized DCI Edge dealers. DCI warns that service by anyone else voids the warranty. Before you buy, find out who the authorized tech near you is and how fast they respond.
  • The ortho line is small. There are two chair options and one cart family. Ortho-focused makers offer more chair variants (drop-toe consult chairs, records-room chairs, multiple delivery widths). Check that DCI’s two chairs cover your consult, records, and treatment needs.
  • Spec drift between documents. The older ortho brochure and current pages differ on some details (founding year 1982 vs. 1983; adjustable seat top height 31” vs. 30”). Lock exact models and specs on the formal quote.
  • No published prices. DCI Edge sells through dealers, so expect quotes to vary by dealer, package, and promotion.
  • Promotions and NSK bundles. Bundles with free extras and NSK handpiece packages are mostly aimed at GP buyers. Make sure a bundle isn’t adding features an ortho bay won’t use.
  • Tax framing. The cabinetry page pitches Section 179 savings on “serialized” cabinets and tells buyers to consult a tax advisor. Do that before timing a purchase around it.
  • Self-reported rankings. “Fastest-growing,” “second-largest,” and “most chosen chair” claims come from DCI and its press releases. We haven’t verified them independently.
  • No imaging or ortho-specific supplies. Plan imaging, scanners, sterilizers, compressors, and vacuums separately.

Evaluation notes

  • Start with the floor plan. Count bonding/adjustment chairs, consult, and records rooms. Then decide which chairs can be fixed-base (cheaper, stable) and which need the Series 4 adjustable base (sit/stand work). Decide whether each bay gets a standard 28” or narrow 24” ortho cart, fixed or mobile.
  • If you plan mobile carts, confirm with your contractor how utilities will run. If you plan fixed carts, confirm the sub-base sits correctly over the utility hookups.
  • Sit in both chairs. Check back width, headrest options (ponytail vs. V vs. dual-articulating), armrest choices, and how quickly the chair repositions between patients.
  • If you cure chairside, check the Series 5 light’s no-cure setting against your adhesive and curing-light workflow.
  • Get labor rates, response times, and the name of the authorized service company in writing. With a parts-only warranty, labor is your real long-term cost.
  • Ask which upholstery and cleaning agents DCI approves. Disinfectant damage is excluded from the upholstery warranty, and DCI’s warranty text excludes damage from contact with non-recommended materials.
  • Compare quotes from at least two dealers. Ask for an itemized quote (chair, cart, light, stools, freight, install, plumbing/electrical) so you can compare it with other operatory brands.

Bottom line

DCI is a long-running Oregon parts maker that has built a full operatory line, DCI Edge, since 2016, and NSK has owned it fully since 2023. For an ortho office, the practical shortlist is the fixed-base ortho chair or Series 4 adjustable chair, the standard or narrow ortho cart (fixed or mobile), the Series 4/5 LED lights (the Series 5 adds a no-cure setting), Series 5 Plus stools, and sterilization cabinetry. The Series 5 chair is the upgrade if you want more patient comfort.

The trade-offs are practical, not clinical: a small ortho chair range, a long parts warranty that doesn’t include labor, warranty coverage tied to authorized dealer service, and no published pricing. Line up local service, cleaning-agent rules, and exact specs before signing.

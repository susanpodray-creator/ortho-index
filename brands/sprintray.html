SprintRay

Chairside dental 3D printing ecosystem from SprintRay Inc. of Los Angeles, California: resin printers, automated wash and cure, AI design software, and dental resins, including orthodontic workflows such as models, direct-printed retainers, and indirect bonding trays.

HQ: Los Angeles, California, USA · Founded 2014 · Researched 2026-11-01 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

SprintRay makes 3D printers, post-processing equipment, software, and resins for dental offices and labs. The site footer names the company SprintRay Inc., and its contact page gives a mailing address of 2705 Media Center Drive, Los Angeles, California. SprintRay says it was founded in Los Angeles in 2014.

Ownership: SprintRay appears to be privately held and venture-backed. Trade press (AVCJ, October 2022) reported a Series D of roughly US$100 million led by SoftBank Vision Fund 2. Some of that coverage describes SprintRay as China-based and links it to a Zhejiang-founded entity; SprintRay itself presents as a Los Angeles company. We could not confirm the corporate structure between the US company and any affiliates, so treat that as unconfirmed. We found no later funding round and no report of SprintRay being acquired through October 2026.

Recent developments: In September 2025 SprintRay announced it had acquired the EnvisionTEC/ETEC dental product portfolio (formerly part of Desktop Health), including patents, trademarks, and materials such as Flexcera. Its company page also mentions a partnership with Align Technology.

FDA status: openFDA lists several 510(k) clearances with SprintRay, Inc. as applicant, mainly restorative and prosthetic resins (for example denture base K220979 and K221678, Digital Crown K222623, Crown HT K242277, Digital Temp K242559, OnX Tough K230445, Apex Flex K250617, Midas Restore K260661) and NightGuard Flex (K212448). We did not find a SprintRay-applicant 510(k) for the retainer or indirect bonding tray resins; check the product IFU and regulatory status for those before use.

Who it’s for

General dental and orthodontic offices that want to print in-house, as well as dental labs. For orthodontics the relevant uses are printed models for in-office aligner thermoforming, directly printed clear retainers, and indirect bonding (IDB) trays.

Notable products

  • Printers: Pro 2 and Pro S resin printers, and Midas, which uses SprintRay’s patented Digital Press Stereolithography for highly filled restorative materials.
  • Post-processing: ProWash S, ProCure 2, and NanoCure wash and cure units.
  • Software: RayWare desktop print setup, cloud print setup, and an AI-assisted design suite with on-demand design services.
  • Scanning: the Foundation intraoral scanner, which feeds into SprintRay’s own design and print workflow.
  • Materials: SprintRay resins (models, retainers, IDB, night guards, crowns, temporaries, dentures), plus third-party resins validated through its Open Certified Resin System.
  • Orthodontic workflows: direct-printed retainers with AI retainer design, clear IDB trays that let you cure through the tray, and aligner models (the aligners themselves are thermoformed, not printed).

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • A single vendor for printer, wash/cure, software, and resins, which can simplify validation and support
  • Several of its restorative and appliance resins have FDA 510(k) clearances on public record
  • Orthodontic-specific workflows (retainers, IDB trays, aligner models) are documented on the official site
  • Offers an open resin program in addition to its own materials

Cons

  • Pricing for printers, resins, and software isn’t fully published; expect quotes, kits, and possible subscriptions
  • Ecosystem lock-in: the smoothest workflows assume SprintRay hardware, software, and resins together
  • Marketing uses broad “most advanced” and “industry-leading” claims that we couldn’t verify independently
  • The corporate structure and links to China-based affiliates aren’t clearly disclosed by the company
  • We found no public 510(k) under SprintRay’s name for the retainer and IDB resins; confirm regulatory status for your region
  • In-house printing adds staff training, resin handling, and post-processing quality control

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

SprintRay would sit after the scanner and design step. Offices print models, retainers, or IDB trays in-house instead of sending them to a lab. It works alongside existing orthodontic CAD and aligner software, or SprintRay’s own AI design tools. When you evaluate it, ask for total cost (printer, kits, resins, consumables, software subscriptions), which resins are cleared for each indication, compatibility with your scanner and design software, service terms, and the training included.

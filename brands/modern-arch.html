Modern Arch

Small, family-run orthodontic supply company in Wyomissing, Pennsylvania, started in 2002. Best known for archwires: NiTi, copper NiTi, stainless steel, beta titanium, lingual, dimpled, pre-torqued, coated, plated, and its Tri-Sectional (dual-dimension) finishing arches. It also sells value-priced brackets, crimpable stops and loops, elastomerics, pliers, and aligner and retainer materials, ordered direct online, by phone, fax, or email.

HQ: Wyomissing, Pennsylvania, USA · Founded 2002 · Researched 2026-10-09 · Official site

Overview

Modern Arch (legal name Modern Arch, LLC; the site footer says “Modern Arch Orthodontic Supplies”) is a small orthodontic supply company at 1402 Penn Avenue, Wyomissing, Pennsylvania. Its official site is modernarches.com. Note the plural “arches” in the web address.

The About page says Modern Arch started in 2002 with Dr. Art Wool, an orthodontist, and his daughter Suzanne, and that she has led it since 2013, after her father’s death. A 2019 advertorial in Orthodontic Products names Suzanne Wool as President and CEO. It also says the business grew out of Wonder Wire, the name Dr. Wool first used to sell his own orthodontic devices to other orthodontists. A 2020 clinical paper still cites his dual-dimension arch technique manual as published by Wonder Wire in Wyomissing, 1991. Modern Arch calls itself a family company and a “small to mid-level distributor” that also manufactures.

What it is in practice: an archwire-first catalog with a long tail of supplies. Wires are the core: many alloys, arch forms, and sizes, plus specialty shapes most big catalogs don’t list as separate items. Around them are value-priced brackets, crimpable accessories, elastomerics, pliers, and lab and aligner materials. You order direct, with no account or contract needed. Website purchases are U.S.-only. International customers order by email or phone.

Maker vs. relabeler, and quality certificates: Modern Arch says it manufactures, and both ISO 13485:2016 certificates on its site cover “manufacture and distribution” of orthodontic products. But its current FDA registration (3006791365, Wyomissing) lists the site as a “Repack or Relabel Medical Device” establishment. The only listed product code is DZC (orthodontic wire), first listed in 2005. We could not confirm which items are made in Wyomissing and which are sourced and relabeled. If country of origin matters to you, ask per SKU.

The site links two quality certificates:

  • An ACM-CCAS ISO 13485 certificate (198337U). It expired October 18, 2025, but the homepage still links it.
  • A second ISO 13485 certificate (US037639C02240301), valid March 29, 2024 to March 28, 2027, linked as “Health Technology Certification.” Its scope covers non-active dental devices and distribution.

Product details below come from modernarches.com and its linked PDFs, researched October 9, 2026.

Who it fits

  • Offices that want one place for unusual wire shapes: dimpled NiTi, pre-torqued NiTi, reverse curve, lingual mushroom forms, gold- or rhodium-plated, and coated wires, plus ordinary round and rectangular wires in packs of 10, 25, or 100
  • Doctors who use, or want to try, dual-dimension (Tri-Sectional) finishing and retraction arches. That’s Modern Arch’s signature product, rooted in its founder’s patents.
  • Practices that want to buy direct with no contract, and like simple ordering by web, phone, fax, or email from a small, reachable team
  • Budget-minded offices looking for value twin brackets (MIM Roth/MBT), a passive SLB, ceramic and polycarbonate options, and everyday supplies to fill gaps
  • Less of a fit if you want a large distributor’s rep network, consolidated invoicing with your general dental supplier, published clinical data on each product, or a fully current, polished website

Notable products

Availability, sizes, and pricing change, and many product pages show a $0.00 base price until you pick options. Read this as a map, not a quote. Phrases like “holds hard bends,” “no breakage,” and “antimicrobial coating” are Modern Arch’s own claims, not independent test results.

Archwires (the core line)

  • NiTi: superelastic (SE) and thermal (TH) wire in round (.010–.020) and rectangular sizes up to .021 × .025. Forms are Natural (“True Form”), Universal (which the catalog compares to Ormco’s Damon arch form), and European. Packs come in 10, 25, or 100. Variants include reverse curve (toe-in or straight legs), pre-stopped (midline stop), dimpled (dimple in or out, to stop wire shifting), pre-torqued (20° anterior torque, Natural form only), and lingual mushroom shapes.
  • Copper NiTi: listed in Natural and Universal forms. Watch the spec: the website says it’s responsive at a “low 80 degrees F,” while the 2020 catalog says “fully active at 95°F (35°C).” Ask which applies to the current lot.
  • Coated and plated: MDW (PTFE-coated, labial or anterior surface only) in NiTi and stainless steel, a “NanoTech” ceramic-coated NiTi (marketed as slick and antimicrobial), and NiTi or steel plated in 24K gold or rhodium, positioned for esthetics or nickel-allergy cases. Ask about bare core alloy exposure at cut ends and nickel content before relying on plating for an allergy patient.
  • Stainless steel (304): round and rectangular in three forms, plus heat-treated hard, 8-strand braided and coaxial, keyhole-looped, brass-posted closing arches, and lingual round and rectangular wire in small to extra-large.
  • Beta titanium (TMA): nickel-free, round and rectangular, in Natural and Universal forms, plus looped versions.
  • Tri-Sectional (dual-dimension) finishing arches: a full-size anterior segment joined to reduced steel posterior segments. The idea is to keep anterior torque control while the back of the wire slides freely during space closure. Anterior alloys include steel, beta titanium (optionally with 20° anterior torque), and NiTi. Anterior lengths run every 2 mm from 22 to 50 mm, in Natural, Universal, and European forms. A newer Finishing-Retraction arch (steel or beta titanium, sized by anterior mm, with short retraction hooks, 5/pk) sits alongside it.
  • Force charts: Modern Arch publishes recovery-force bar charts for its NiTi, thermal copper NiTi, TMA, and multistrand wires. That’s more than many small vendors share. The test method isn’t stated, so treat the numbers as relative within its own line.

Crimpable accessories

  • Magic Crimp Stops: U-shaped crimp-on stops in steel, MDW-coated, and 24K versions. They’re covered by US 10,143,539, which Modern Arch owns.
  • Crimpable Retraction Loop: a crimp-on loop that turns a straight wire into a looped one. It’s covered by US 10,278,792, held by Suzanne Wool. The 2020 catalog also lists a Universal Retraction Loop.
  • Crimpable ball posts, hooks, eyelets, buttons, cleats, and tongue tamers in steel, gold-plated, and esthetic finishes

Brackets, bands, and tubes (secondary line)

  • Twin and V-slot bracket cases: MIM, Roth or MBT, .018 or .022, with a “buy 10 cases, get 1 free” offer, plus singles
  • Diamond-T: a passive self-ligating bracket (Roth, MBT, or high torque, .022, MIM monoblock base, horizontal auxiliary slot). Silver X is listed as an active-slot SLB.
  • Esthetic: ceramic cases and singles (Roth or MBT), a sapphire Roth case, BioCrystal hardened-polycarbonate brackets ($52 per 20-bracket kit as of October 9, 2026), and a solid 24K gold-plated twin case
  • Others: double-slot twin brackets, lingual bracket cases, buccal tubes (convertible, non-convertible, SL, double slot), molar bands in half and whole sizes, palatal bars, and Agility-360 distalizers
  • Brand names and manufacturers behind the brackets aren’t listed. Ask who makes each line before you standardize.

Everything else

Elastomerics (latex and non-latex elastics, O-rings, chain in 34 colors, thread and tubing), chewies, separators, and cheek retractors; adhesives and curing lights; NiTi and steel springs and coils; lab and retainer wire; Tru-Tain DX (PCTG) and Tru-Pu (polyurethane) aligner and retainer sheet plus Keystone sheet materials; and instruments, including the Zero-Bend crimping plier, Mathieu, distal end cutter, debonding and aligner pliers, arch-wire measuring templates, and plier and arch-wire racks. Modern Arch’s instrument instructions for use list a 5-year warranty on material, workmanship, and corrosion resistance, with the usual exclusions for misuse and improper sterilization.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Deep wire selection. Few catalogs list this many arch forms, alloys, and specialty shapes (dimpled, pre-stopped, pre-torqued, reverse curve, lingual, plated, coated, dual-dimension) as standard items in 10/25/100 packs.
  • A real design history behind the signature product. The Tri-Sectional arch traces back to the founder’s dual-dimension wire patents and technique, and peer-reviewed literature discusses the general dual-dimension concept. A 2020 prospective study found faster space closure with dual-dimension wires than rectangular wires during mini-implant retraction, with no difference in anchorage loss. That study evaluated the concept, not Modern Arch’s product specifically, and its authors said it can’t fully replace rectangular wire.
  • Simple, low-commitment ordering. No contracts and no account needed to start. You can order by web, phone, fax, or email. Shipping is UPS, and the company says most orders ship the same day. The site offers 3% off for ACH or prepaid check.
  • More technical paperwork than you might expect. Published force charts, safety data sheets for each alloy and material, an instrument cleaning and sterilization instructions-for-use document, and a current ISO 13485 certificate.
  • Patented crimpables you won’t find everywhere, with active U.S. patents behind the crimp stop and the retraction loop.

Cons / watch-outs

  • Maker of record isn’t clear. Modern Arch says it manufactures, but FDA registration lists it as a repackager/relabeler, and bracket makers aren’t named. Ask for country of origin and the maker per SKU if that matters to you.
  • Outdated paperwork on the site. The homepage still links an ISO certificate that expired in October 2025. A separate certificate is valid to March 2027. The downloadable catalog is from 2020, and the Customer Service page still says “for the last 15 years.”
  • Policies don’t match. The website says returns must be unopened full packs within 30 days, with no refund after that. The 2020 catalog allows returns up to 90 days with a 15% restocking charge after 30 days. The FAQ says purchase orders are accepted, but the catalog says “we do not bill or have open accounts.” Get the current terms in writing.
  • Spec inconsistencies. The copper NiTi activation temperature differs between the website and the catalog, and many product pages show $0.00 until you pick options. Confirm specs and pricing on the quote and the package insert, not the web page.
  • “Patented” labels need context. The core three-segment arch patent (US 6,811,397) is shown as expired on Google Patents, and the 2010 “Trisectional Arch Wire” application was abandoned. Treat “patented” as product history, not proof of exclusivity.
  • Small company. Expect a small team, not a field rep network. That’s great for direct answers, but think about backorder risk if one vendor supplies most of your wire sequence.
  • Limited independent data. Apart from the general dual-dimension literature, we found no independent comparative testing of Modern Arch wires or brackets. The site’s review section reprints one doctor’s favorable magazine review. Useful, but it’s one opinion.

Evaluation notes

  • Request samples of the wires you’d use most, such as a NiTi sequence, rectangular steel, TMA, and a Tri-Sectional arch. Check arch-form match against your current templates, finish, cut-end quality, and how the steel posterior segments slide in your brackets and tubes.
  • For copper NiTi, confirm the stated activation temperature and whether force charts reflect the current lot. Compare its feel to your current thermal wire before switching a whole sequence.
  • For Tri-Sectional or Finishing-Retraction arches, read Modern Arch’s technique notes (on its Articles page) and decide the anterior alloy, torque, and mm sizing up front. Mechanics and case selection are the treating orthodontist’s call.
  • For plated or coated wires used in nickel-sensitive patients, ask about the core alloy, plating thickness, and what’s exposed at cut ends and bends. Use your own clinical protocol.
  • Ask who makes each bracket line (Diamond-T, Silver X, twins, ceramic, sapphire) and get prescription tables and instructions for use before bonding a first case.
  • Get the current return, purchase-order, and shipping terms by email, and ask for the current ISO certificate and FDA registration details if your office keeps vendor quality files.
  • If you plan to make Modern Arch a primary wire vendor, ask about stock depth and typical backorder times for your core sizes, and keep a second source in mind.

Bottom line

Modern Arch is a small, family-run Pennsylvania supplier whose real strength is archwires. It offers a broad, unusual range of shapes and alloys, built around the founder’s dual-dimension Tri-Sectional arch and a few patented crimpable accessories. Ordering is easy and contract-free, and prices are positioned as low. The trade-offs are about documentation and transparency, not clinical red flags: unclear sourcing (FDA lists it as a relabeler), an expired certificate still on the homepage, mismatched return and billing terms, and a dated catalog. Samples and written terms are worth getting before you build a wire sequence around it. It’s a good secondary or specialty-wire source to know.

Cloud 9 Ortho

Browser-based orthodontic and pediatric practice management software, now a product of Planet DDS of Irvine, California. Covers scheduling, financial arrangements and ledgers, insurance claims, patient communication, digital forms, and multi-location (OSO/DSO) management, with optional Apteryx imaging and CephX tracing integrations.

HQ: Irvine, California, USA (parent Planet DDS) · Founded 2009 · Researched 2026-11-12 · Official site

Overview

Cloud 9 is cloud-based practice management software built for orthodontic offices, and it also serves pediatric dental practices. It is now sold and supported by Planet DDS, which also makes the Denticon general-dental practice management system and Apteryx imaging. The old cloud9.software address now redirects to a Cloud 9 product page on planetdds.com, and Cloud 9 support runs through a Planet DDS help center. The product still carries the Cloud 9 name. Planet DDS markets it alongside its other products and now ties them together under a shared login and admin layer it calls DentalOS.

History and ownership: According to the January 2023 acquisition release, Cloud 9 Software was founded in 2009 and was then headquartered in Roswell, Georgia. The private investment firm Accel-KKR invested in Cloud 9 in 2019. Planet DDS announced on January 4, 2023 that it had completed its acquisition of Cloud 9 Software from Accel-KKR. It did not disclose financial terms. Planet DDS’s own About page also dates the Cloud 9 acquisition to 2023.

Planet DDS itself has been privately held and backed by private equity for years. Level Equity invested in 2019. In June 2022, Planet DDS announced an agreement to be recapitalized by Aquiline Capital Partners, with Level Equity rolling part of its stake. Level Equity says the deal closed in July 2022 and now lists its investment as “partially realized.” Aquiline lists Planet DDS as a current portfolio company. An October 2025 law-firm announcement described a debt refinancing for “Aquiline portfolio company Planet DDS.” We found no public report of a later sale of Planet DDS through November 2026. Ownership percentages are not disclosed.

Legal entity and HQ: Planet DDS gives its headquarters as 17872 Gillette Avenue, Suite 250, Irvine, California. Its 2022 and 2023 releases were datelined Newport Beach, California. The website terms of use name PDDS Buyer, LLC, a Delaware limited liability company at that Irvine address, and say the site is operated by Planet DDS, Inc. We did not find a current public statement of the legal entity that holds Cloud 9, or of whether Cloud 9 still has its own office in Georgia. Treat Roswell, Georgia as Cloud 9’s base at the time of the 2023 acquisition, not as a confirmed current address.

Scale (company figures, not independently verified): The 2023 acquisition release said Cloud 9 served more than 2,900 locations. Accel-KKR’s copy of the same release says 2,300, so the exact count is unclear. A January 2026 Planet DDS release said its products together support 14,500 practices and 175,000 users, and named DSO customers adding or converting orthodontic locations to Cloud 9.

Who it’s for

Orthodontic practices, pediatric dental practices, and multi-location groups, including orthodontic and dental service organizations (OSOs and DSOs). The product page says the implementation approach is designed for DSOs, OSOs, and groups, and it mentions special packages for new graduates. Groups that run both general-dental and orthodontic locations are a stated target for the Cloud 9 and Denticon integration.

Notable products

  • Cloud 9 practice management: A browser-based system that Planet DDS says was written in HTML5 “from the ground up” for the cloud, and that runs on Mac or PC. The product page describes a scheduler with new-patient tracking and automated tasks, financial arrangements (fee schedules, insurance benefits, down payments, and autopay with primary and secondary payment methods), paperless contract signing, a ledger that keeps adjustment history, and a multi-option insurance claims queue.
  • Cloud 9 Connect: Built-in patient communication: automated text and email reminders, two-way texting, marketing and recurring care campaigns, and billing outreach.
  • Cloud 9 Signature and patient portal: Digital forms that file into the patient record, plus online booking, online payments, and appointment confirmation.
  • Payments: Planet DDS Pay (now marketed as Payments+) for card and mobile-wallet payments that post to the Cloud 9 ledger, plus an automation suite for pending payments, receipts, and returned-ACH fees.
  • Imaging: Cloud 9 stores and lays out patient images. A small local helper called the C9 Agent acquires images from TWAIN-compatible scanners and x-ray devices. Help documentation says the C9 Agent supports 2D only (JPG, up to 5 MB per image) and that Cloud 9 has no built-in 3D viewer. For more, practices can add Apteryx, Planet DDS’s cloud imaging product, which opens inside Cloud 9 from the patient record and can display x-rays and STL files. Apteryx is added to the subscription at extra cost. CephX (from Orca Dental) is a paid, case-count-based add-on for AI cephalometric tracing and 3D CBCT and STL viewing inside Cloud 9. The help article describes its AI as “FDA-approved”; Ortho Index has not independently confirmed an FDA listing for it.
  • Documented integrations: Planet DDS announced Cloud 9–Denticon and Cloud 9–Apteryx integrations in May 2024. Help documentation names DentalXchange as the only integrated clearinghouse for electronic claims. OrthoBanc is among the partner logos on the product page. The product page claims “over 25 integrations” and shows partner logos for communication, payment, lab-Rx, and marketing vendors. Planet DDS’s shared integrations list covers Denticon, Cloud 9, and Apteryx together, so confirm which partners connect to Cloud 9 specifically.
  • DentalOS: Planet DDS’s platform layer that gives Denticon, Cloud 9, and Apteryx one staff login, centralized user management, multi-factor authentication, and single sign-on through Microsoft Entra ID. It also lets groups organize locations by region or brand.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Browser-based, with no practice-owned server, and it runs on Mac or PC
  • Built for orthodontics (contracts, responsible parties, autopay, ortho charting), with pediatric support
  • Communication, digital forms, patient portal, and payments are built in or offered by the same vendor
  • Clear multi-location and DSO focus, with a single login across Planet DDS products through DentalOS
  • A documented integration path to Planet DDS’s own imaging (Apteryx) and general-dental system (Denticon)

Cons

  • Pricing isn’t published; the FAQ says you’ll get a quote after a needs review
  • Built-in image capture is 2D only, through a local helper app; 3D viewing and AI ceph tracing need paid add-ons (Apteryx or CephX)
  • Only one clearinghouse (DentalXchange) is integrated for electronic claims, according to help documentation
  • Cloud 9 is one product inside a larger private-equity-backed company. Its own legal entity and current office aren’t publicly stated, and product direction depends on Planet DDS’s wider platform plans
  • Marketing claims (“#1,” “only fully cloud-based”) and location counts come from the company and weren’t independently verified; the 2023 release’s two versions give different location counts
  • A browser-only system depends on a reliable internet connection; the company itself suggests a backup connection
  • Hosting records off-site means reviewing the business associate agreement, backup and export terms, and what happens to data if the subscription ends

How it fits in a practice

Cloud 9 would be the office’s system of record for the schedule, patient accounts, contracts and ledgers, insurance claims, and patient communication. It would replace another orthodontic practice management system. Imaging can stay basic, using in-app 2D capture, or be extended with Apteryx, CephX, or another imaging vendor. Groups that also run general-dental offices on Denticon can link the two systems through Planet DDS’s integration and shared DentalOS login.

When evaluating it, ask for pricing and contract terms in writing, including add-ons such as Apteryx, CephX, payments, and marketing. Also ask how data and images will be converted from your current system, which integrations your office actually needs and whether they work with Cloud 9 specifically, which imaging devices are compatible, what security and backup measures are in place, whether a business associate agreement is provided, and how you would export records if you changed systems. These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

OrthoSelect (DIBS AI)

American Fork, Utah digital orthodontic lab and software company (OrthoSelect, LLC) best known for DIBS AI, a digital indirect-bonding platform that plans bracket positions and supplies 3D-printed transfer trays, plus 3D-printed study models and occlusal splints (BiteShield). Company-reported start date is 2004.

HQ: American Fork, Utah, USA · Researched 2026-11-19 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

OrthoSelect is a Utah digital orthodontic laboratory and software company. Its flagship is DIBS AI, a digital indirect-bonding (IDB) platform. The practice sends an intraoral scan; the company’s software proposes bracket positions; the clinician reviews, adjusts and approves the plan; and OrthoSelect 3D-prints bracket transfer trays and ships them to the practice. The company also makes digital and 3D-printed study models and 3D-printed occlusal splints, sold through a separate BiteShield portal.

Company and status: FDA records name the operator OrthoSelect, LLC, in American Fork, Utah. The company says it began in 2004, but its own pages disagree on when it started making study models (2002 on one page, 2004 on others), so this page gives no founding year in the structured data. DIBS began as legacy software in 2016 and DIBS AI launched in July 2020, according to the company. The business is active: the websites carry a 2026 copyright, its FDA establishment registration is active with an expiry year of 2026 in openFDA, and a peer-reviewed study that used DIBS AI appeared in 2025. We found no public report of an acquisition or merger. Third-party databases describe the company as unfunded and privately held. Those are secondary sources, so treat them as unconfirmed.

Names and sites: the company is marketed under two names. OrthoSelect is the lab and company brand on myorthoselect.com. DIBS AI is the product, with its own newer site (dibsai.com). The company site (myorthoselect.com) is listed as the official website; both were live on the research date. The company states it serves orthodontists in more than a dozen countries and completes thousands of cases a month; these are company claims we could not verify.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontic practices that want to move bracket positioning from the chair to a digital plan and keep their current bracket brand and prescription. The company says DIBS AI accepts any bracket the practice supplies and lists several bracket manufacturers as compatible.
  • Practices that already take intraoral scans (the company says any scanner producing STL or PLY files works, with optional CBCT) and are willing to send cases to an outside lab for trays.
  • Practices that want an outside lab for study models or occlusal splints.
  • Practices or labs interested in the company’s partner-lab network. The company’s partner page lists partner labs by region, and some practices print trays in-house on supported printers, according to the company.

Notable products

  • DIBS AI: browser-based case review, AI-proposed bracket placement that the clinician edits and approves, and predicted-outcome tools. The company also lists automated CBCT merging and torque prediction. DIBS AI 7.0 was announced in May 2022, and an April 2023 announcement described non-stick trays and a browser-based version.
  • Indirect-bonding transfer trays: 3D-printed in the lab and shipped with the practice’s brackets loaded, according to the company’s current doctor page. The page quotes a turnaround of 5–6 business days with shipping included; earlier announcements described different arrangements, so confirm current terms.
  • Digital and 3D-printed study models: stone-model or scan inputs, with finishing options, printed models and analysis reports. The company’s pages list prices and turnaround times, and those pages are not consistent with each other, so we do not repeat them.
  • BiteShield splints: 3D-printed, digitally designed occlusal splints, including a one-piece option and a two-piece tripod design licensed from an outside inventor. The company describes the resin as FDA-cleared. That claim concerns a third-party resin and we did not verify it independently.
  • Clear aligners (via partner): a 2022 announcement named a third-party aligner system from another company as OrthoSelect’s official clear-aligner partner. The “Aligners” link on the OrthoSelect site goes to a page that currently returns 404, so we could not confirm this is still offered.

Regulatory record (openFDA and FDA): FDA 510(k) K192701, “DIBS (Digital Indirect Bonding System),” applicant OrthoSelect, LLC, was cleared on April 21, 2020 as a Class II software device (product codes PNN and LLZ). FDA’s summary names 3Shape’s Ortho System (K152086) as the predicate and describes the device as software for virtual treatment planning and design of indirect bonding transfer media; the device itself has no patient contact. The openFDA registration listing for OrthoSelect shows DIBS AI listed under K192701 and a separate listing for a DIBS AI Indirect Bonding Tray under product code QJJ (Class I) with a 2024 listing date. The FDA record uses the name “DIBS,” so we are inferring that the current “DIBS AI” product falls under that clearance based on the registration listing. We did not review the cleared-versus-current feature set. In openFDA we found no OrthoSelect listing for study models or splints.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • The planning software has a public FDA 510(k) clearance (K192701), and the establishment registration lists the DIBS AI product and the tray
  • Works with a practice’s existing bracket brand and prescription, per the company, and the clinician can edit every bracket position before anything is printed
  • Outsourced tray production means the practice does not need a printer or resin of its own, though some practices print in-house on supported printers, per the company
  • One independent 2025 in vivo study that used DIBS AI is available (see below), in addition to company-published material
  • Reported turnaround and a case-based fee model (per the company’s own site) instead of a capital purchase; no pricing is repeated here because it changes
  • A single lab can supply bonding trays, study models and splints

Cons

  • Most efficiency figures are company claims. A March 2026 Orthotown article reports 1,308 cases across 16 practices, with treatment 21.1% shorter and 23.8% fewer visits for DIBS AI than for direct bonding. The article is authored by OrthoSelect itself and describes a retrospective, post hoc comparison with no randomization; practices followed OrthoSelect’s protocols for DIBS AI cases but not for the direct-bonding cases, and the article acknowledges there “likely may have been some variation” in direct-bonding technique
  • A 2019 company-published accuracy summary (446 brackets, 23 patients, mean deviation reported as 0.121 mm, 97.3% within 0.5 mm) predates DIBS AI and was done on the earlier DIBS software
  • The independent 2025 in vivo study (840 teeth, 14 patients, published in Orthodontics & Craniofacial Research) found statistically and clinically significant differences between AI-placed and directly bonded brackets: RMS discrepancies exceeded 0.50 mm in four tooth types for AI vs. direct. The authors suggested the AI and clinician-adjusted virtual placements were within 0.25 mm of each other. The study is small, so it neither confirms nor refutes the company’s claims
  • Public details are uneven: the company’s pages differ on founding and study-model dates, quoted turnaround times, and one DIBS AI partner page displays “HQ: New Jersey” while every other source places the company in American Fork, Utah
  • The “Aligners” link on the lab site is dead, and the 2022 aligner partnership is not confirmed as current
  • Patients’ scans and case data go to an outside lab and cloud service, so review the data-protection terms and whether a business associate agreement is available

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

OrthoSelect sits at the bracket-placement planning and bonding-tray step of a fixed-appliance workflow, upstream of bonding day. It is neither a practice-management system nor an aligner planner. A practice would typically scan, submit the case, review and approve the digital bracket plan, then receive trays and bond. Study models and splints are separate lab services that could run in parallel.

When evaluating it, ask:

  • Current per-case fees and any program or commitment terms (the company’s pages mention switching programs and ROI claims that are marketing, not independent data).
  • Which bracket brands and prescriptions are supported for tray loading, and who supplies and invoices the brackets.
  • Current tray turnaround and shipping terms, and what happens to a case if the tray does not seat.
  • Which FDA clearance and registration covers each product you would use, and the status of the tray listing.
  • What independent data exist beyond the company’s published analyses, and how your own cases compare.
  • How patient scans are stored and retained, whether a business associate agreement is offered, and how you could export your cases.
  • Which services are currently offered (aligner partnership, splint portal) given inconsistencies on the lab site.

These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

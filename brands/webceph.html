WebCeph

Browser- and app-based orthodontic analysis software from AssembleCircle Corp. of Seongnam, South Korea. It offers AI-assisted cephalometric landmark detection, tracing and analysis, VTO/STO and face simulation, superimposition, image galleries and case presentation, on a free tier with paid monthly upgrades. FDA 510(k) K220903 (2022).

HQ: Seongnam, Gyeonggi-do, South Korea · Founded 2019 · Researched 2026-11-23 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

WebCeph is orthodontic analysis software that runs in a web browser and in mobile apps, with an optional desktop app on its top tier. Dentists upload lateral cephalograms and clinical photos; the software detects cephalometric landmarks automatically, lets the user adjust them, and produces tracings, measurements and reports. It also offers treatment and surgical simulation (VTO/STO), superimposition, image galleries and patient-presentation tools.

It is made by AssembleCircle Corp., a South Korean company that names itself as the manufacturer on WebCeph’s site and in FDA records. AssembleCircle’s own corporate website currently points only to WebCeph. A 2022 company interview published by the Korean government-backed K-Global startup program says the company was incorporated in October 2019, after WebCeph began as an earlier cloud-based project that added AI later. We found no public record of an acquisition or outside parent company; the company appears to be privately held.

Location. The website lists a business address in the Pangyo technology district of Seongnam, Gyeonggi-do, South Korea. The 2022 FDA 510(k) record shows an earlier address in the same city.

FDA status. openFDA and the FDA 510(k) database list K220903, “WebCeph,” submitted by AssembleCircle Corp. and found substantially equivalent on August 17, 2022 (Traditional 510(k); product code LLZ, radiological image processing system, Class II, 21 CFR 892.2050). The predicate was EzOrtho from Ewoosoft (K192888). The cleared indications are for use “by dentists who provide orthodontic treatment for image analysis, simulation, profilogram, VTO/STO (Visual Treatment Objective) and patient consultation,” with results “dependent on the interpretation of trained and licensed practitioners or dentists,” and only for patients above 7 years old. It is a prescription device. The 510(k) summary describes landmark detection as an aid to tracing that the user can adjust. The website labels the US release as a different software version (1.0.5) from the Korean release (2.0.3), so features shown on the site may not all fall under the US clearance; ask the company which features are cleared in the US.

Korea. The site lists a Korean medical device certification (D제인 21-4431호, issued through the National Institute of Medical Device Safety Information) as dental image treatment-planning software, and a manufacturing license (D제7366호). Korean trade press reported in October 2021 that WebCeph had completed Korean regulatory (KFDA) and GMP approval in May 2021. We did not look these numbers up in the Korean regulator’s database. The company also lists ISO 13485:2016 certification, a 2025 medical-device cybersecurity verification from KISA (Korea Internet & Security Agency), and several Korean patents and one US patent. These are company statements.

Europe. The site has EU-specific terms and privacy policy pages, and its EU version names a Malta-based company as its EU authorized representative (“EC REP”). The site’s regulatory list does not include a CE certificate, and the 2021 trade report described CE marking as in preparation at that time. We did not confirm current CE status, so check it before use in the EU.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists and general dentists providing orthodontic treatment who want cephalometric tracing and analysis without installing desktop software.
  • Clinicians and residents who want a free or low-cost entry point. A basic tier is free; paid tiers add features.
  • Clinicians planning orthognathic cases who use VTO/STO and face simulation for planning and patient discussion.
  • Researchers and teaching programs. WebCeph appears in a large number of published cephalometric studies, many of them comparing its automated output against manual tracing.

The company has given varying user figures over time (for example, about 37,000 members as of September 2021, per Korean trade press). These are company claims and the definitions of “member” and “user” differ between reports.

Notable products

  • Automated cephalometric tracing and analysis: AI detection of landmarks on lateral cephalograms, with user adjustment, multiple built-in analyses, and custom analysis and measurement wizards. On its About page, the company says its models (support vector machine, gradient boosting and convolutional neural network methods) showed a mean deviation of 1.5 mm or less on lateral cephalograms and 2.5 mm or less on frontal radiographs and clinical photos against expert-marked ground truth, using 10,550 training images including augmentation. These are the company’s own figures.
  • Simulation: VTO/STO for orthodontic and orthognathic surgery simulation, plus face simulation of profile changes from clinical photos. Simulations are planning and communication aids, not predictions of outcome.
  • Superimposition: Automated cephalometric superimposition for evaluating progress; maxilla/mandible, multiple-image and profile superimposition on paid tiers.
  • Image viewer, gallery and case room: Single, dual and template views, PDF export, and case organization.
  • Practice and patient tools: A scheduler, notes (WebNotes) and Toosapp, a companion app for sending educational materials to patients.
  • Paid tiers (prices as listed on the website on 2026-11-23, VAT excluded):
    • Free: automated tracing, cephalometric analysis, VTO/STO simulation, automated superimposition, image gallery and case room. Paid tiers are described as ad-free, which suggests the free tier shows ads.
    • PLUS ($9.99/month): adds ad-free use, time-lapse videos, extra landmarks, landmark tables with Excel export, maxilla/mandible and multiple-image superimposition, clinic logo instead of a watermark on reports, and more.
    • PREMIUM ($14.99/month): adds PA (frontal) cephalometric, frontal face and lateral face viewers.
    • ELITE ($19.99/month): adds a 3D STL model viewer with up to 5 GB storage, an occlusogram viewer, patient analysis reports, automated photo upload, full-screen case presentation and an installable PC desktop app.
  • API and integrations: A documented API for patient, record and image upload. The API terms say access requires prior approval and is not included with a subscription. The site also offers a plugin for DentWeb, a Korean dental practice-management system.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • FDA 510(k) clearance (K220903) is in the public FDA database, alongside Korean certification listed by the company
  • Runs in a browser and on mobile, with no installation needed for the core features
  • A usable free tier and low published monthly prices for paid tiers
  • Lets the user correct AI-placed landmarks before measurements are used, which independent studies suggest is important
  • Widely studied: many peer-reviewed papers compare it with manual tracing and other software, so its strengths and weaknesses are documented

Cons

  • Independent studies show that fully automatic results need clinician review:
    • A 2025 study in the American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics found that “the automated systems demonstrated low reliability and accuracy for cephalometric analysis,” that CephX and WebCeph “are still not suitable for assessing soft-tissue parameters,” and that they “are valuable diagnosis tools only when landmark correction is applied.”
    • A 2024 study in BMC Oral Health found significant differences from human experts in 10 of 11 measurements for WebCeph, and concluded that all three AI programs tested “showed inaccuracies compared to human expert measurements.”
    • A 2025 study in The Angle Orthodontist found that WebCeph and one other program “showed greater variability, especially for linear measurements,” and that errors in head positioning on the radiograph reduced accuracy for all software tested.
    • A 2025 study in the Journal of Orofacial Orthopedics found significant differences between all four commercial providers tested and an expert reference, and concluded that fully automated analyses “should only be used under supervision of experienced clinicians.”
    • Not all results are negative: a 2024 study in Clinical Oral Investigations found mean differences from manual tracing under 1 mm/degree for most measurements and called most automated measurements “clinically acceptable,” while noting that SNA, SN-PP, IMPA and soft-tissue measurements need extra attention and manual adjustment.
  • Accuracy figures on the company’s site are its own and were not produced independently
  • The US-cleared version and the version described on the website may differ, and current CE status in Europe is unconfirmed
  • Patient images and data are stored in the vendor’s cloud (the company says on Amazon Web Services) outside the practice, and the company is based in South Korea. Data-protection terms, data location and business associate terms matter
  • The company’s US HIPAA guide says AssembleCircle may act as a business associate, and tells practices they “must receive a valid HIPAA authorization from your patient in order to use WebCeph services.” That is the company’s position; practices should review it with their own compliance adviser
  • The free tier appears to be ad-supported, and some practice-presentation features are only on higher tiers

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

WebCeph sits at the diagnostic and records-analysis step. It is not a full imaging-capture suite or a practice-management system; images are taken on the practice’s existing radiography and camera equipment and uploaded. A practice might use it alongside, or instead of, desktop imaging and ceph software such as Dolphin Imaging or OnyxCeph, and alongside a practice-management system such as Cloud 9, which offers a different AI ceph add-on (CephX).

When evaluating it, ask:

  • Which features and software version are covered by the US 510(k), and whether the version you would use matches it.
  • Current CE and other regulatory status if you practice outside the US or Korea.
  • How AI-placed landmarks are reviewed and corrected in your workflow, and who signs off on the final tracing.
  • Where patient data is hosted, how long it’s kept, whether a business associate agreement is provided, and how the company’s HIPAA guidance fits your own policies.
  • How you would export tracings, images and records if you stopped using the service.
  • Whether you need API access or a PMS integration, and whether one is available for your system.

These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

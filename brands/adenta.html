Adenta

Family-run German bracket and wire maker (Adenta GmbH, Gilching, near Munich) that dates its roots to 1949. Its live web shop lists CNC-milled metal twins (CROWN, ECONOLINE), aesthetic brackets (CLEAR sapphire, DISCREET, BREEZE SL self-ligating), and the TRIAMOND Wedgewise bracket. Older Evolution SLT and JOY lingual lines are not in the current shop, and the U.S. office site is a placeholder.

HQ: Gilching, Germany · Founded 1949 · Researched 2026-10-07 · Official site

Overview

Adenta is the brand of Adenta GmbH Herstellung und Vertrieb kieferorthopädischer Produkte, an orthodontic manufacturer at Gutenbergstraße 9-11, 82205 Gilching, Germany, which Adenta describes as near Munich. The Impressum lists Claus-Dieter Schendell-Gröling as managing director and the company as registered at the Munich local court under HRB 70812. The German site is adenta.de, and the web shop is shop.adenta.com. The old global domain, adenta.com, did not load for us during research.

History (from Adenta’s own catalogs): Adenta’s 2018 Spain catalog says the German head office was founded in 1949 by Wolfram Schendell and the U.S. office in 1995. It presents the company as three generations of the Schendell family. The 2019 catalog says the company started out in medical and technical parts “more than 70 years ago” near Munich, before moving into orthodontics. A third-party summary of the German commercial register dates the current GmbH to May 4, 1983. So 1949 is the family business’s start, not the date the current legal entity was registered. The year Adenta specialized in orthodontics shows up as 1989 in an indexed copy of a 2015 catalog, but we couldn’t open that file to confirm it.

Self-ligation history: A 2007 U.S. Federal Circuit opinion in a patent case Adenta brought says Adenta’s Time self-ligating bracket was shown at the 1994 AAO annual session in Orlando. It also says American Orthodontics “manufactured and distributed brackets for Adenta,” and that Adenta sold a bracket called Evolution under a license from American Orthodontics. A review article in an orthodontic journal says Time was designed by Austrian orthodontist Dr. Wolfgang Heiser and reached the market in 1995. None of the current sources we found say whether the American Orthodontics arrangement still exists.

How Adenta presents itself today: The adenta.de homepage reads as much like a purchasing partner as a bracket maker. It offers to consolidate a practice’s ordering, and it says Adenta also makes products “for the market leaders,” meaning some of its manufacturing may be sold under other companies’ names. These are Adenta’s own figures, not audited: “23,000 practices supplied worldwide,” “60% of German practices use Adenta,” and “11.7 million teeth moved in the last 5 years.” The homepage copy refers to 2022, and the site footer says 2024, so it hasn’t been updated recently.

A contact detail worth knowing: Both the adenta.de homepage and the Triamond product site route all inquiries and orders to an @psm-medical.com email address. The Triamond site also gives PSM Medical GmbH in Gunningen, Germany as its mailing address. PSM Medical is a separate German maker of orthodontic implants and TADs (BENEfit, quattro). Its own About page lists bracket production among its new directions under 2023, but it doesn’t mention Adenta. We could not confirm whether this is an ownership change, a sales partnership, or something else. Ask before you assume who invoices, ships, or supports Adenta products.

U.S. distribution: The 2019 catalog lists Adenta USA Inc, 81 Clover Road, Ivyland, PA 18974. At research time, adentausa.com showed only a “Coming Soon” page. We couldn’t confirm a current U.S. distributor.

Product names below come from Adenta’s live web shop and its 2019 English catalog, researched October 7, 2026.

Who it fits

  • Offices that want CNC-milled, one-piece metal brackets from a German maker and care about how a bracket is made (Adenta pitches milling over metal injection molding)
  • Practices that use Roth, MBT, Ricketts, or standard edgewise in .018 or .022 slots. Those are the options shown in Adenta’s shop listings.
  • Doctors who want several aesthetic options from one catalog: monocrystalline sapphire (CLEAR), Adenta’s “α-Ceramic” (DISCREET PLUS), a second ceramic twin (DISCREET), and a passive self-ligating aesthetic bracket (BREEZE SL)
  • Clinicians curious about the TRIAMOND Wedgewise triangular-slot system, who are willing to learn its two-wire protocol and judge the vendor’s claims on their own cases
  • European offices that can order by phone or email. The shop shows prices in EUR plus VAT, and product pages say this maker’s items can currently be ordered only by phone and/or email.
  • Not a fit (as far as we can verify) for a U.S. office that needs a local rep, a U.S. catalog, and dependable domestic stock. Confirm U.S. distribution first.

Notable products

Availability, prescriptions, and kit contents change. Read this as a map, not a quote. Phrases like “ultra-precise,” “accelerated tooth movement,” “no enamel abrasion,” and “faster leveling” are manufacturer positioning, not clinical outcome data.

Metal brackets (current shop)

  • CROWN™ / CROWN Mini™: CNC-milled twin with an anatomical base designed around the tooth’s visible lines to help with positioning (2019 catalog). The catalog says CROWN Mini is “20% more compact” with a full mesial-distal slot width. The shop lists Roth and MBT in .018 and .022, plus CROWN Mini.
  • ECONOLINE™: full-size, one-piece, CNC-milled conventional twin positioned as the “workhorse” bracket. The shop lists MBT, Roth, Ricketts, and standard edgewise. The 2019 catalog also lists a DEEP BITE™ lower anterior bracket with lower incisal wings.

Aesthetic brackets (current shop)

  • CLEAR™: monocrystalline sapphire with CNC-cut slots and a zirconia-coated base for retention (2019 catalog). The homepage features it as a best-seller. Roth and MBT, .018 and .022.
  • DISCREET™ / DISCREET PLUS™: aesthetic twins. The 2019 catalog describes DISCREET PLUS as made from what Adenta calls α-Ceramic, which it positions as easier on enamel and easier to debond than conventional ceramics, and says PLUS adds deeper undertie wings and stronger hooks than DISCREET. Treat the enamel and debonding claims as Adenta’s, not independently shown.
  • BREEZE SL™: an aesthetic passive self-ligating bracket with a clip and a reduced slot height (2019 catalog). The shop lists “Tooth colored” versions and “Translucent” versions in Roth and MBT, .018 and .022. To its credit, the catalog notes that food can collect under the clip and that the translucent version suits patients with very good hygiene.

TRIAMOND™ (current shop)

  • TRIAMOND Bracket MBT Wedgewise: a self-ligating bracket with a two-wall triangular slot that holds a square archwire rotated 45° (a “Wedgewise” wire). The protocol uses about two wire sets. The adenta.de homepage features it and links to triamondbraces.com. Treat these as vendor or author claims, not settled evidence: the Triamond site says treatment time drops about 50% and practices can treat “up to 60% more patients,” and a 2020 trade-journal article claims 40–60% shorter treatment. Neither Adenta’s pages nor the Triamond site name the manufacturer of record.

Lingual and self-ligating lines in the 2019 catalog, not found in the current shop

  • EVOLUTION SLT™: a self-ligating lingual bracket with a “non-locking” clip and 40° or 60° base inclinations. The catalog says it was available in OraMetrix/SureSmile, 3Shape, and OnyxCeph libraries and came with Adenta lingual lab services.
  • JOY™: a low-profile lingual bracket for direct bonding without a lab setup, positioned for simpler (often 2D) adult cases. Its “12 to 18 weeks” timeline in the catalog is vendor copy.
  • SMART CAP™: an indirect-bonding cap and tray system for Adenta’s lingual brackets.
  • FLAIR SLT™: a labial self-ligating metal bracket with a “dynamic non-locking” clip.

None of these four came up when we browsed or searched the public web shop at research time. They may still be available by phone or email, or they may be discontinued. Confirm before planning a lingual case around Evolution SLT or JOY. The shop does list a lingual retainer wire.

Wires, tubes, and auxiliaries (current shop)

  • Wires: FLEXADENT™ NiTi SE (including pre-torqued, reverse-curve, and intrusion versions), THERMADENT™ thermal NiTi, COPPERDENT™ copper NiTi, BEAUTYDENT™ aesthetic NiTi, DURADENT™ stainless steel, plus ligatures, springs, Quad Helix, and Goshgarian palatal bars
  • PREMIUM bondable tubes (single convertible, single non-convertible, double convertible, mini), elastomerics, headgear, chin cups, and safety modules
  • “Smart Innovations”: Distal Glide (a modified pendulum appliance), Easy-Way-Coil-System, Flex Developer 2.0 (Class II), and HYCON safety screw keys. Flex Developer 2.0 parts carry a “TD-” item prefix rather than Adenta’s usual numeric codes, so ask who makes each auxiliary.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • A focused bracket house with a clear manufacturing story. Adenta’s catalogs stress one-piece CNC-milled brackets, a real difference from the molded brackets most makers sell. Whether milling helps your finishing is something to judge chairside, but you know what you’re buying.
  • A good spread of aesthetic options. Sapphire (CLEAR), α-Ceramic (DISCREET / PLUS), and passive SL aesthetic (BREEZE SL) give you real choices in one catalog. The catalog is also candid about hygiene trade-offs under SL clips.
  • Long self-ligation history. Court records and the literature tie Adenta to the Time bracket from the mid-1990s, and a self-ligating lingual line (Evolution) was on the market by the 2000s.
  • A public web shop with list prices (EUR plus VAT) and item numbers by tooth, which makes budgeting easier than quote-only brands.
  • Family-run and transparent about it, with a current Impressum naming the managing director and register number.

Cons / watch-outs

  • Who you’re actually dealing with is unclear. Orders route to an @psm-medical.com address, and the Triamond site lists PSM Medical’s Gunningen address. We couldn’t find a public explanation. Get it in writing: who sells, ships, handles complaints and recalls, and holds the regulatory paperwork.
  • The U.S. path is unconfirmed. The Pennsylvania Adenta USA office from the 2019 catalog now has only a placeholder site. A U.S. office may face import lead times, EUR pricing, and no local rep.
  • Phone or email orders only. The shop shows prices, but product pages say this maker’s items can currently be ordered only by phone and/or email. That’s fine for planned restocks and awkward for a last-minute reorder.
  • Lingual lines may be legacy. Evolution SLT, JOY, SMART CAP, and FLAIR SLT are in the 2019 catalog but not the live shop. Don’t build a lingual program on them without written confirmation of supply.
  • Big marketing claims. Statements like “accelerated tooth movement,” “no enamel abrasion,” Triamond’s “50% less treatment time” and “60% more patients,” and the homepage’s market-share figures are vendor or author claims. We haven’t seen independent data, so ask for peer-reviewed evidence before relying on them.
  • The website looks dated. The homepage copy talks about 2022, adenta.com didn’t resolve for us, and the contact link in the shop footer (adenta.de/kontakt) returns a 404. That suggests a company in transition. Check that the documents and instructions for use you receive are current.
  • Some shop items may be made by others. Adenta says it also acts as a purchasing partner for practices, so the shop may include items it doesn’t make. Confirm the legal manufacturer on each label.

Evaluation notes

  • Ask for sample brackets. Some shop listings, such as BREEZE SL, say viewing samples (“Ansichtsmuster”) are available. Check slot finish, pad retention, and how the BREEZE SL clip opens before you commit a whole formulary.
  • Lock in prescription and slot (Roth vs. MBT vs. Ricketts vs. standard edgewise; .018 vs. .022) per line. Not every prescription is available in every line.
  • For TRIAMOND, treat it as a different system, not a drop-in bracket swap. It needs its own wires and protocol. Try it on a few cases first, and measure your own treatment times instead of relying on the published percentages.
  • For lingual, ask in writing whether Evolution SLT, JOY, and SMART CAP are still made, and whether lab support and digital library files are current.
  • Ask who the contracting and shipping party is (Adenta GmbH, PSM Medical, or a distributor), and where complaints, recalls, and declarations of conformity come from.
  • U.S. offices: ask who imports, who handles FDA listing, and who handles returns before you place a first order. Don’t assume the 2019 Pennsylvania address is still active.
  • Don’t confuse brands at purchasing time: Quick®/BioQuick® are Forestadent bracket names, not Adenta lines.

Bottom line

Adenta is a small, family-run German bracket and wire maker in Gilching. It traces its roots to 1949 and has a real place in self-ligation history through the Time bracket. Today’s public shop centers on milled metal twins (CROWN, ECONOLINE), several aesthetic lines (CLEAR sapphire, DISCREET, BREEZE SL), and the TRIAMOND Wedgewise system. The lingual Evolution SLT and JOY lines from older catalogs aren’t in the shop. The products may be well made, but the business side is less clear: orders route through a PSM Medical address, the U.S. office site is a placeholder, and ordering is by phone or email. Sort those out before Adenta goes on a startup office’s formulary.

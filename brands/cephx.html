CephX

Cloud-based AI orthodontic imaging-analysis software from Orca Dental AI Ltd., an Israeli company. It offers automated 2D cephalometric tracing and analysis, ceph tracing from CBCT, AI segmentation of CBCT and intraoral scans, airway reports and a web 3D viewer, on monthly case-based plans. FDA 510(k) K231396 (2024, 2D) and K252538 (2026, CephX3D).

HQ: Ra'anana, Israel · Researched 2026-11-24 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

CephX is cloud-based orthodontic image-analysis software. Clinicians upload lateral cephalograms, CBCT volumes (DICOM), clinical photos or intraoral scans in a web browser; the software places cephalometric landmarks automatically, produces tracings and analyses, and offers 3D tools such as CBCT segmentation of bone, teeth and nerve canals, airway measurements, and merging of intraoral scans with CBCT. Nothing needs to be installed, and the company says it runs on desktop, tablet and phone.

It is made by Orca Dental AI Ltd., which describes itself as the creator of CephX and appears as the applicant in both FDA 510(k) records. The website’s privacy policy names a US subsidiary, Orca Dental AI LLC, and gives a US mailing address in Dover, Delaware, which is also the dateline on the company’s press releases. We found no public record of an outside parent company; it appears to be privately held.

CephX is not WebCeph. WebCeph is a separate product from AssembleCircle Corp. of South Korea. The two are competitors: CephX’s 2024 FDA submission named WebCeph as its predicate device, and several independent studies test the two side by side.

Location. The most recent FDA record (2026) and the Israeli company registry both place Orca Dental AI Ltd. in Ra’anana, Israel. The 2024 FDA record gave an earlier address in Herzliya, Israel, and some earlier studies described the company as based in Las Vegas, Nevada. Ortho Index lists Ra’anana based on the FDA and registry records; the company’s website does not state a headquarters city.

Founding. The company says CephX by Orca Dental AI was founded in 2015. The Israeli company registry, as mirrored by a third-party listing site, shows the legal entity Orca Dental AI Ltd. as incorporated in 1991. We did not find an explanation for the gap (for example, a renamed earlier company), so Ortho Index does not give a founding year.

FDA status.

  • K231396, “CEPHX - Cephalometric Analysis Software,” submitted by Orca Dental AI, Ltd. and found substantially equivalent on January 31, 2024 (Traditional 510(k); Class II, 21 CFR 892.2050, medical image management and processing system). The FDA letter gives product code QIH; openFDA’s record lists LLZ. The predicate was WebCeph (K220903). The cleared indications are for use “by dentists who provide orthodontic treatment for image analysis, simulation, profilogram, VTO (Visual Treatment Objective), and patient consultation,” with results “dependent on the interpretation of trained and licensed practitioners or dentists,” and only for patients 14 years and older. The 510(k) summary notes that, unlike its predicate, the cleared device did not include STO (surgical treatment objective), and describes the AI as recommending landmark positions rather than replacing the clinician. It is a prescription device.
  • K252538, “CEPHX3D,” submitted by Orca Dental AI, Ltd. and found substantially equivalent on March 5, 2026 (Traditional 510(k); Class II, 21 CFR 892.2050; product code QIH). The predicate was Relu Creator (K233925). The summary describes cloud-based, deep-learning segmentation of the mandible, maxilla, full dentition and inferior alveolar nerve from CBCT, with a web 3D viewer and measurement tools, for patients aged 12 and older.

The website and partner announcements describe features, such as STO planning, intraoral-scan segmentation and IOS–CBCT stitching, that are not clearly named in the cleared indications above. Ask the company which features in the version you would use are covered by each US clearance.

Other markets. The company says the platform is used in more than 180 countries and supports several languages. We did not find a CE certificate or other non-US regulatory listing on its website, so check local status before use outside the US.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists and general dentists providing orthodontic treatment who want automated cephalometric tracing without desktop software.
  • Practices that take CBCT scans and want ceph tracing generated from the CBCT volume, or 3D segmentation of teeth, roots, bone, nerve canals and airway.
  • Multi-location groups and DSOs. The company markets standardized records, audit trails and centralized oversight to them, along with California-specific record and billing compliance features. These are company claims that we did not verify.
  • Software vendors and labs. The company offers an API, embeddable views and white-label options.
  • Practices already on partner platforms. CephX is offered inside Greyfinch and Cloud 9 practice management and DEXIS’s DTX Studio Clinic imaging software (see DEXIS). The company also lists integrations with Planmeca, Envista, Dentsply and others.

The company says its platform is used by “thousands” of orthodontists and DSOs and has segmented about one million teeth, bone, airway and nerve structures. These are company claims.

Notable products

  • 2D cephalometric tracing and analysis: AI placement of 90+ landmarks (company figure) on lateral cephalograms, automatic calibration, built-in and custom analyses, superimposition, profile overlay and image templates. The user can correct landmarks.
  • Ceph from CBCT: a patent-pending method, per the company, that converts a 3D DICOM volume into a 2D cephalometric image and traces it automatically.
  • CBCT segmentation (CephX3D): AI segmentation of bone, individual teeth with roots, and the inferior alveolar nerve, exported as STL or segmented DICOM, with a web 3D viewer for measurements.
  • Airway analysis: automatic airway measurements and volume reports from cephs and CBCT.
  • Intraoral scan tools: IOS segmentation, and stitching of intraoral scans to CBCT so crowns and segmented roots can be viewed together; the company also describes root-position visualization over time without a new CBCT.
  • Planning and communication: VTO tools on cephalograms, orthognathic planning tools, and collaboration with colleagues. Simulations are planning and communication aids, not predictions of outcome.
  • Plans (as listed on the website on 2026-11-24, USD per month): 2D Basic from $69, 2D Pro from $89, 3D Basic from $105 and 3D Pro from $119, each with a choice of monthly case packages (3 to 50 cases). Unused cases do not roll over. A free trial covers 7 days or 3 cases, whichever comes first, and then charges the card on file automatically. The sign-up window quotes $79 per month for 2D Basic after the trial, which does not match the pricing page, so confirm the price in writing. The FAQ says subscriptions run to the end of the current, non-refundable billing cycle when cancelled.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Two FDA 510(k) clearances (2D in 2024, 3D in 2026) are in the public FDA database
  • Covers both 2D ceph work and 3D CBCT and intraoral-scan tasks in one browser-based service
  • Built into several practice-management and imaging platforms, which can avoid switching between systems
  • Landmarks can be corrected before measurements are used, which independent studies suggest is important
  • Widely studied: many peer-reviewed papers test it against manual tracing and competing software

Cons

  • Independent studies show that fully automatic results need clinician review:
    • A 2020 study in the Turkish Journal of Orthodontics found “greater variability” with fully automatic CephX than with other methods, concluded that fully automatic analysis “needs to be more reliable,” and found results comparable to other software after landmark correction, with the shortest analysis time.
    • A 2024 study in Progress in Orthodontics found “notable variation in accuracy and reliability” for CephX and two other AI platforms compared with digital tracing by orthodontists.
    • A 2025 study in the American Journal of Orthodontics and Dentofacial Orthopedics concluded that CephX and WebCeph “are valuable diagnosis tools only when landmark correction is applied” and “are still not suitable for assessing soft-tissue parameters.”
    • A 2026 study in Progress in Orthodontics found systematic differences between CephX’s CBCT-based ceph analysis and manual 2D cephalometry and concluded the tools tested “do not match manual 2D cephalometry closely enough for standalone clinical use.”
  • On 3D tasks, results vary by task. A 2026 Journal of Dentistry pilot study ranked CephX lowest of five tools for trueness of CBCT tooth segmentation against extracted teeth, while a 2026 study in Diagnostics found its automated airway volumes reliable against manual segmentation. A 2024 Journal of Clinical Medicine study comparing three AI ceph tools found substantial agreement in repeated analyses.
  • The cleared indications cover patients 14 and older for 2D analysis and 12 and older for 3D, which leaves out many younger orthodontic patients
  • Monthly case caps with no rollover, automatic charging after the trial, and non-refundable billing cycles; published prices are not fully consistent
  • Some marketed features may fall outside the cleared indications; check before relying on them
  • Cloud-only: images and patient data are hosted by the company (on AWS, per its FAQ, with servers in the US, Europe and Israel per its privacy policy)

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

CephX sits at the diagnostic and records-analysis step. It does not capture images; cephalograms, CBCT scans and intraoral scans come from the practice’s existing equipment and are uploaded, or sent from a partner platform. A practice might use it alongside or instead of other ceph software such as WebCeph, Dolphin Imaging or OnyxCeph, and alongside a practice-management system. Practices already on Greyfinch or Cloud 9, or using DTX Studio Clinic, may be able to use it without leaving that software.

When evaluating it, ask:

  • Which features and software version are covered by each US 510(k), and whether the features you plan to use, such as STO or intraoral-scan tools, are included.
  • How AI-placed landmarks and segmentations are reviewed and corrected in your workflow, and who signs off on the final result.
  • What the company’s described human review of edge cases involves, and whether your images are seen by its staff.
  • Current CE and other regulatory status if you practice outside the US.
  • The real monthly price for your case volume, how overage works, and the cancellation terms, in writing.
  • Where patient data is hosted, how long it’s kept, how the company’s business associate agreement fits your own policies, and how you would export tracings, models and images if you stopped using the service.
  • If you use it through a partner platform, how billing, support and data access are split between the partner and the company.

These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.

Tomy

Japanese orthodontic manufacturer (Tomy Inc., Tokyo, with a Fukushima factory) behind Clippy self-ligating brackets, Crystaline ceramics, OPA-K, and wires. Sold in Japan and Asia by Tomy International Inc.; in the U.S., Tomy-made brackets are marketed by GC Orthodontics America under GC brand names.

HQ: Tokyo, Japan · Researched 2026-10-15 · Official site

Overview

Tomy is a Japanese orthodontic appliance maker. It has no connection to the toy company of a similar name. The official site describes a two-company “Tomy Group”:

  • Tomy Inc. (トミー株式会社) is the manufacturer, with manufacturing and R&D in Tokyo and Fukushima (the site shows a Fukushima Yumoto factory).
  • Tomy International Inc. (株式会社トミーインターナショナル) is the sales company. It was established December 16, 1983, is based in Chiyoda-ku, Tokyo, and is the general distributor for Tomy products in Japan and Asia. Its company page lists Tomy Inc. as its main supplier.

Tomy International calls the group Japan’s only full-line domestic orthodontic appliance maker. That’s the company’s own claim. Outside Japan and Asia, it says it works through distributors.

The founding year in the frontmatter is left blank on purpose. 1983 is when the sales company was set up, not when the manufacturer was founded. A 2014 trade article said Tomy had been making orthodontic products “for close to 50 years,” but we did not find a founding year for Tomy Inc. on an official page.

U.S. distribution (as of October 2026): We found no Tomy-branded U.S. subsidiary. GC Orthodontics America (Alsip, IL; a subsidiary of GC Corporation) was formed in 2014 to market and service the U.S., Canada, and Latin America, and partnered with Tomy Inc. for manufacturing. GC’s live U.S. site says its core products are “manufactured by TOMY Inc.,” including EXPERIENCE self-ligating brackets, LEGEND metal twins, and Chic ceramics. We could not confirm that Clippy-C or other Tomy-branded lines are sold in the U.S. under the Tomy name. U.S. offices will most likely get Tomy-made hardware through GC Orthodontics under GC brand names. We found no public record of an acquisition. GC’s site describes the relationship as an agreement, not an ownership change.

Who it’s for

  • Practices that want Japanese-made self-ligating brackets (the Clippy family) and can buy them through Tomy International or a regional distributor
  • Clinicians interested in OPA-K, a pre-adjusted prescription that Tomy says was built from data on Japanese patients with normal occlusion
  • U.S. offices already using GC Orthodontics EXPERIENCE, LEGEND, or Chic brackets that want to know who makes them

Notable products

  • Clippy-C: a ceramic self-ligating bracket with a rhodium-coated clip that opens from the front or the gingival side, on a mechanical-lock base. Japanese certification number 218ADBZX00023000.
  • Mini Clippy, Clippy Micro Mini RC, Clippy-L, Clippy tubes: other self-ligating products listed with IFUs on the download page.
  • Crystaline 7: the smallest ceramic twin in the Crystaline series. Tomy calls Crystaline the world’s first ceramic bracket, which is a company claim.
  • Esthe MB: a “hybrid plastic” esthetic bracket.
  • OPA-K: a pre-adjusted appliance system, including Clippy-C, Mini Clippy, Crystaline, and metal bracket versions.
  • T21: brackets and caps for a passive, low-friction approach, with matching T21 wires.
  • Wires and auxiliaries: Sentalloy and Neo Sentalloy NiTi wires, white-coated wires, open-coil springs, Seagull buttons, and Super Chain, plus buccal tubes, pliers, and supplies.

Anchor screws (TADs) did not appear on the official lineup pages we reviewed, so they are not listed here.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • A real manufacturer with decades in orthodontics and Japanese medical-device certification numbers and IFUs posted publicly
  • A broad fixed-appliance catalog: self-ligating, ceramic, plastic, and metal brackets, plus wires, tubes, and auxiliaries
  • The Clippy clip design (front or gingival opening, rhodium-coated on Clippy-C) is a practical feature for wire changes
  • In the U.S., GC Orthodontics’ Tomy-made lines come with a domestic sales and service organization

Cons

  • Tomy’s official sites are mainly in Japanese. The English site is thin, which makes specs harder to check.
  • U.S. buying is indirect. Tomy-branded lines like Clippy-C are not confirmed as sold in the U.S., and GC sells Tomy-made products under its own names, so matching products takes work.
  • No public pricing
  • The founding year of the manufacturer is not confirmed on an official page
  • “First,” “only,” and “smallest” claims are the company’s marketing, not independent data

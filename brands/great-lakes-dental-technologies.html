Great Lakes Dental Technologies

Employee-owned orthodontic lab and supplier in Tonawanda, New York, offering custom lab appliances (expanders, retainers, splints, sleep appliances), Biostar and MiniSTAR thermoforming machines and materials, expansion screws, digital equipment, and hands-on and online lab training.

HQ: Tonawanda, New York, USA · Founded 1965 · Researched 2026-10-20 · Official site

Overview

Great Lakes Dental Technologies is a combined orthodontic laboratory and product supplier based in Tonawanda, New York, a suburb of Buffalo. Its official history dates the business to 1965, when it began as a small orthodontic lab making appliances and study models for local orthodontists. It became Great Lakes Orthodontic Laboratory in 1967, added a products division a few years later, merged the two into Great Lakes Orthodontics, Ltd. in 1985, and renamed itself Great Lakes Dental Technologies in 2018 to reflect a wider dental, sleep, and airway focus. You’ll still see the older “Great Lakes Orthodontics” name and greatlakesortho.com email addresses around.

It is employee-owned: the company says it became an ESOP company in 1990 and now has 200+ employee-owners. It describes itself as one of North America’s largest orthodontic laboratories, offering more than 4,000 products and services from a 78,000 sq ft headquarters with an on-site training center.

The business has two sides. The lab fabricates custom appliances you send cases for: expanders (including MSE and a custom MARPE), retainers, functional and habit appliances, nylon splints, sleep appliances, laser-sintered metal appliances, and its own smartmoves® clear aligners. The products side sells equipment and materials to practices and labs that make appliances in-house.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontic practices that want an established full-service lab for expanders, retainers, functional appliances, and splints
  • Practices and labs bringing appliance fabrication in-house and looking for thermoforming machines, materials, screws, wire, and welders from one source
  • Teams that need training: hands-on lab courses in Tonawanda, online CE courses, and free product videos
  • Offices that also treat sleep, airway, or TMD cases and want those appliances from the same lab

Notable products

  • Biostar® and MiniSTAR® thermoforming machines, including the MiniSTAR S® with scan technology, plus a wide range of thermal forming materials (for example, its ComfortTrack™ clear aligner material)
  • Expansion screws and the MSE (Maxillary Skeletal Expander), with starter and refill kits that include the screw, TADs, drivers, and activation keys, and a published MSE guidebook
  • Custom lab appliances: rapid palatal expanders (including sintered), space maintainers, Memotain® nitinol bonded lingual retainers, finishing and retention appliances, functional and habit appliances, and study models
  • Sleep, airway, and TMD: nylon sleep Herbst® designs, splints and deprogrammers, and non-custom sleep appliances
  • Brackets, archwires, Gummetal® wire, impression materials, and lab equipment such as spot, tack, and laser welders
  • Digital equipment: resold intraoral scanners (3Shape TRIOS® and Shining 3D Aoralscan), desktop scanners, and 3D printers
  • Education: multi-day hands-on courses (wire bending, retainer and active plate fabrication, soldering) and online NBC CE courses, including a free Biostar/MiniSTAR course

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • A long track record, with documented company history going back to 1965
  • Lab services and in-house supplies under one roof, so you can outsource some appliances and make others yourself
  • Real training infrastructure: an on-site training center plus online CE courses
  • Many supply items show list prices and ship-same-day status online
  • Employee-owned and independent, which some buyers value for continuity

Cons

  • The catalog is huge (4,000+ items) and spans ortho, general dentistry, and sleep, so finding the right item can take time
  • Much of the digital lineup is other companies’ scanners and printers, and many of those are “call to order” with no public price
  • Custom lab appliances don’t list prices online; you’ll need a quote or account pricing
  • As a U.S. lab, turnaround and shipping matter more for practices far from Western New York or outside North America
  • Clinical claims on product pages are the company’s own; protocols like MSE activation limits come from company guides, not independent reviews

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

Most practices use Great Lakes one of two ways. As a lab, you send impressions or scans with a prescription (the MSE form is a good example of the detail it asks for) and get the finished appliance back. As a supplier, you buy a Biostar or MiniSTAR, sheet materials, screws, and wire to make retainers and simple appliances in-house, and use the online or hands-on courses to train staff. Plenty of offices do both: thermoform retainers in-house and send complex expanders or sleep appliances out. Ask about turnaround, digital file submission, and account pricing before switching labs.

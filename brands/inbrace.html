InSmile Braces (InBrace)

Custom lingual braces system using programmed Smartwire technology, self-ligating brackets and indirect-bonding trays. InBrace (Swift Health Systems, Inc.) ceased operations in April 2025; InSmile Braces, sold by Lorelli Technologies LLC, relaunched the system in August 2025.

HQ: Fountain Valley, California, USA (FDA registration and company terms; a North Babylon, New York address also appears on legal pages) · Researched 2026-11-06 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

InSmile Braces is a custom lingual (behind-the-teeth) braces system sold to orthodontists. The appliance is built around a patient-specific programmed shape-memory wire the company calls Smartwire®, used with self-ligating lingual brackets and custom indirect-bonding (IDB) trays. It is a fixed appliance, not a clear aligner: patients do not insert and remove trays.

Ownership and status: The system was previously sold as InBrace by Swift Health Systems, Inc., of Irvine, California. In April 2025 InBrace ceased operations. The Orange County Business Journal (August 2025) reported that the company cited an inability to secure additional funding and macroeconomic challenges, and quoted a provider message that “the funding environment has presented challenges we could not overcome.” The same article said InBrace had been Irvine-based, with about 100 Orange County employees before closing, and that its Irvine and Tustin customer-center and training sites appeared to have closed.

The current seller is InSmile Braces, operated by Lorelli Technologies LLC. Company legal pages list offices at 17287 Mt. Herrmann Street, Fountain Valley, California. Those pages also use a North Babylon, New York mailing address for some legal notices, and the terms call Lorelli both an “LLC” and a “Delaware corporation,” so the entity type and headquarters details are written here as the company and FDA registration present them, not as independently reconciled corporate records. Trade press and the company’s own story page say a U.S. orthodontist who had been a large InBrace provider acquired the intellectual property, production equipment and manufacturing assets and launched InSmile Braces as a new, independent company, not a continuation of Swift Health Systems. A company post dated August 14, 2026, says InSmile reopened for case submissions on August 14, 2025, and marked that date as the start of its first year. The former inbrace.com domain now serves InSmile Braces content. The InSmile site and newsroom were active in 2026, including coverage in Orthodontic Products. We found no later announcement that InSmile itself had been acquired or shut down as of this page’s research date.

FDA status (openFDA): FDA cleared the Inbrace Orthodontic System under 510(k) K203442 on February 19, 2021, to Swift Health Systems, Inc. (Irvine, CA). The clearance covers a Class II treatment-planning software and appliance system “used to correct malocclusions in orthodontic patients using appliances individualized for the orthodontic patient,” with product codes PNN (orthodontic software), EJF (orthodontic metal bracket) and DZC (orthodontic wire). The summary describes lingual metal brackets, patient-specific programmed multiloop archwires, and patient-specific IDB trays, with the treating orthodontist reviewing and approving the digital setup before fabrication. The named predicate was Insignia (K121524).

An openFDA device registration for Lorelli Technologies LLC at the Fountain Valley address lists the proprietary name “InSmile Braces Orthodontic System,” cites K203442, and shows product codes EJF, PNN and DZC with listing created dates of November 20, 2025. The establishment type is manufacture of a medical device (registration/FEI 3042369188). We found no separate 510(k) under a Lorelli or InSmile applicant name. A registration that cites K203442 is not, by itself, proof of a formal 510(k) transfer. Practices should ask the company which clearance and labeling cover the devices they order today.

Public company-profile sources often date Swift Health Systems to 2014; the current InSmile site does not state a founding year for either company, so no founded year is listed in the frontmatter.

Who it’s for

Orthodontists who want a hidden fixed option for patients who prefer not to wear visible buccal braces and who may be a poor fit for aligner wear schedules. The workflow assumes the office is comfortable sending records (photos, a panoramic radiograph and an intraoral scan) for a digital plan, approving a setup, and bonding from custom IDB trays.

It is a weaker fit if you only want stock buccal brackets and wires with a same-day start, if you do not want a lab-dependent custom appliance, or if lingual speech and tongue-adaptation coaching is not part of your chairside routine. InSmile’s terms present the service as intended for visitors in the United States. Trade press in 2026 described the company as U.S.-focused, with international expansion discussed as a future plan rather than current availability. Confirm territory, training and eligibility with the company.

Patients cannot buy the system directly; treatment goes through a participating orthodontist.

Notable products

Names below match InSmile’s public practice, workflow and legal pages, its August 2026 anniversary post, 2026 Orthodontic Products coverage, and the FDA records above. Hardware, software, turnaround and product names can change; confirm current doctor materials before you brief patients or sign an agreement.

  • InSmile Braces / InSmile Braces Orthodontic System: the current custom lingual system (brackets, programmed wires and IDB trays). This is the name on Lorelli’s FDA listing.
  • Smartwire®: a patient-specific shape-memory wire the company says is programmed to a digital setup so tip, torque, rotation and space opening or closing are built into the wire rather than added as routine chairside bends. The company and trade press also describe an interproximal (IP) Loop (force stored in loops, contoured for hygiene in vendor copy) and a Locket (a locking portion of the wire that the company says reduces slot play).
  • Self-ligating lingual brackets: InSmile’s practice page lists anterior upper and lower brackets and posterior small and large brackets. In vendor descriptions the bracket mainly holds the wire, and the programmed wire expresses the planned movement.
  • Indirect bonding trays: brackets arrive pre-positioned in a custom tray. Company workflow copy says placement may be done by the orthodontist or delegated to trained staff where permitted.
  • Smile Design: the digital treatment-planning step. Records are used to plan bracket placement and the Smartwire sequence; the orthodontist reviews the setup before manufacturing. InBrace’s 2021 510(k) summary described the same general scan → virtual setup → doctor approval → fabricate path for the predecessor system.
  • InSmile 4-4: a limited option covering first premolar to first premolar, described for simpler crowding, spacing, or patients who have had trouble with aligners or relapse. Orthodontic Products (July 2026) described it as recently launched; InSmile’s August 14, 2026 anniversary post still referred to beta testing for this limited version. Treat commercial status as unconfirmed and ask the company whether it is generally available.
  • Training and case support: company pages describe provider onboarding, direct training and a knowledge base. The August 2026 post also mentioned a self-guided Smartwire video training course and, as vendor operational targets at that time, Smile Design turnaround “within 2 business days” and manufacturing and shipping “within 15 business days after case approval.” Those figures are company-stated and may have changed.
  • Historic InBrace system: the predecessor product cleared as the Inbrace Orthodontic System (K203442). InBrace is no longer the operating company.

July 2026 trade coverage said typical visits were spaced about every 10 to 16 weeks and that many cases used about two wire sets, and that the chairside instrument set was a lingual Weingart, a custom wire-removal plier and an engager. Those are vendor and trade-press descriptions, not a protocol.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Hidden, fixed lingual option for patients who want treatment behind the teeth and do not want to rely on aligner wear.
  • Digital setup plus custom IDB trays and a programmed wire may reduce routine chairside wire bending compared with traditional lingual mechanics.
  • Company workflow copy allows trained staff to handle parts of bonding and follow-up where state rules and office protocols permit.
  • A public FDA 510(k) exists for the predecessor Inbrace Orthodontic System (K203442), and Lorelli’s current device listing cites that clearance.
  • The successor company appears to be operating: its site, legal pages and newsroom were active in 2026, and it published a one-year anniversary note in August 2026.

Cons

  • The original maker, InBrace / Swift Health Systems, ceased operations in April 2025 and left providers with mid-treatment patients to finish. Some clinicians may weigh that history when judging vendor stability.
  • InSmile is a new legal entity (2025), separate from Swift. We found no 510(k) under a Lorelli or InSmile applicant name; current devices appear on a Lorelli registration that cites K203442, which is not the same as a newly published clearance.
  • Pricing is not published. Trade press has said the successor aimed to price below the predecessor and may add tiers later; that is unverified.
  • Marketing claims of faster treatment, fewer visits, “easiest lingual system,” high staff-delegability percentages and “predictable results” are vendor copy, not independently verified outcomes.
  • Lingual appliances have a well-known adaptation period. InSmile’s own patient FAQ notes possible tongue irritation and speech changes at first, with experiences varying.
  • Cases depend on custom fabrication and shipping; they are not a same-day stock start. Turnaround times quoted by the company can change.
  • The limited 4-4 product’s commercial status (beta vs generally available) was described differently in mid-2026 sources.
  • Headquarters and entity-type details differ slightly across legal pages (Fountain Valley production address vs a New York notice address; LLC vs “Delaware corporation” wording).

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

InSmile Braces is an alternative to buccal braces, other lingual systems and provider-directed aligners for patients who want a hidden fixed appliance. A typical public workflow is: gather photos, a panoramic radiograph and an intraoral scan; submit records for a digital Smile Design; approve bracket placement and the Smartwire sequence; receive brackets, wires and IDB trays; bond; see the patient at the intervals you choose based on the plan and the IFU; then debond and retain.

Before adopting it, practices typically confirm the current regulatory basis and labeling (including how K203442 relates to today’s InSmile-branded devices), pricing and mid-course wire or refinement policies, training, laboratory turnaround, what support exists if a vendor relationship changes, and how delegation of bonding steps fits their state’s rules. Given the 2025 shutdown of the previous maker, it is reasonable to ask how unfinished cases would be supported if operations were interrupted again.

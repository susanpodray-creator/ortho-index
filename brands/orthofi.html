OrthoFi

Cloud software and tech-enabled services from OrthoFi, Inc. of Denver, Colorado, covering orthodontic patient onboarding, payment-plan presentation, insurance claims, and collections. Related brands include AcceptCare and OrthoBanc.

HQ: Denver, Colorado, USA · Founded 2013 · Researched 2026-11-10 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

OrthoFi is cloud software and a set of tech-enabled services for patient onboarding, payment-plan presentation, insurance billing, and collections, aimed mainly at orthodontic practices and, through some products, at general dental offices as well. It is not a clinical practice-management system. The privacy policy, last updated in May 2026, names the operator OrthoFi, Inc. (also written OrthoFi Inc.) at 900 South Broadway, Suite 100, Denver, CO 80209. The about page says the company was founded in 2013 “by orthodontists for orthodontists.” Accel-KKR’s May 2019 announcement also states that OrthoFi was founded in 2013 and is headquartered in Denver.

Ownership: Accel-KKR, a technology-focused private equity firm, announced a growth investment in OrthoFi, Inc. on May 3, 2019. That release did not state a controlling-ownership percentage. Boathouse Capital, a prior investor, announced on May 8, 2019 that it had exited in connection with the Accel-KKR round after about four years. Accel-KKR still lists OrthoFi as a portfolio company (equity investment, 2019). We found no public announcement of a later sale of OrthoFi through this page’s research date.

OrthoBanc (2020): On December 31, 2020, OrthoFi announced that Accel-KKR had purchased OrthoBanc, LLC, a Chattanooga, Tennessee patient-payment company, and that the two businesses would be combined under the OrthoFi name. OrthoBanc remains an active product brand on the current OrthoFi website.

Comprehensive Finance (2021): On May 28, 2021, OrthoFi announced it had acquired Comprehensive Finance Inc. (CFI). The release was datelined Denver and Grapevine, Texas. CFI’s brands include Compassionate Finance (in-office payment plans) and Abella (patient accounts-receivable software). The announcement said both companies would keep their respective brands. Those names still appear on OrthoFi’s AcceptCare page.

Offices: The current website footer and privacy policy list only the Denver address. A May 2021 company release described offices in Denver and Chattanooga; an April 2023 release also listed Grapevine, Texas. Treat additional offices as unconfirmed from current public pages.

Status: The public website is active, the privacy policy was updated in May 2026, and Accel-KKR still lists the company. OrthoFi continues to operate under that name. Current marketing organizes the lineup as three solutions: the OrthoFi platform, AcceptCare, and OrthoBanc.

Who it’s for

Orthodontic offices that want help with new-patient intake, presenting payment options, verifying and billing insurance, and collecting patient balances — without replacing their clinical practice-management software. AcceptCare is marketed to both dental and orthodontic practices for case acceptance and financing. OrthoBanc is aimed at offices that mainly want patient-payment management. The company also describes tools for scaling across locations; those claims are the company’s, not independently measured here.

Notable products

  • OrthoFi platform: The full orthodontic offering. Company pages describe mobile patient-intake forms and text reminders, insurance eligibility and benefits verification (including same-day checks), an Open Choice financial-presentation slider (also called Intelligent Flexibility), a soft credit check, Sign@Home remote contract review, signing, and down payment, follow-up for pending and observation patients, full-service patient billing and collections (the company describes a 180-day protocol), insurance-claim submission and follow-up, iMaxX for what the company calls in-network claim maximization (allowable fees and coverage), a lockbox and wireless payment terminals the company describes as HIPAA- and PCI-compliant, and KPI / financial reporting. The compare-solutions table lists these capabilities under the OrthoFi column, including PMS system integration. It does not check third-party financing in that column.

  • AcceptCare: A case-acceptance and financing platform marketed to dental and orthodontic practices. It includes a payment-option slider, a multi-lender third-party financing marketplace (the company describes a single application, soft credit checks, terms up to 72 months, and loans “up to $40K” — company figures, not independently verified), configurable in-office financing, automated pending-patient follow-up and Sign@Home, and reporting. The company says a Power BI connection is available on request. The AcceptCare page also features Compassionate Finance (in-office plans the company says can be offered when a patient has an active bank account, with the practice setting terms) and Abella (patient AR invoicing and online pay).

  • OrthoBanc: The patient-payment and collections brand that continued after the 2020 combination. Company pages describe automated drafts plus staff follow-up, a patient payment portal, multi-channel reminders, and reporting. Add-on tools marketed on the OrthoBanc page include ZACC (a credit-risk grade the company describes as a soft inquiry), Breeze (point-of-sale card processing with PCI-certified terminals), and AccepTx Pro (treatment and fee presentation).

  • OrthoFi Yes!: Launched in April 2023 at the AAO annual session as a stand-alone patient-acquisition product focused on intake and conversion. The current marketing site emphasizes OrthoFi, AcceptCare, and OrthoBanc and does not clearly list Yes! as a separate SKU, so its present status as a stand-alone product is unconfirmed.

  • Practice-management integrations: All three current products are listed with “PMS System Integration,” but OrthoFi does not publish a full public list of supported systems. Ortho2’s Edge Cloud help documents an integration that can send patient, responsible-party, and appointment information from Edge Cloud into OrthoFi. Practices should ask the company which other systems are supported today and how ledgers stay in sync.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • Covers a wide financial workflow — onboarding, payment-option presentation, insurance claims, and collections — or smaller pieces of it through AcceptCare or OrthoBanc
  • Combines software with staffed services (eligibility checks, claim follow-up, payment collection), which may reduce front-office administrative time
  • More than one financing path is described: in-office plans, a third-party lender marketplace (AcceptCare), and payment-draft management (OrthoBanc)
  • At least one PMS connection (Ortho2 Edge Cloud) is documented by the PMS vendor
  • The company has operated under the OrthoFi name since 2013, with public product and policy updates into 2026

Cons

  • Pricing and contract terms are not published; a quote or demo is required
  • This is not a full clinical PMS. Offices still need their own practice-management system, and the public integration list is incomplete
  • Outsourcing billing, insurance, and collections means sharing financial and health-related data with a vendor. Practices need to review a business associate agreement, data-handling terms, and what happens to accounts if the service ends
  • Published conversion-rate, collection-rate, and delinquency figures are company marketing and were not independently verified
  • Credit checks, lender applications, and in-office financing sit under consumer-credit and privacy rules that vary by state; public pages are not a substitute for reviewing those requirements with qualified counsel
  • Product names overlap (Yes!, AcceptCare, AccepTx Pro, Compassionate Finance, Abella), and current SKU boundaries are not always clear from the website
  • Additional office locations named in older press releases are not shown on the current site

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

OrthoFi sits beside a practice-management system rather than replacing it. Depending on the product, it can take on new-patient paperwork and eligibility checks before an exam, present in-office or third-party payment options at the consult, let a responsible party sign a contract and pay a deposit remotely, submit and follow insurance claims, and draft or collect patient balances. Clinical scheduling, imaging, and treatment records typically stay in the office’s PMS.

When evaluating it, ask for pricing and contract terms in writing, a current list of PMS integrations and how dual ledgers are reconciled, security and backup details, a business associate agreement, how financing partners are disclosed to patients, and how historical accounts and payment methods are handled if the relationship ends. Those are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy or to offer any particular payment plan.

LightForce Orthodontics

Boston-based generative braces platform — patient-specific 3D-printed ceramic and metal brackets, LightPlan digital planning, and LightTray indirect bonding from each orthodontist’s treatment plan.

HQ: Boston, Massachusetts, USA · Founded 2015 · Researched 2026-09-25 · Official site

Overview

LightForce Orthodontics makes generative braces — fully patient-specific, 3D-printed fixed appliances generated from the orthodontist’s digital treatment plan rather than from a stock prescription catalog. The public brand frames the model as plan in, appliance out: if the plan changes, the bracket changes with it. Official materials describe customization across six dimensions (base, slot, prescription, position, tie wings, and hooks), with brackets and trays 3D-printed in the USA (patient copy cites Boston, MA manufacturing).

The company is based in Boston, Massachusetts. Public company history places the founding concept with orthodontist Alfred Griffin, DMD, PhD during his Harvard residency era (~2015; early name Signature Orthodontics), with commercial debut of the custom ceramic system around the 2019 AAO Annual Session (Orthodontic Products coverage) and later expansion into metal. The live About page (reviewed September 25, 2026) cites roughly 200,000 cases, U.S. manufacturing at what it calls the world’s largest facility for directly 3D-printed functional medical devices by volume, and roughly $200 million in backing from investors including Kleiner Perkins, Ally Bridge Group, and the American Association of Orthodontists.

Doctor and patient entry points live on lf.co (doctor advantage, patient why-LightForce, and company blog). Support and SDS materials also appear under lightforceortho.com domains. Legal entity language on product safety sheets uses LightForce Orthodontics LLC.

This brief is for practices comparing custom fixed-appliance labs, digital planning for braces, and IDB workflows. It is not patient marketing, a protocol, or medical advice. Case selection, bonding protocols, and finishing sit with the treating doctor.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists who want digital treatment planning for fixed appliances with a lab-printed, patient-specific bracket instead of (or alongside) stock twin/SL inventory
  • Offices already comfortable with intraoral scanning → cloud plan → approve → ship workflows who want that model for braces, not only aligners
  • Practices looking to reduce bracket inventory and lean on indirect bonding trays designed with the brackets
  • Clinics that treat a large metal-bracket share of starts and want a custom path beyond ceramic-only customization (LightBracket Metal announced April 28, 2026)

It is a weaker shortlist if you only need off-the-shelf twin brackets and wires with no lab SLA, if you refuse cloud planning vendors, or if your state/practice rules make staff-delegated bonding impractical. Confirm pricing, turnaround, scanner paths, and training with LightForce — not from this page.

Notable products

Names below match LightForce’s public doctor pages, patient overview, April 2026 metal launch materials, and 2019 Orthodontic Products coverage reviewed September 25, 2026. Slot options, materials, turnaround, and software features change; verify current doctor materials before you brief patients or sign a contract.

Brackets (generative braces)

  • LightBracket Ceramic™ — translucent ceramic, fully 3D-printed patient-specific bracket system. Marketed as the first-of-its-kind generative braces line; polycrystalline ceramic framing in early trade coverage. Vendor materials emphasize a base that conforms to individual tooth morphology and a prescription driven by the digital plan rather than a universal Roth/MBT shelf.
  • LightBracket Metal™ — patient-specific 3D-printed metal bracket announced April 28, 2026 (Business Wire / company blog; AAO Annual Session 2026 debut in Orlando). Support SDS language describes stainless steel brackets. Public specs called out in the launch: individualization across the same six dimensions; slot sizes .018″, .020″, .022″ with bidimensional combinations; lower-profile design with patient-specific tie wings/hooks. Comfort and debond-rate language is vendor messaging.
  • Six-dimension customization — base, slot height, slot prescription, bracket position, tie wings, and hooks, all generated from the approved plan (homepage and metal launch copy).

Trays and bonding aids

  • LightTray™ — patient-specific indirect bonding trays generated with the bracket set; positioned for staff-delegated bonding where state law allows.
  • LightTray: Turbo™ — 3D-printed turbo trays for bite opening; public doctor page notes placement on incisors, canines, or molars.

Software and practice tools

  • LightPlan™ — proprietary digital treatment-planning software; doctor designs/approves the setup that drives appliance generation. Early Orthodontic Products coverage described open STL intake from commonly used intraoral scanners and a cloud review/approval path (confirm live scanner list with LightForce).
  • Bonding Visit tools — doctor-page software lane for chairside bonding communication and record-keeping.
  • Data / practice insights — vendor dashboards framed for efficiency, training opportunities, and operational metrics (availability and metrics definitions are company features, not Ortho Index audits).
  • Education & clinical integration — peer mentoring, in-person/virtual education, and Clinical Integration training called out on the doctor advantage page; Digital Treatment Planners support doctor-directed setups.

Workflow (public Scan → Plan → Print → Bond → Check)

  1. Digitize the arches with an in-office scanner
  2. Design/approve the individualized plan in LightPlan
  3. LightForce 3D-prints brackets and trays in the USA
  4. Bond via LightTray IDB
  5. Monitor with fewer planned adjustment visits per vendor positioning

Always verify territory rules, turnaround SLAs, remake/backup bracket policies, wire inventory expectations, and current SKU naming with LightForce directly. Ortho Index does not publish prices.

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • True patient-specific fixed appliances — brackets generated from the doctor’s plan and tooth anatomy, not a one-prescription-fits-all catalog; rare among mainstream brace systems
  • Ceramic and metal generative options after the April 2026 metal launch — covers the large metal-preferring segment (teens and others) that ceramic-only customization missed
  • Digital fixed workflow with IDB trays and planning software in one vendor stack — useful if you already think in “setup first, appliance second”
  • U.S. manufacturing story and scale narrative (~200k cases on the About page; large direct-print medical-device facility claim) — material for diligence conversations, not a clinical ranking
  • Inventory shift — on-demand custom brackets can reduce shelf SKUs versus stocking hundreds of stock prescriptions (practice-economics claim; model depends on case volume and lab fees)

Cons / watch-outs

  • Lab dependency and SLA — every start waits on scan quality, plan approval, and print/ship; stock brackets bond same-day from the drawer. Confirm remakes, backups, and rush rules in writing
  • Efficiency statistics are published/vendor-cited, not Ortho Index tests — homepage cites up to 60% fewer appointments, 43% shorter treatment, and 70% fewer wire adjustments, attributing data to Journal of Clinical Orthodontics (2024) and International Orthodontics (2026). Treat as study/vendor figures with clinician/patient variation — not a guarantee for your office
  • Learning curve and change management — staff must own scan quality, LightPlan review habits, and IDB technique; weak digital ops will not magically become efficient
  • Cost structure differs from catalog brackets — custom print + software + training is a different P&L than buying twin cases by the box; Ortho Index does not publish fees — get a live quote
  • Not an aligner substitute narrative — this is still fixed appliances (wires, elastics, hygiene coaching). Patients seeking removable clear trays need a different shortlist
  • Leadership/branding evolve quickly — About page lists Erica Rogers as CEO and Alfred Griffin as founder / Chief Product Officer; some April 2026 launch copy still styles Griffin as CEO. Use current company contacts for contracts, not archived headlines

Warm and direct: LightForce is a custom fixed-appliance manufacturing and planning relationship, not a promise. Ortho Index does not give medical advice or guarantee outcomes.

How it compares briefly

Against stock twin / self-ligating catalogs (American Orthodontics, Ormco, RMO, Forestadent, etc.), LightForce trades same-day inventory and familiar mechanics for plan-driven, per-tooth printed brackets and a lab IDB kit. Against other custom fixed systems (historically Incognito lingual, SureSmile wires/IDB, in-house printed experiments), LightForce’s public differentiator is directly 3D-printed end-use brackets (ceramic and now metal) at commercial scale rather than robotically bent wires alone or lab-bent lingual setups. Against clear aligners (Invisalign, Spark, ClearCorrect, SureSmile Aligners), it stays in the braces lane for complex mechanics and patients who prefer or need fixed — digital planning is the shared language, not the appliance. None of that ranks clinical superiority — match case mix, lab fees, scanner stack, and staff readiness to your office.

How practices use it

Typical public story: scan → LightPlan setup (doctor-directed, sometimes with LightForce treatment-planning support) → approve → receive LightBrackets + LightTrays → IDB bond → progress with fewer wire-bending visits if the prescription holds. Practices keep conventional archwire inventories in the usual slot sizes while outsourcing bracket SKUs to the print lab. Metal vs ceramic is a case/patient preference choice within the same generative platform after the 2026 metal rollout.

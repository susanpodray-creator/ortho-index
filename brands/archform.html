ArchForm

U.S. clear-aligner treatment-planning software company (ArchForm, Inc.) whose FDA-cleared ArchForm Orthodontic Software System lets practices design aligners and indirect-bonding trays in-house, with optional factory-made aligners, direct-print aligner workflows, a desktop aligner trimming machine, and white-label tools for labs and aligner brands.

HQ: Santa Clara, California, USA · Researched 2026-11-18 · Official site

Get a quote Claim this brand

Overview

ArchForm makes clear-aligner treatment-planning software for orthodontists and dentists who want to design aligners themselves. Its core product, the ArchForm Orthodontic Software System, takes an intraoral scan (STL), segments the teeth, and lets the clinician plan tooth movements, attachments, staging and IPR, then exports print-ready models or aligner shells. Around that software the company now sells several production options: in-office thermoformed aligners, factory-made aligners shipped to the practice, direct-printed aligners, indirect-bonding (IDB) trays, an automated aligner trimming machine called ArchMill, and white-label tools for aligner brands and labs.

Company and status: FDA records name the operator ArchForm, Inc. The company went through Y Combinator’s Winter 2018 batch, and TechCrunch covered its launch in March 2018 as a startup letting orthodontists design and 3D-print aligners in their own offices. It is still active: the website, software release notes, and a December 2025 clinical blog post were live on the research date, and FDA’s establishment registration runs through 2026. We found no public report that ArchForm has been acquired or merged. It appears to be privately held. Third-party databases report venture funding, but ArchForm has not published amounts or investors, so none are stated here.

Headquarters: Public sources disagree. FDA’s establishment registration lists ArchForm, Inc. in Santa Clara, California, which is the location used in this page’s summary field. Y Combinator’s profile says San Francisco, and LinkedIn says Sunnyvale. The 2021 510(k) was filed from a different California address. The website does not publish a street address; it gives an email contact. The homepage says factory-made aligners come from “our robotic factory in California” without naming the site.

Founding: Y Combinator’s profile lists 2013 as the founding year, while LinkedIn says 2017. Because they disagree, Ortho Index has left the founding year out of the summary fields.

Scale (company figures, not independently verified): The homepage claims 7M+ aligners made and $300M+ saved for customers, the latter calculated by the company against “a leading alternative aligner company.” The enterprise page says the software has been used on 800,000+ patient cases. The in-office page claims savings of “84% in lab bills” and the homepage “over 50%” on aligner costs. These are marketing claims.

Recent partnership: In May 2026, trade press reported that Graphy, a Korean maker of direct-print aligner resins, announced a joint project with ArchForm and DentalMonitoring to connect aligner design (ArchForm), production (Graphy) and remote monitoring (DentalMonitoring). Its scope and launch timing hadn’t been published as of the research date. See the Graphy and DentalMonitoring pages.

Who it’s for

  • Orthodontists and dentists making aligners in-house who already own, or plan to buy, a 3D printer and thermoforming equipment and want to plan cases themselves rather than through a large aligner brand.
  • Practices that want a hybrid workflow: the company positions the same software for starting cases in brackets (via indirect bonding) and finishing in aligners.
  • Practices that want to plan in-house but outsource fabrication, using ArchForm’s factory-made aligners, which can carry practice-branded packaging in the U.S.
  • Aligner brands and labs that need production planning software, a brandable browser-based editor for their clients, API and bulk export, or white-label aligner manufacturing.

It is a weaker fit for practices that want a fully managed aligner system with case-by-case planning done for them, or that have no appetite for running in-office printing and finishing.

Notable products

Product names and features below come from ArchForm’s website as of November 2026. Confirm current features and pricing in a demo.

  • ArchForm design software: Company pages describe automatic tooth segmentation and labeling, auto-orientation, drag-to-align tooth movement, adjustable arch shape, attachments (including lingual attachments) with size and tilt control, bite stops and bite shifts, pontics for extraction cases, one-click IPR adjustment, staging with intermediate goals and overcorrection, automatic sequential distalization, hooks and elastic cutouts, a lasso trimming tool, and automatic print prep (vertical supports, labels, hollowed models with internal honeycomb support). Cases sync across computers. Version 2.3 release notes add a Bolton analysis panel, an occlusal-contact heatmap, per-tooth blockout and trim-line improvements. The company says ArchForm works with all intraoral scanners via STL upload.
  • Dashboard: a web dashboard for ordering design services and aligners and tracking production, whether in the practice’s own lab or with ArchForm.
  • In-office aligners: the software exports models for printing on the practice’s own printer, followed by thermoforming, trimming and polishing in the office. The company says hollow model export cuts resin use by about half.
  • Factory-made aligners (“Aligner Manufacturing”): aligners made by ArchForm and shipped to the practice. The company says they use Zendura FLX, a three-layer thermoplastic, with robotic trimming and scalloped trimlines. Free sample aligners are offered on request.
  • Direct print aligners: a workflow for printing aligners directly from resin, with automatically generated aligner shells and tear-away supports, followed by centrifuging, nitrogen-environment UV curing and a hot-water rinse. The FAQ lists validated printers (SprintRay Pro 95 and Pro 55, Uniz Slash 2 and NBEE, Asiga MAX, Shining Acufab-L4K) and says a liter of resin makes about 125–135 aligners. The resin naming is inconsistent: the direct-print page calls it ShapeForm™, the homepage says MemoryForm™, and the FAQ refers to aligners made from TC-85, which is the name of Graphy’s direct-print resin. ArchForm doesn’t say who makes the resin, so confirm the material, its manufacturer and its regulatory status before buying.
  • Indirect bonding (IDB): digital bracket placement with tip, torque and rotation fine-tuning, a bracket library from several manufacturers (or scan your own brackets), collision warnings and printable transfer trays. Practices can design trays themselves, have ArchForm design them, or have ArchForm print and ship them.
  • ArchMill: a 5-axis desktop machine that trims thermoformed aligners. A camera reads a code on the model and pulls the trim path from ArchForm’s cloud. The company says trimming takes under a minute and usually leaves an edge that doesn’t need polishing.
  • Enterprise: a white-label, browser-based 3D editor for aligner brands’ clients, API and bulk-export scripts, laser-marking and 5-axis trimline exports, and white-label aligner manufacturing.

FDA status: openFDA and the FDA 510(k) database list K213916, “ArchForm Orthodontic Software System”, submitted by Archform, Inc. and found substantially equivalent on December 16, 2021 (product code PNN, orthodontic software, Class II, via third-party review). The predicate was Orchestrate 3D Orthodontic Software (K181112). The cleared use is a front-end software tool for managing and analyzing orthodontic models, simulating treatment, and virtually designing appliances, including dental casts that may be used to make sequential aligner trays or retainers. FDA’s establishment registration for ArchForm, Inc. (Santa Clara, CA; expiry year 2026) also lists:

  • “Form Aligners” under product code NXC (sequential aligner), referencing K151965. That 510(k) is a 2016 clearance for a device called the “Dentti System,” issued to a different, separately named California company. ArchForm doesn’t explain the relationship publicly, so we could not confirm how that clearance applies.
  • “Archform Retainer” under product code DYT, a Class I orthodontic code, with no 510(k) number listed.

We found no 510(k) under an ArchForm name for the direct-print resin (ShapeForm/MemoryForm). The direct-print page says the hardware is “a validated part of the FDA approval,” which is a company statement we could not tie to a specific record.

Independent research: Two peer-reviewed studies included ArchForm’s automatic tooth segmentation. A 2025 typodont study comparing ArchForm, OrthoSystem and VISION found that all three tended to oversize segmented teeth and reported that “OrthoSystem and VISION demonstrated better accuracy compared to ArchForm,” though differences were mostly not clinically significant. It warned that overestimated tooth widths can affect IPR planning. A 2026 study of four platforms (Medit LINK, DentOne, BlueSkyPlan and ArchForm) on 270 digital models found that BlueSkyPlan outperformed the others and that all of them overestimated mesio-distal widths. It concluded that automatic segmentation “still requires clinician supervision.”

Pros / cons (honest)

Pros

  • The planning software has an FDA 510(k) clearance (K213916) that is in the public FDA database
  • One platform covers aligner planning, indirect-bonding trays and several production routes (in-office thermoforming, direct print, or factory-made), so a practice can change how it fabricates without changing software
  • Practice-controlled planning, rather than back-and-forth with an outside technician
  • Open STL workflow; the company says it works with any intraoral scanner, and the direct-print FAQ names specific validated printers
  • Active development, with published release notes and a 2026 partnership announced with Graphy and DentalMonitoring
  • Options for labs and aligner brands (API, bulk export, white-label editor and manufacturing)

Cons

  • Headline figures (aligners made, savings, case counts, percentage savings on lab bills) are company claims, and the savings figure is benchmarked by the company against an unnamed competitor
  • Public sources disagree on headquarters city and founding year, and the company does not publish its address, funding or investors
  • The FDA listing for “Form Aligners” references a 2016 clearance held by a differently named company, and we found no ArchForm-named clearance for the direct-print resin. Resin naming is inconsistent across the site
  • In independent studies, ArchForm’s automatic segmentation was less accurate than some competitors’, and every tested program oversized teeth, so clinicians should check segmentation and IPR rather than relying on automation
  • In-office production puts printing, thermoforming or curing, trimming, quality control and the related regulatory responsibilities on the practice
  • Current pricing isn’t published on the website. A 2018 press report quoted about $50 per patient, which may not reflect today’s pricing. Get quotes in writing
  • Cloud sync and dashboards mean patient scans are stored with the vendor, so review data-protection and business associate terms

Informational only. No outcome guarantees; follow manufacturer IFUs and your clinical judgment.

How it fits in a practice

ArchForm sits at the treatment-planning step of aligner and indirect-bonding workflows. It is not a practice-management system. A practice could use it in one of three ways:

  • Plan in ArchForm and make aligners in the office (print models and thermoform, or direct-print), optionally adding ArchMill for trimming.
  • Plan in ArchForm and order finished aligners or IDB trays from the company.
  • Use ArchForm for indirect bonding at the start of treatment and aligners to finish.

When evaluating it, ask:

  • Current software pricing (subscription or per case) and what design services cost.
  • Which FDA clearances cover each product you would use: the software (K213916), the factory-made aligners, and any direct-print resin, and who manufactures that resin.
  • Which printers, resins, thermoforming materials and scanners are validated for your workflow.
  • Where factory-made aligners are made and the turnaround times.
  • How patient data is stored, how long it’s kept, and whether a business associate agreement is offered.
  • How you would export your cases if you stopped using the platform.

These are diligence questions, not a recommendation to buy.
